# RETENTION.md — what actually keeps people coming back

**August 13, 2026.** Two research runs: the gentle daily loop (Animal
Crossing, Wordle, Neko Atsume, Sky, Duolingo, Finch) and long-term
attachment (Love and Deepspace, Stardew Valley, Genshin, Pokémon GO).
Research only — no build impact until we decide. Figures are marked
**[D]** disclosed or **[E]** estimated.

---

## 1. The one number

Same acquisition. Wildly different year-one retention **[E, AppMagic]**:

| | D1 | D30 | **D365** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pokémon GO** | 40.4% | 30.3% | **20.4%** |
| Genshin Impact | 41.2% | 7.5% | **1.8%** |

They acquire *identically*. A year later Pokémon GO retains **eleven
times more of them** — despite Genshin shipping vastly more authored
content and spending vastly more to make it.

**The variable is where the content comes from.** Pokémon GO's world
changes because the real world changes — weather, time of day, season,
hemisphere, location. It cannot run out. Genshin's content is authored,
so it can, and between patches the daily loop is revealed as empty.

**We are structurally in the first category.** Every night the sky is
different, for free, forever, and differently for every user because it
plays against their birth chart. That's the asset. Everything below is
about not squandering it.

Pokémon GO's disclosed numbers make the point harder: **100M+ unique
players in 2024, 20M weekly actives, 40 minutes average daily playtime,
and about half of all players return seven days a week** — in a
ten-year-old game **[D, Scopely]**.

## 2. The free wins we already have

1. **Global synchrony.** Wordle's growth (90 → 300,000 players in 60
   days, on zero marketing) ran on everyone getting the *same puzzle*,
   which is what made the share grid meaningful — you're comparing on
   identical input. **Everyone on Earth is under the same moon
   tonight.** We get Wordle's core social property for free, and we
   should build any social layer on it.
2. **Recurrence solves the finite-collection cliff.** 28 stations is
   completable — that's an honest week-one promise and our clearest
   anti-gacha signal. Then cycle two is visibly different, because
   station 14 in October under a waning moon is not station 14 in March
   under a full one. Animal Crossing's engagement cliff came from a
   finite museum with nothing behind it; ours refills itself.
3. **Structural personalization.** The research separates three tiers:
   cosmetic (a name), recall (it remembers what you said), and
   *structural* — the content is genuinely different because of who you
   are. Almost nobody has the third. Pokémon GO personalizes by
   location; **we personalize by location, birth moment, and tonight's
   sky simultaneously.**

## 3. The finding I did not expect — 28 = 4 × 7

Stardew Valley's month is 28 days, and the reason is not flavour. **28
days = exactly four seven-day weeks, so every date falls on the same
weekday forever.** Spring 1 is always a Monday. Birthdays, festivals,
shop schedules become *memorizable*, and after about one in-game year
players stop consulting the calendar and start simply knowing it.

**Internalized calendars produce anticipation without notifications** —
which is the cheapest retention there is, and the only kind that's
completely clean with a teen audience.

We have 28 stations. If station-day maps predictably onto weekdays
within a cycle, users learn the road the way Stardew players learn the
valley. Worth an hour of thought before the loop ships; the sidereal
month is 27.3 days, so the mapping isn't automatic and would need
deciding deliberately.

## 4. Take these

Ranked by strength of evidence.

**1. Absence generates content, not decay.** Neko Atsume's whole design:
you close the app, cats visit *while you're gone*, you return to
photographs. The designer's line — *"if you put food out, the cats will
come. If you don't, then nothing bad happens."* Finch does the same with
a bird that never dies. **Our version:** *"the moon crossed three
stations while you were away — here's what they said."* That converts
the return moment from guilt into curiosity, and it's the single highest
-leverage inversion available to us.

**2. A hard daily cap, non-exceedable.** Wordle's one puzzle, Sky's five
candles, Animal Crossing's ~20 minutes. And the killer evidence:
**Duolingo's own data says learners who binge are much more likely to
abandon the app than those who pace** **[D]**. The cap isn't an ethical
sacrifice — it's retention-optimal. One station a night, full stop.

**3. Ship the archive on day one.** The clearest sequencing lesson in
both reports. Week-one retention is novelty; **month-six retention is
the record of what the app has of yours** — and it has to have been
accumulating since day one or it won't exist when it's needed. Every
night's reading, saved, timestamped, chart-anchored.

**4. Recall, not intelligence.** The dominant theme in the parasocial
literature is *"the love he gives me is different from what he gives to
others."* That effect needs **storage and callback, not AI**. *"Last
time the moon crossed your Venus, you wrote ___"* is a database query,
and it will land harder than anything a model generates.

**5. Craft is a retention lever, measurably.** Duolingo redesigned their
streak animation — art only, no mechanical change — and got **+1.7%
Day-7 retention [D]**. The moment of collecting a station is a feature,
not a transition. Budget for it.

**6. Decay the relationship, never the artifact.** Stardew's one real
daily obligation is friendship decay — 20 points a day for a spouse,
forever. It punishes absence without touching your assets, and it can't
be caught up by binging (gifts are capped at two per villager per week).
Compare Genshin's resin, which punishes absence by wasting a resource.
Identical math, opposite affect.

**7. Ticketed real-world occasions as monetization.** Pokémon GO sells
tickets to *scheduled events* — 2M for GO Fest 2024 **[D]** — not random
pulls. Fixed price, disclosed contents, no randomness, works for a
13-year-old's parent. An eclipse, a solstice, a major transit. **This is
the only monetization model in either report that clears our ethics
floor cleanly, and it's proven at roughly $500M/year.**

**8. Say their name.** Love and Deepspace pre-recorded a large library
of names so the character speaks the player's chosen nickname aloud. The
highest-ROI intimacy mechanic in the research, pure asset cost, zero
systems complexity, and entirely separable from gacha.

## 5. Refuse these

| Mechanic | Why |
|---|---|
| **Escalating streak counter** | Breaking a streak predicts abandoning the *platform*, not just a reset. 5.6M Wordle streaks died on a single hard word **[D]** |
| **Paid streak repair** | Paying to undo a punishment we designed |
| **Permanent seasonal expiry** | Sky's "gone forever" gifts are its most ethically loaded mechanic and give it a reputation for feeling obligatory despite gentle presentation |
| **Stamina gating a grind** | Genshin's resin. Rations old content rather than giving new — chore-affect, 1.8% D365 |
| **Silent retroactive account changes** | Duolingo's auto-applied freeze. Benevolent, still opaque, poor look with minors |
| **Behaviourally-timed re-engagement** | The one thing that would turn our compliant schedule into a non-compliant one (§7) |

And a subtle trap worth naming: **visible repairability weakens the
thing it protects** — users are measurably less motivated to keep
streaks when they know they can repair them. So don't build forgiveness
as a feature people manage. Build it as *the absence of anything needing
forgiveness*: the collection is append-only, the cycle is perpetual, and
the station you missed comes back in about 27 days, guaranteed, forever.
That's a true statement about the sky, and it replaces "don't lose your
streak" with "the sky comes back around."

## 6. The warning I'd tape to the monitor

> **If collecting a station is instrumentally valuable — a token toward
> something else — we have built resin. If the station is intrinsically
> the point — a reading worth having on its own — we have built
> Stardew.**

Resin's daily loop retains 1.8% at one year. This is the one place our
minigame idea (PRODUCT.md Part Three) needs care: minigames must make
the night *richer*, never make the station a currency you spend
somewhere else.

## 7. A compliance correction in our favour

I've been over-cautious about the UK Age Appropriate Design Code. It is
a **data-protection** code, and it bites where **personal data powers
the engagement mechanic**. Standard 5 targets features that "use
personal data to automatically extend use"; Standard 13 is narrower
still — about nudging children into surrendering privacy — and it
*explicitly permits* wellbeing nudges toward breaks.

**A schedule set by the sky is identical for every user and derived from
no one's behavioural profile.** That is structurally outside the worst
of Standard 5. A schedule set by *our model of when this user is likely
to lapse* would be squarely inside it.

Our astronomy premise hands us the compliant version for free — and it
means the nightly loop is safer than I'd assumed, provided we never
personalize the *timing*.

## 8. What I'd want to decide before the loop ships

1. **Does station-day map onto weekdays?** (§3 — cheap now, impossible
   to retrofit once people have learned the road.)
2. **What does the return screen say after three days away?** The answer
   is "here's what you missed, as a record" — but the exact copy is the
   difference between curiosity and guilt.
3. **Is there a social layer at all?** If yes, it's built on synchrony
   (everyone under tonight's station), never on comparative counters.
   Friend-streak-style *mutual* accountability outperformed solo streak
   interventions by a wide margin (+22% vs 1–14%) **[D]**.
