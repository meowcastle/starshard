## 0. What was built

`research/v2-sim/engine_v2.py`, on top of `engine_v1.py` imported unchanged (the cards, the faces, the captures, the cascades, every signature, the count law, the Gate's lead, the Return, the Glance, the xorshift deal, and the deterministic agents' shape). What the Moving Sky adds, from `MANZIL-TCG.md` section 1.1 and the S1 line:

- Nine mansions visible; slot i shows mansion `(tonight - 1 + offset + i) mod 28 + 1`. The v1 functions are called with the mansion standing at slot 0 as "tonight", so none of them changed.
- A round is the leader's placement then the follower's; a side with no card passes. At the end of the round the leftmost mansion **sets**: its card's weight under the v1 count law (dominion at home, Jupiter, the Gathered Stars, the Crown at the edge, the District, the Thread's claim on the edge, the Hideaway's silence) is banked to whoever holds it; the road moves one to the left; the next mansion in the real sequence **rises** at slot 8, and if it is a planet's home and she still holds that planet, the planet is already standing on it: it lodges as her placement, its captures resolve, and she neither chooses it nor spends a turn on it.
- Six sets a night; after the sixth it is dawn and every mansion still on the road sets at once. The bank decides the night; a tied bank is yours, as in v1.
- A card scores when its mansion sets, never when it is placed. Recapturing a card the round before it sets is the counter the spec asks for.
- The agents keep v1's evaluation shape with the total (bank plus the road as dawn would count it now) in place of the road count, one reply ahead, the reply including the set it causes. Same depth dial, same strict `>` and first-best order.

Choices made rather than asked, each named in the engine's docstring: no draw pile (the spec's section 7 says Moving Sky alone, on the hands as dealt; a deck is S4 and S5), so with five cards a side the sixth round is a bare set before dawn; the rising planet's arrival resolves its captures (it is a placement by the sky, the sky as engine); the arrival is not glanced; dawn sets the whole road rather than leaving it unscored (with a six-set watch the alternative makes slots 6 to 8 and every risen mansion worth nothing, which reads as a worse game, not a stricter one).

## 2. What failed and why

**The bands fail.** Against her v1 reader at depth 8, the configuration that produced every August and October number:

```

    pack of 12  careful: lead-changes 2.57  comeback 34.6%  margin 3.54  close(<=1) 29.0%  flips 3.72
```

Careful is above the band both ways (67.9 and 77.0 against 55 to 65), casual is far above it (55.4 and 55.8 against 35 to 45), and the gap is 12.5 on the five (21.2 on the twelve passes). The casual number is the telling one: a player who looks no moves ahead beats her reader more than half the time, where on v1's road he lost three in four. Her evaluation, margin now times ten minus your best reply times depth, was already wrong in shape on the fixed road (`manzil-why-storm3.md` section 4); on a road where the setting edge is worth a point every round and the right side keeps opening, it is wrong in a second way. The bands are a measurement of the pair, the road and the reader, and the reader is S8's packet.

**Three of six drama rows fail.** Comebacks 32.1 against 55.4 (five) and 34.6 against 43.8 (twelve), lead changes on the twelve 2.57 against 2.62. Two cautions on comparability, which do not change the verdict: the comeback proxy reads the margin after the sixth placement, which is two thirds of a v1 board and three fifths of a v2 night, and the margin now includes a bank that cannot be taken back, so a side behind at the sixth placement has less road to come back on; and "close" is a final margin of one or less on a scale that runs to fifteen instead of nine, which makes the passing close rows the stronger result.

**The six-set perfect-play solve does not fit, here or in the sandbox.** The v1 solve was about 230 thousand nodes a night. The moving road does not fill, so the branching stays wide to the end and the tree grows about eleven times per set: three sets about 100 thousand nodes, four sets about one million, and the six-set night (ten placements) is beyond both machines as the solver stands. In this shell the part runs to its node cap: 

```
--- solve-you-1  (engine_v2, seeds 8)
    you leads, night  1: unsolved within 1500000 nodes (49s)
--- solve-sky-1  (engine_v2, seeds 8)
    sky leads, night  1: unsolved within 1500000 nodes (43s)
```

In the cloud sandbox (8 GB, two cores) the uncapped six-set solve of night 1 for each leader was killed by the memory limit at 4.4 GB of memo after about five minutes; a memory-bounded single-process attempt at the you-leads night was still running when this report was written and is not counted. The solver was improved on the way (children built once and tried greedy-first, the memo key capping ages above one, which is all the Veil and the Chamber read, and the bank entering as its margin) and the three- and four-set solves above are its product. What would solve six sets: a compact-state solver (slots as integers, no dict copies, a bytes key) in Python, about a ten-fold gain, or the C2 JS engine, or a machine without the 180 s and 4 GB walls. The gate runner now accepts solve parts from a longer run elsewhere and shows unsolved nights as unsolved; it never counts them.
