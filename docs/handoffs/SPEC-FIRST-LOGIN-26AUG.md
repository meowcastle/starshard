# Manzil — the game as it now stands, from first login

**26 August 2026. For Design.** This is the spec, not a research note. Everything here is decided
unless it says otherwise. Open questions are collected at the end rather than scattered through.

---

## 1. Before the first night

The player gives a birth date, time and place. From it we know their sun and their planets.

**Their collection is all twenty-eight mansions from the start.** Nothing is locked away. What
differs is what each card knows how to do.

| | level | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **the sun's mansion** | **3** | the signature and the quadrant grant, both chosen |
| **the five planets' mansions** | **2** | the signature, chosen |
| **the other twenty-two** | **1** | numbers only |

So a new player opens the game holding one strong card, five real ones and twenty-two plain ones,
and the strong one is theirs specifically. Nobody else's deck opens the same way.

## 2. The night

**The moon stands in one mansion each night.** That is the only mansion whose road can be walked for
progress. Everything already beaten stays open to replay, but replay does not level a card.

The app opens with one line from the sky: which mansion tonight is, and the player's history with it.

## 3. The hand

**Seven cards, shuffled from all twenty-eight.** Not chosen. Dealt.

**The mulligan.** If the player does not like the hand they may shuffle again and take **six**. Once
more after that and they take **five**. Two mulligans, then they play what they have.

That is the whole hand system. There is no drafting step and no pre-selection, and **no card can be
guaranteed into a hand until it reaches level 4.** The sun is strong but it is not always there.

## 4. The board

**Nine stations in a row.** Station one is tonight's mansion. Each station after it is the next
mansion the moon will cross.

Two hands of seven. Players alternate, one card lodged per turn, until the road is full. Then it is
counted and the higher total takes the board.

Every card has a **left face** and a **right face**. A card fights with the face pointing at what it
is fighting. **Bigger takes it. Equal takes it too.**

**Dominion.** A card standing on its own mansion counts two instead of one, whoever ends up holding
it.

**A taken card does not move.** It flips in place and changes side.

## 5. The nine battles

Eight walkers on the road, rising in strength. Then the mansion itself: its own avatar, playing its
planets and its own cards.

**The mansion carries a board rule** drawn from its lore. The rule always changes the *shape* of the
board, never the *numbers*. It leads every board, or its own station counts double, or the two ends
belong to it until taken. Never an extra card, never bigger numbers, never winning ties.

Beat all nine and the mansion's card is yours at level 1. Walk the same road again on a later night
the moon stands there and it wakes a level.

## 6. Levelling

**A card levels only by beating its own mansion, on a night the moon is in it.** One mansion a night,
so nobody rushes it.

| level | what happens |
|---|---|
| **1** | the numbers. Nothing else. |
| **2** | **choose:** +1 to both faces, or this card's signature |
| **3** | **choose:** +1 to both faces, or this card's quadrant grant |
| **4** | the full artwork, its animation, and a respec |

**Level 4 also unlocks presetting.** A level-4 card may be locked into every hand. **A maximum of
two cards may be locked at once**, and every locked card takes one of the seven slots.

**Respec.** Level 4 grants one. After that, every time the player beats that mansion again they may
respec the card once more. So a choice is never permanent and never free.

### The four grants

| quadrant | mansions | grant |
|---|---|---|
| **Byakko**, the white tiger | 1-6, 28 | its ground cannot be taken |
| **Suzaku**, the vermilion bird | 7-13 | once a board, tap it to strike its neighbours again |
| **Seiryuu**, the azure dragon | 14-20 | it may be lodged either way round |
| **Genbu**, the black tortoise | 21-27 | its station counts one more |

### The four builds

Because both choices are live, every card exists in four versions:

+2 numbers · +1 and the signature · the signature and the grant · +1 and the grant

The player's copy of a card is not the same object as anybody else's.

## 7. The seven things a card can do

Every signature and every grant is exactly one of these. There are no others.

**change a number** · **turn a card** · **add a fight** · **deny a fight** · **move a card** ·
**change what a station is worth** · **change whose a station is**

And exactly one of six triggers: **on lodge** · **on claim** · **on being struck** · **at the count**
· **always** · **on tap**.

Each quadrant owns two of the seven, and gets one card allowed to reach outside them.

## 8. Two rules the whole design rests on

**Depth goes in the faces and the fights, where the player can see it. The count stays simple,
because that is what they have to read.**

**A mansion's rule changes the geometry, never the arithmetic.**

## Open, and flagged rather than guessed

**Does the opponent also hold seven?** Written as yes. Seven each on nine stations is fourteen cards
for nine slots, against ten today. That is a much larger surplus and it changes the board's character
in ways nothing has measured yet.

**Is the mulligan cost right?** Seven to six to five. Hand size is the single largest lever measured
in this project, so dropping two cards is severe. It might want to be seven, six, six.

**The mansion rule is written for one mansion only.** The other twenty-seven need writing, against
the geometry rule.

**Level 4 is art, respec and presetting.** It carries no new mechanical power, on purpose. Whether
that feels like enough is a question for players rather than for a simulation.
