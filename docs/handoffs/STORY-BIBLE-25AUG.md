# The Moonroad — story bible and astrology bridge

**25 August 2026.** Assembled from the existing research corpus: `lore-source.md`,
`mansions.md`, `mansion-names.md`, the four `mansions-batch*.md`, `corpus-stations-22-28.md`,
`hunger-axis.md`, `jungian-folk.md`, `iching-model.md`, `rave-mandala.md`, and all eight files
in `manzil-loop/`. Roughly 470KB of prior work.

Nothing here invents lore. Everything is either quoted from that corpus or derived from it, and
where I depart from an existing decision I say so and give the reason.

---

## Part 1. Why the storytelling reads thin

The research is not thin. It is the strongest part of this project. Primary sources in Arabic and
Sanskrit, al-Bīrūnī's numbering declared canonical over Robson's, a three-flag honesty system
(`[THIN]` / `[VERIFY]` / `[NEEDS-HUMAN]`), a verify pass that logged real corrections, and a
copyright analysis sharp enough to identify a corrupt transliteration as a copying fingerprint.
Most shipped astrology products have nothing remotely like it.

The problem is five specific things, and none of them is "we need more lore."

**One. It is a museum, not a story.** Two independent passes over the whole corpus found no
protagonist, no place, no conflict, and no plot. What exists is reference material. Reference
does not move anyone. It supports something that moves people, and that something was never
written.

**Two. The fiction was classified as a skin.** The loop research literally calls it "the
astrology skin." Once fiction is a skin it is the first thing cut and the last thing polished.
It reads thin because it was scoped thin, on purpose, by a decision nobody has revisited.

**Three. The two halves speak different languages.** The astrology readings are second person,
lowercase, warm, addressed directly to the reader. The game is third person and spatial: roads,
mansions, lodging, slots. A player can play forty nights and never once be spoken to. The
product's best writing is in a place the game never visits.

**Four. There is no reason to be here.** The player claims a mansion and "name and date go on
it." That is a mechanic, not a motive. Meanwhile the corpus already contains the best motive
anyone is going to find and does not use it anywhere in the product.

**Five. The antagonist is missing, and the team keeps almost noticing.** The design docs call the
sky the opponent, then correctly observe that the sky is fixed, true and indifferent. Something
indifferent cannot be an antagonist. So there isn't one. But there is a pressure, and it has been
sitting in plain sight.

## Part 2. The spine

One sentence, and every part of it is already in the corpus:

> **The sky remembers everything. People remember almost nothing. The road is where you go to get
> some of it back.**

This is derived, not invented. The pieces:

- **Recollection is already canon.** `lore-source.md` maps the dream's recognition question onto
  Plato's *anamnesis*, using his own word: "to re-collect is to remember."
- **The recognition question is already written.** *do you know your history.* The corpus's own
  verdict: "It is not a belief, an assertion or a doctrine. It is one stranger asking another
  something, with the answer left open. That is the register the whole product has been reaching
  for, and it arrived pre-formed."
- **The physics is already written.** "every atom in you heavier than helium was made inside a
  star. you are, chemically, a piece of one." The Star Shard is a piece of the first star, older
  than the Earth by nine billion years.
- **The four traditions disagreeing is memory.** Four civilizations looked at the same sky and
  remembered it differently. None of them is wrong. That is exactly what memory does, and the
  project already decided to render the disagreement rather than harmonise it.
- **The card familiarity law is recollection, mechanically.** Level 2 drops the name, level 3
  drops the printed move, level 4 is nearly all art. You stop reading because you recognise.
  The corpus calls this "a progress signal impossible to render as a percentage." It is also,
  precisely, anamnesis.
- **The phrase already exists.** "The sky remembers" was proposed in `dormant-powers.md` as an
  in-fiction relief valve for clock manipulation. It is a better thesis than it is a patch.

### The antagonist is forgetting

Not a villain. A pressure, and a true one. The sky keeps perfect records and you do not. Every
night you do not play, the moon walks the road anyway.

This resolves something the ethics floor already demands. No streaks, no guilt states, absence
generates content rather than debt. Under the spine that stops being a restriction and becomes
the point: **the sky is not keeping score against you, it is waiting for you to catch up.** You
cannot fall behind something that is not chasing you.

### The protagonist is a latecomer

Not a chosen one. Someone who arrived late to something very old and is trying to catch up. That
is the honest emotional position of every person who gets interested in astrology, it is the
dream's own position ("we are very old", "you have survived so much"), and it is the only
protagonist stance that survives the product's refusal to prophesy.

### What the spine costs

Nothing. It changes no rule. Here is the entire mechanic set, reread:

| mechanic | what it means today | what it means under the spine |
|---|---|---|
| the road, nine mansions ahead | a 1x9 board | the moon's next nine nights. You can see ahead because the sky keeps its appointments |
| lodging a card | placing it | giving a traveler a room for the night |
| dominion, a card on its own mansion | doubled value | someone home, where they are known |
| claiming a mansion | name and date go on it | signing the guest book. Attendance, not conquest |
| First Light | an unlock | being recognised by a place you have been before |
| levels stripping the card's text | the familiarity law | you no longer read the name because you know the face |
| the sky as opponent | a deterministic AI | not an enemy. The sky is what actually happened. Losing to it is fine |
| losing costs nothing | an ethics-floor rule | of course. You cannot lose to a fact |
| four traditions disagreeing | divergence-as-content | memory is unreliable and still true |

That table is the pitch. It converts a list of ethical constraints into a coherent fiction
without touching the engine.

## Part 3. The thesis image

The corpus contains one fact better than everything else in it, and it is attached to the game's
own flagship mansion.

**Mansion 21, al-Balda, "the empty district."** The Arabic tradition marks it as the blank
stretch of sky, the station with nothing in it; π Sagittarii stands at its edge as a marker
rather than a resident. Ibn Qutaybah describes it as "a patch of sky with no stars in it."

And modern astronomy places **Sagittarius A\***, the black hole at the centre of the galaxy,
inside that station's span. Independently checked: the galactic centre sits at roughly 27
degrees of Sagittarius in the tropical frame, drifting about one degree per 72 years. The
station runs 17°09′ Sagittarius to 0°00′ Capricorn. It is inside, and it has been for centuries.

> **The emptiest room in the sky turns out to be the middle of everything.**

That is the brand thesis, the flagship card, and the tagline, and it is true. It also happens to
be what the whole product argues about people.

**One correction to make.** The research names mansion 3, the Gathered Stars, as "the flagship
quadruple," and mansion 21 has no long-form reading written at all. I would swap them. The
Gathered Stars is the best *demonstration* of the four-traditions method and should stay the
proof. Al-Balda is the better *flagship*, because it carries the sentence above.

**One contradiction to kill.** Mansion 21's card copy is about rest, space and stillness. Its
hunger line, inherited from the Sanskrit station Uttarāṣāḍhā, is "to win the thing that stays
won." Those are different people. The card copy is right and the hunger line should be rewritten
from al-Balda rather than from the Indian station, which is defensible precisely because the
research already documents that the offset between the two systems begins here.

## Part 4. The three changes that make it stop being thin

### 1. Give the sky one line a night

The brief forbids prophecy and it is right to. But there is a large gap between prophecy and
silence, and memory lives in it. The sky does not predict. It **notes**.

One line, before the board, drawn from state the game already has:

> the moon enters al-balda tonight. the district with no stars in it.
> you have been here four times.

> tonight is your own mansion. it has been twenty-eight days.

> the moon enters the void. the arabs called it the luckiest star there is.
> you have never taken it.

This is one string per mansion per state. It is the cheapest story change available and the
highest leverage, because it is the first time in forty nights the game speaks to the player.
No prophecy, no fourth wall, no fate. Only what happened, and when.

### 2. Write the 28 as characters, not entries

They already have hungers, and a hunger is a character. From `hunger-axis.md`:

- the listener: "to listen until the pattern shows itself." Cost: "the listener is rarely the
  one heard."
- the void: "to heal the thing nobody could name." Cost: "extremely good at holding space and
  notably worse at being held."
- the guide: "to see everyone home." Cost: "you stopped choosing a destination a while ago
  because escorting was simpler."
- the thread: "to tie the ends together." Cost: "you belong to both sides and are counted by
  neither."

Those are people. Right now they exist in a reading and not in the game, and the game's cards
are mechanics with names attached.

**This is also the fix for a live engine problem.** Sixteen of the twenty-eight signature
abilities currently do nothing measurable. The reason is that they were designed as mechanics
first and matched to lore second, which produces a lot of near-identical small effects. Design
each signature *from* its hunger and the variety arrives for free, because twenty-eight distinct
motives cannot produce sixteen identical mechanics. Worked examples:

| card | the hunger | what the mechanic should therefore do |
|---|---|---|
| the listener | listen until the pattern shows itself | the strongest ability in the game, measured. Already true. Keep it as the reference |
| the guide | see everyone home | move a card to its own mansion, or toward it. Currently trades two neighbours, which is generic |
| the thread | tie the ends together | act on slot 0 and slot 8 at once, the two ends of the road. Currently inert |
| the void | heal what nobody could name | restore a card that was flipped, once. Currently inert and weakens neighbours, which is the opposite of its reading |
| the hideaway | everyone's shelter, nobody's guest | protect a neighbour but never itself |
| the chamber | go still and deep enough to hold weight | cannot be moved or flipped, and does not act. A wall, honestly |

Each of those is a different verb. The current set is mostly one verb with different numbers.

### 3. Make the birth mansion the player's own card

This is the bridge and it does not exist yet.

Your sun's mansion is one of the twenty-eight. It should be in your deck from the first night,
it should be the first card the game hands you, and **claiming your own mansion on its own
night** should be the single most marked moment in the product. It comes around roughly every
27.3 days, it costs nothing, and it is already half built: First Light exists, and the corpus
already names the exact feeling, "I took al-Thurayyā on al-Thurayyā's night."

## Part 5. The astrology bridge

The real relationship between the two products is not that they share a theme. It is this:

> **The card game is the onboarding for the astrology app's symbol system.**

Nobody reads a glossary. Everybody knows a card they have played forty times. After a month of
Manzil a player knows what al-Thurayyā is, knows the Void was the luckiest star in one sky and
an empty circle of healers in another, knows the road wraps from 28 back to 1. That knowledge is
exactly what makes a reading land instead of wash over someone. Star Shard's hardest problem is
that its depth is invisible to a new user. The game solves that by making the depth playable
before it is ever explained.

Five concrete bridges, in build order:

**1. Deck to chart.** Sun, moon and rising mansions are three specific cards the player owns and
levels. The app's reading of them is then literally about objects they have held. This is the
one no competitor can copy quickly, because it requires both products.

**2. Claims to a personal ephemeris.** "Name and date go on it" accumulates into a dated record
of nights played. That is a diary, and the app can read it back: *you have claimed the Void three
times, always in winter.* A real pattern from real behaviour is more honest and more interesting
than any transit reading, and it costs no new content.

**3. First Light opens the reading.** Already designed. First Light "opens a further stanza," so
the game unlocks depth in the app. This is the Card Mod lesson the research already identified:
the minigame's cards must have value outside the minigame.

**4. The hunger axis serves both.** One system, two products. The app states the motive, the game
demonstrates it. A player who has watched the guide walk cards home for a month does not need to
be told what the guide wants.

**5. Divergence becomes a game mode.** When the four traditions disagree about a patch of sky,
that is a variant board. The Chinese sky counts 斗, the Dipper, where the Arabs count al-Balda.
Two ways to read the same night is two ways to play it. The project's clearest innovation claim
turns into content instead of a footnote.

## Part 6. The register, and the one line to hold

The corpus already fixed the tone and it is good: lowercase, second person, short declaratives,
one concrete verifiable fact per entry, "tradition says" before any claim, and the "before you
panic" move for ominous stations. Quiet, no fourth wall, no prophecy.

The rule that governs all of it, from `lore-source.md`:

> **Every miss must open a door, not close one.**

> Never "this is who you are." Always "that is what your sun's mansion says, your moon's says
> something different," which has the advantage of being true.

And the structural argument that keeps a many-layered system from hardening into the thing it is
competing with:

> **A system that shows you where its own sources conflict cannot become a doctrine. It has to
> stay a conversation.**

*No em dashes in any of this copy. That is a standing product rule and the sample lines above
follow it.*

## Part 7. Three decisions that need a person

**1. The dream's status.** `rave-mandala.md` sells "assembled from documented sources, computed
from the real sky, no voice in the night" as the differentiating origin story. `lore-source.md`,
written two days later and marked "Not copy. Source.", canonises a literal voice in the night.
Nothing reconciles them.

My recommendation, in writing: **the dream is lore, never authority.** It supplies the world's
emotional canon, which is survival, deep age, love as the assembling force, scale, and the
recognition question. It never supplies a truth claim about how the system works. That is exactly
the line Human Design crossed, and crossing it is what bought them permanent debunking. Star
Shard's origin story stays "assembled from documented sources." The dream lives in the fiction,
where it is safe and where it is genuinely moving.

**2. The flagship.** Al-Balda over the Gathered Stars, for the reason in Part 3. If that is
accepted, mansion 21 needs its long-form reading written, because it currently has none.

**3. Mansion 21's hunger line.** Rewrite from al-Balda, not from Uttarāṣāḍhā. See Part 3.

## Part 8. What to do next, in order

1. **Decide the three items in Part 7.** They are cheap and everything else leans on them.
2. **Write the sky's nightly line.** One string per mansion per state, maybe sixty lines total.
   Highest leverage per word in the project.
3. **Redesign the sixteen inert signatures from their hunger lines**, not from mechanical
   symmetry. This is already on Design's plate as an engine problem; it should be handed over as
   a writing problem with a mechanical constraint, which is a much easier brief to answer well.
4. **Build the birth-mansion card.** Deck seeding plus First Light on the player's own station.
5. **Write mansion 21's long-form reading** and put the galactic-centre sentence in it.
6. **Then** the marketing line, which writes itself once 1 through 5 exist.

### Where this document sits

This is a synthesis of prior research, not a replacement for it. The corpus stays canonical for
facts, sources and per-mansion detail. This file is canonical only for the spine, the diagnosis
and the sequencing above.

Nothing here changes any measured number, any engine behaviour, or any item in
`HANDOFF-CODE-ENGINE-25AUG.md`. The one place it touches live work is item 3 above, which is a
reframing of the sixteen inert signatures rather than a change to what has to be built.
