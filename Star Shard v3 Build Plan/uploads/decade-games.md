# Games people still play 5–20 years later, and feel good about

*Research notes for MANZIL-LOOP.md · agent report, 19 Aug 2026. Builds on, does not repeat, RETENTION.md.*

## 0. The frame: loops beat arcs, and "fun is learning" has a half-life

**Daniel Cook, "Loops and Arcs" (2012).** Arcs "burn out rapidly"; loops "are well suited for mastery tasks." "Remove any elements of a computer game that you can 'beat' or that render the game boring or meaningless upon repeated play." ([Lostgarden](https://lostgarden.com/2012/04/30/loops-and-arcs/comment-page-1/))

**Raph Koster, *A Theory of Fun*.** "Fun is just another word for learning." "A game is destined to become boring, automated, cheated, and exploited." ([Goodreads quotes](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/19639-a-theory-of-fun-for-game-design)) A game that lasts 20 years must either (a) be too deep to fully learn (chess, Go, TGM), (b) keep feeding new *situations* without new *rules* (FreeCell seeds, Trackmania TOTD, Into the Breach), or (c) turn the player's own growth into the content (Celeste, Monster Hunter, speedrunning). Manzil sits in (b) and wants (a) and (c).

Quantic Foundry's 1.57M-gamer panel: the Strategy motivation slid from the 50th to the 33rd percentile, June 2015 → April 2024, across gender and region ([Quantic Foundry 2024](https://quanticfoundry.com/2024/05/21/strategy-decline/)). Depth must be *legible*, not homework.

## 1. Infinite depth from tiny rule sets

- **Sid Meier** (GDC 2012): "Games are a series of interesting decisions." Uninteresting if players always pick the same option or pick at random. Three shapes: risk vs reward, short vs long term, *personal preference*. ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-sid-meier-on-how-to-see-games-as-sets-of-interesting-decisions))
- **Reiner Knizia:** "simple games, but then the people bring themselves into it. And you see out of the simplicity, a second level of depth. That keeps you playing." ([Daniel Solis](https://danielsolisblog.blogspot.com/2015/11/8-quotes-for-game-designers-from.html))
- **Rosewater, "Lenticular Design"** (2014): comprehension / board / strategic complexity; a lenticular card reads simple to a novice and deep to an expert ([Wizards](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/lenticular-design-2014-12-15)).
- **Rosewater, "Twenty Years, Twenty Lessons"** (GDC 2016): #5 "Don't confuse 'interesting' with 'fun'"; #10 "Leave room for the player to explore"; #11 "If everyone likes your game but no one loves it, it will fail"; #13 "Make the fun part also the correct strategy to win"; #16 "Be more afraid of boring your players than challenging them"; #17 "You don't have to change much to change everything"; #18 "Restrictions breed creativity." ([Part 1](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/twenty-years-twenty-lessons-part-1-2016-05-30), [Part 2](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/twenty-years-twenty-lessons-part-2-2016-06-06), [Part 3](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/twenty-years-twenty-lessons-part-3-2016-06-13))
- **Timmy/Johnny/Spike** ([Wizards 2006](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/timmy-johnny-and-spike-revisited-2006-03-20-2)); Commander's rise ([MTG Rocks](https://mtgrocks.com/mtg-lead-designer-reveals-why-commander-is-so-popular/)).
- **Garfield / *Characteristics of Games***: randomness as catch-up; heuristics for new and advanced players ([review](https://onlinedungeonmaster.com/2013/01/28/book-review-characteristics-of-games/); [BGDL interview](https://boardgamedesignlab.com/luck-vs-skill-with-richard-garfield/)). The "luck gives the loser an excuse" line: paraphrase, not verified verbatim.
- Counter-argument: Elyot Grant, "Why luck kills strategy games" — deterministic-but-varied starting states ([Prismata blog](http://blog.prismata.net/2014/07/15/luck-in-games/) — not fetched; paraphrase).
- **Tetris: The Grand Master**: 20G, level counter makes speed the consequence of play, grades measure consistency, Gm requires an invisible roll ([Tildes](https://tildes.net/~games/rph/a_comprehensive_deep_dive_into_tetris_the_grandmaster_tgm_design_the_hidden_japanese_tetris_version); [tetris.wiki](https://www.tetris.wiki/20G)).
- Failure mode: the *optimize-the-fun-out* trap — Mark Brown on Balatro: "given the opportunity, players will optimize the fun out of a game" ([GMTK](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/balatros-cursed-design-problem)).

## 2. Long-tail single-player loops

**Into the Breach**: every enemy telegraphs its exact attack; losing sends a pilot back in time; loss condition is collateral damage, not mech death ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_the_Breach); [Cane and Rinse](https://caneandrinse.com/into-the-breach/)). GDC 2019 postmortem: [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026333/-Into-the-Breach-Design) (slides not fetched). Put all RNG *before* the player's turn so the turn is a pure puzzle. Lazzaro's Hard Fun / *fiero* in its purest form ([Lazzaro](https://www.nicolelazzaro.com/the4-keys-to-fun/)).

**Hades**: Kasavin: "the part where roguelikes can be brutally difficult is, ironically, directly at odds with the part where they're so replayable"; God Mode +20% resistance, +2% per death, cap 80%; story advances on death ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/hades-god-mode-interview); [GDC podcast](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/roguelikes-and-narrative-design-with-i-hades-i-creative-director-greg-kasavin)).

**Slay the Spire**: Ascension is 20 stacked *subtractions* ([wiki](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Ascension)); "The core design of Slay the Spire is risk versus reward" ([Think Like a Game Designer](https://justingarydesign.substack.com/p/anthony-giovannetti-crafting-slay)).

**Dead Cells** Boss Stem Cells ([Medium](https://medium.com/@tunganh0806/difficulty-as-design-dead-cells-progressive-challenge-and-player-engagement-74f086064bf6)). **Dwarf Fortress** "losing is fun" ([Doug Clow](https://dougclow.org/2011/11/23/losing-is-fun-what-we-can-learn-from-dwarf-fortress/)). **Vampire Survivors** ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/vampire-survivors-how-developers-used-gambling-psychology-to-create-a-bafta-winning-game-203613)). **Juul, *The Art of Failure*** ([jesperjuul.net](https://jesperjuul.net/artoffailure/)).

## 3. Collection and mastery systems

- **Living Card Games (FFG)**: fixed distribution removes *chance* anxiety but can import *completion* anxiety ([Polyhedron Collider](https://www.polyhedroncollider.com/2015/12/the-lcg-system-is-just-as-broken-as.html)).
- **Monster Hunter**: "predictable structure with unpredictable execution"; progression is player knowledge ([Kokutech](https://www.kokutech.com/blog/gamedev/design-patterns/flow-state/monster-hunter-world)).
- **Endowed progress**: 19% vs 34% completion, blank 8-stamp card vs 10-stamp with 2 pre-stamped ([Coglode](https://www.coglode.com/nuggets/endowed-progress-effect)). **IKEA effect** only when the build is completed ([HBS](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=41121)).
- **Destiny 2 Trials**: ~400k to 154k in one season ([Forbes/Tassi](https://www.forbes.com/sites/paultassi/2020/05/11/everyone-is-wrong-about-whats-wrong-with-trials-in-destiny-2/)); catalyst grinds ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/destiny-2-exotic-weapon-catalyst-kills-progression-bad/)).

## 4. Mastery curves; deterministic dailies

**Trackmania TOTD / COTD**: daily at 19:00 CET, 24-hour leaderboard, medals; COTD: 15-min qualifier, divisions of ≤64 by own time, knockout ([TOTD doc](https://doc.trackmania.com/play/what-is-totd/); [COTD doc](https://doc.trackmania.com/play/how-to-play-cotd/)). ~8k finishers per track in a 2021 scrape ([vjeux](https://blog.vjeux.com/2021/javascript/trackmania-medal-distribution.html)).

**FreeCell**: 32,000 numbered deals; #11982 unsolvable; ~1 in 84,000 impossible across 8.6B ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FreeCell)).

**Devil Daggers**: 500-second achievement reached by 641 of 277,789 ranked players ([Hookshot](https://www.hookshot.ink/blog/devil-daggers)). **Super Hexagon**: Cavanagh on frustration vs challenge ([Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/features/the-making-of-super-hexagon-the-game-terry-cavanagh-never-expected-anyone-else-to-get)). **Celeste**: Assist Mode renamed from "Cheat Mode" ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/celeste-difficulty-assist-mode/)). **Geometry Dash**: "each death contains information" ([playgeometrydash](https://playgeometrydash.com/the-psychology-of-grinding-how-top-players-stay-motivated/)).

## 5. Tabletop enduring loops

**Backgammon's doubling cube**: *Vogue* (1929) said doubling "gave it a new stimulus" to a game that had become "a dead and discarded pastime" ([NE Backgammon Club](https://nebackgammon.org/index.php/early-doublers/)). Snap's snap is the cube plus hidden information ([Eric Guan](https://ericguan.substack.com/p/marvel-snaps-ancient-inspiration); [Brode GDC](https://gdcvault.com/play/1029024/Designing-MARVEL-SNAP)).

## 6. Theory, briefly

- Lazzaro's four keys; "best selling games offer at least three of the 4" ([Lazzaro](https://www.nicolelazzaro.com/the4-keys-to-fun/)).
- Cozy (Project Horseshoe / Lostgarden): safety, abundance, softness; broken by extrinsic rewards, notifications, social comparison ([Lostgarden 2018](https://lostgarden.com/2018/01/24/cozy-games/)); 2024–26 academic debate ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/392857647_If_all_is_cozy_what_isn't_Some_conceptual_problems_regarding_cozy_games); [Game Studies](https://gamestudies.org/2602/articles/svelch_playdate)).
- Bogost, *Play Anything* ([Slate](https://slate.com/technology/2016/09/ian-bogosts-new-book-explains-what-it-means-to-play.html)).

## Implications for Manzil

1. You have built Track of the Day / FreeCell #N without the social handle: give each night a receipt, and publish "every night solvable".
2. Divisions, not a leaderboard.
3. Your tension is Into the Breach with the enemy's attack hidden; add an optional Open rule as a nightly variant.
4. Add a cube ("call the sky").
5. The 1–4 ladder is an unlock curve, not a mastery curve; put the long tail on the opponent and constraints.
6. The familiarity law is lenticular design; extend it to the sky.
7. Failure needs to produce something (codex line, replay).
8. The collection is an LCG: watch completion anxiety; 28-grid complete-able, event set openly endless.
9. The walking five must be the *correct* layer (Rosewater #13); dominion is the defence; signatures strong against named planets.
10. For year three the AI needs to change character (temperaments), plus async human play.
11. Keep the ItB/Super Hexagon cadence; show progress per night.
12. Year-three Manzil: same board, same two numbers; variance, constraint, legibility.
