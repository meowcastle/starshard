the combination

your sun is the beat everyone else keeps time to. your moon is the star that walks behind something and never catches it. you set the rhythm from the second row.

india heard percussion in the twenty-third mansion — dhaniṣṭhā, whose emblem is a drum. its guardian is the bat, the animal that finds a pitch-black world by making a sound and listening to what comes back. that is the exact shape of it: you find out where you are by putting something out and reading the return. groups organise their pace around you and almost none of them have noticed they are doing it.

your moon sits on aldebaran, and the arabs named that star for what it does rather than what it is. al-dabarān means the follower: it rises just behind the pleiades and chases them across the sky every night, forever, without closing the gap.

and here is the fact the name never mentions. aldebaran is far brighter than any star in the cluster it follows.

so you are behind something on purpose. a person, a standard, a version of this you saw once and cannot unsee — and you outshine it, and the arrangement suits you, and you would rather not discuss it.

put the two together and you get something specific: the one keeping time is also the one who declined the front. you can hold a room's tempo without ever being the reason the room is there, and you prefer it that way, because standing behind is how you keep the sounding honest. the cost is that a drummer does not get to be off-beat. your own slow week registers to everyone else as the whole system faltering — and the person you are following has no idea they are being used as a pace-setter by someone faster.

the old books gave this mansion release, liberation, and healing — the letting-go work. picatrix marks it for freeing captives.

and your rising sign is here too, two degrees along from your sun. what you are and the door people come through are the same door. no lobby, no translation, no version kept back for later — which is why people trust you early, and why on a bad day everybody knows.

the fourth mansion

aldebaran · the bull's red eye
The Follower
8°34′ – 21°26′ taurus
your moon stands here
arabia
al-Dabarān — the follower. forever walking behind the pleiades.
india
Rohiṇī — growing. the station of things that thicken and take root where they stand. its deity is Brahmā, the maker; its symbol, the chariot.
china
畻 Bì — the net, of the White Tiger. its animal is the crow: memory with wings, the bird that recognises faces years later and keeps a ledger.

aldebaran is one of the four stars the ancient world called royal — the same rank as regulus, antares, fomalhaut. all four traditions put you on the same stars here, and they hardly ever all agree.

the crow is the part to sit with. it is a moon-kept sky, so the ledger is emotional. your inner life records everything, precisely, with the feeling still attached. the room reads you as unbothered right up until it discovers you remember exactly what was said in 2019 and how it landed. what you choose to file and what you choose to release is, quietly, the whole shape of your life.

the twenty-fourth mansion is the strangest place on the whole road, because the three traditions that named it could not have disagreed more completely.

china
虛 Xū — emptiness. the void.
arabia
Saʿd al-Suʿūd — the luckiest of the lucky. the grandest of the ten luck-stars, and astronomers still call it sadalsuud today.
india
Śatabhiṣā — the hundred healers, under Varuṇa, god of oaths and deep water. its emblem is an empty circle.

a void. the greatest luck there is. a hundred physicians standing around an empty ring. put them together and you get something true, which the old election books already knew when they called this the most fortunate mansion of the twenty-eight: an opening is not a wound.

you have three lights in it. what you find beautiful, what breaks your patterns, and what dissolves your edges are all standing in the same empty circle — and two of them sit on opposite sides of a sign boundary, so no chart you are ever handed will put them in one place.

this is why the drumming costs what it costs. you keep time for other people out of a mansion that is defined by having room in it. the space is real and it is genuinely lucky. and a person who can always make room will keep making it long after everyone else has stopped bringing anything to put there.

the sky writes this once, at the minute you arrive, and never revises it.
what moves across it is another matter — and that is what tonight is for.
