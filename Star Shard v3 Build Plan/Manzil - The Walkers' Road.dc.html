the walkers' road

a mansion's first night is not the sky cold. it is a short ladder of other walkers' fives, single boards, piloted by her own hands, and it ends at the sky herself, decently harder, wearing the mansion's own rule. this sheet carries the calls and what kills each one.

the law
the ladder is an introduction, never a toll.
the shape

{{ shapeLine }} walkers are other players' real walking fives: asynchronous, anonymous, positive-only. nobody is told they were beaten, nobody is ranked, nobody is named without consent. clear the rungs and the night ends at the hardened sky. win her board and the mansion is claimed: name and date go on the card, and the ladder there is done forever. every later visit goes straight to her.

why it earns a place: it is the people fun the game lacks, without chat, timers, or a leaderboard. it gives the walking five a second audience (your hand walks other people's nights while you sleep). and twenty-eight authored skies cycling with the moon are the structural answer to the oracle: a deterministic opponent whose behavior cannot be learned once and posted.

the calls · five, with what kills each
cold start: the road's own. before there are players, the rungs are authored hands framed honestly as the road's own: named walkers of the road, not fake accounts. as real fives accumulate they take the rungs over, oldest fiction first. the frame is lore, the replacement is silent.
kill it if: players believe the road's own are real people (the frame must read as the world, not as deception) · or real hands never reach half the rungs by the second moon.
falling off: the night still gives. lose on a rung and the night ends there. the road-shard is given regardless (the 28 are never skill-gated), and the ladder resumes where it stood on the next visit. no re-walk, no decay, nothing lost but the boards you enjoyed.
kill it if: resuming mid-ladder feels like debt on return (then cleared rungs must read as claimed ground, not unfinished business).
rollout: four with the moon. authored skies ship four at a time, riding ahead of the moon's real crossing. the mansion's rule + one planet temperament is the whole authoring unit; the encounter animation carries the rest. mansions without their sky yet end at the plain hardened sky.
kill it if: a night's rule cannot be learned from one loss (then the rule is noise, rewrite it before shipping the next four).
gating: lifetime, per mansion. the ladder gates the game-shard and the duel only, once, per mansion, per lifetime. it never gates the road-shard, and it never returns once the mansion is claimed.
kill it if: anyone proposes a daily ladder, a rotating ladder, or a ladder reset. that is a toll. the answer is no.
after the claim: the walkers keep the mansion. a claimed mansion's nights go back to the walkers, best of five, the walker fixed per mansion so a place keeps a face. the sky herself returns only when the moon crosses one of your chart's own mansions, and then she stays for the whole match. she is an occasion, and the temperaments belong to her alone: they rest on rungs and on walker nights.
kill it if: walker nights read as a downgrade from her rather than as the mansion settling into ordinary life · or her return is frequent enough to stop being an event.
her hardening · the boss at the top
a deeper read. {{ hardenLine }} still deterministic, still seeded, still the same for everyone tonight.
the mansion's own rule. the storm's night, ties are hers. the veil's night, her revealed cards close again. the thread's night, the road wraps. the void's night, a card standing alone cannot be claimed. the rule is printed at the encounter, learnable in one board, and recycles the 28 signatures already written.
one temperament. one planet's flavor on her tie-breaks: mars overvalues captures, saturn defends ground, venus plays late. a tell, never noise. this is also where the one-ply gap lives (29.7% vs the 35–45 target, addendum 4): the casual player's winnable boards are the rungs, not her.
the numbers · addendum 3, ladder_sim.py
the rungs are a rampcareful play: 89 · 53 · 81 · 32 · 58% of boards up the five rungs, casual: 77 · 57 · 65 · 42 · 46. the zigzag is lead alternation (the walker leads rungs two and four). inside three minutes.
the boss is a wallthe hardened sky takes careful play from 65 to 54% of boards, casual from 29 to 12. she is a genuine boss for the careful and a wall for the casual: the ladder is what feeds players over her.
with the stonefirst-to-5 points, her call by her printed rule: careful play wins ~64% of matches (8 to 100% by mansion), casual ~8%. mean 4.3 boards, wall-clock unchanged. the per-mansion spread is the argument for the 28 rules: some nights are already bosses by dominion alone.
single boards, to a pointevery rung is one board, never a match. the sky at the top is the exception: she is a best of five, because reaching her should not be settled by one deal. the ladder's whole wall-clock still stays under the match it leads to.
not simmed yetthe numbers above are addendum 3, run on five rungs at a flat reply weight. eight rungs on a monotone ramp, and the sky as a match rather than a board, are both un-simmed: re-run manzil-engine-v5.js before any of this is locked.
the toll risk

the failure mode is not difficulty, it is obligation. the ladder must read as meeting the road's people, not as stages before the real game. that is why rungs are single boards, why falling off gives the shard anyway, why nothing ever resets, and why a claimed mansion never shows the ladder again. if any surface starts counting rungs at the player, it has become a toll.

kill the whole road if

· playtesters skip-wish the ladder on more than a third of first nights (introduction has become obstruction) · walkers feel like bots with names (the people fun is the point; if it doesn't land, the hardened sky alone is cheaper) · casual players stall on rungs and never meet her (then the ladder must shorten by skill, not lengthen) · the 28 skies drift into agency-removing rules (swap, chaos, random: the refused list holds on boss nights too).

the walker's consent · designed 20 aug 2026
what leaves the devicefive card ids and their levels. nothing else: no name, no chart, no birth data, no record of when you play. the five is piloted by her AI on the other road; your play style never travels.
the askonce, the first time a walking five is saved, in plain words: "your five may walk other roads at night, carried by the sky, wearing a road name. no one sees your name or your chart." two buttons, let it walk / keep it home. no default; the question waits.
the road nameassigned by the road from a fixed list (imra, sef, odel, brann...), never typed by the player. no text entry means no moderation surface: the compliance floor for the 13 to 17 quarter of the audience.
what a walker hears backpositive only, one line in the codex: "your five walked 3 roads this month." never a loss, never who met it, never a count of defeats. the five is an ambassador, not a ranking.
leavingthe toggle lives in the codex beside the walking five, off anytime, removal immediate: the five stops walking that night. roads mid-ladder finish with an authored walker instead; nothing is owed.
open flags

the shape of the ramp (eight rungs and the 3–11 reply ladder are feel calls, un-simmed) · rung count by skill (can the ladder read the player and shorten itself?) · whether ladder boards replay as practice after the claim · whether the stone rides her match on first nights or rests until the mansion is claimed · the name: walkers vs the road's own.
