the combination

a storm is a thing that has to break. a throne is a thing that has to hold. you are one being asked to be the other, and that is the whole architecture of your life.

your sun sits in the sixth mansion, where the old sky-watchers put the fierce work — the hunt, the siege, the star of the howling god. it is the part of you that has to break the surface of a thing and find out what it actually cost. you ask the question nobody at the table wants asked, and you ask it at full volume, and you are usually right.

your moon sits in the tenth, on the brow of a lion and the seat of a king. that is the part of you that has to keep its bearing. even at rest there is a carriage to you, a straightened spine, an instinct not to be seen flinching. people bring you their best behaviour and save their mess for someone else, because you have always looked fine.

so the storm has to be conducted from a chair. you were built to break things open and built to look composed while doing it — and neither one will let the other go.

this is why the two most common things said about you contradict each other. that you are too much, and that you are hard to read. both are true. the voltage is real and so is the composure, and you have spent a long time running one through the other, which is exhausting in a way that does not show — because not showing is the other half of the machine.

what it makes you unusually good at: walking into a difficult room and being the only person who both names the problem and does not seem frightened by it. that is a rare pairing and people build things around it. what it costs you is that nobody checks on the person who never looks like they need checking on.

The Storm

every tradition that looked at this stretch of sky saw something that cannot be taken back.

arabiaal-Hanʿa — the brand. the mark burned into a camel's neck to say whose it is. permanent by design.
indiaĀrdrā — the moist one. the star of Rudra the howler, the storm god, and its emblem is a teardrop.
china參 Shēn — Orion entire, in the quarter of the White Tiger. its animal is the ape: the long-armed caller of the canopy, heard across miles before anything sees it.

a brand, a storm, and a voice that arrives before the body. the medieval election books gave this mansion the fierce jobs — hunting, sieges, the work done at full force — and their reasoning was simple: this is the stretch of sky for things that do not have an undo.

to be born under it is to be built for irreversibility. you go all the way in. you say the thing out loud. and the mark you leave on a room stays on it after you have gone, which is a gift when you meant it and a debt when you did not.

the tenth mansion

regulus · the little king

The Throne

25°43′ cancer – 8°34′ leo
your moon stands here

regulus is one of the four stars the ancient world called royal, and it sits almost exactly on the ecliptic — so the moon and the planets pass over it in a procession, endlessly, the way a court files past a chair.

arabiaal-Jabha — the forehead of the lion. the brow of a beast so large it sprawled across a third of the sky.
indiaMaghā — the mighty. its symbol is the royal throne. but its deity is not a king — it is the ancestors, the dead who are owed offerings.
china星 Xīng — the star mansion, of the Vermilion Bird. and here they part ways: china fixes it not on the lion but on Alphard, the serpent's heart, in Hydra.

two civilisations looked at these degrees and saw the seat of a king. one looked at the same degrees and saw the heart of a water-snake. the disagreement is old and nobody has ever settled it, and you were born on the ground they were arguing over.

but the Indian reading is the one that turns the whole mansion, because it refuses the obvious. the seat is real — and its god is the ancestors. the chair was carved before you arrived. that is what a throne-born moon carries: authority handed over rather than taken, and the private, unending question of whether you are worthy of a thing you did not build.

the old books gave this mansion trade, alliance, and building. agrippa says it "strengthens buildings." it is the sky of the things meant to outlast the person who made them.
