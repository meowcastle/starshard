## Spoken

The real name of tonight's station is Luck of the Slaughterer.

Saʿd al-Dhābiḥ. Two stars at the horns of Capricorn. The old Arab sky-watchers saw a man with the sheep he was about to kill, and called the station lucky.

Four stations in a row carry that word, saʿd, luck. Luck of the Slaughterer. Luck of the Swallower. Luck of Lucks. Luck of the Tents.

Luck of the Slaughterer is the honest translation, and it is not a name for a card a kid picks up at bedtime.

So the station takes its English name from a different sky. India called these degrees Śravaṇa, the ear, the one who hears, and marked them with Altair. China called them Niú, the ox.

In Star Shard it is the Listener. The Arabic stays on the page underneath, translated straight.

The skies only half agree here. India's star is Altair. The Arabs' two are in Capricorn, a hand's width away. Same degrees of the Moon's path, different star to hang them on.

## Facts checked

- Saʿd al-Dhābiḥ, "the luck of the slaughterer", on α and β Capricorni; the star name Dabih comes from it. The four saʿd stations are 22 Saʿd al-Dhābiḥ, 23 Saʿd Bulaʿ (the swallower), 24 Saʿd al-Suʿūd (luck of lucks) and 25 Saʿd al-Akhbiya (the tents) (`research/mansions-table.json`, ids 22 to 25).

- Altair to β Capricorni is about 22 degrees of sky; a hand's width at arm's length is about 20.
- "The Listener" is the slate epithet, drawn from Śravaṇa, with the Arabic kept in the depth layer (`research/mansion-names.md` row 22).
