## Spoken

Two peoples four thousand miles apart named this star the same word.

This is Antares. Red, in the chest of the Scorpion, low in the south on a summer night.

The Arabs called this stretch of the Moon's path al-Qalb. The heart.

The Chinese called it Xīn. The heart.

The Chinese sky had no scorpion. These stars were the heart of the Azure Dragon. Same star, same organ, different animal.

The Greeks had called it the Scorpion's heart too, and the Arabic star name carries their phrase. The Chinese name does not. It was in Chinese star lists by the fifth century BC.

India called the same star Jyeṣṭhā, the eldest, and gave it Indra, king of the gods.

Antares itself means rival of Mars. A red star that holds its own against the red planet whenever the two share a sky.

The Moon has 28 stations. On this one, every sky agrees.

Tonight the moon is in the Heart. starshard.net.

## Facts checked

- al-Qalb is the 18th manzil; 心 Xīn is the fifth xiu of the Azure Dragon of the East, its heart; Jyeṣṭhā, "the eldest", is the nakshatra on Antares with Indra as its deity (`research/mansions-table.json`, id 18).
- The Arabic star name Qalb al-ʿAqrab translates the Greek kardia skorpiou (`research/corpus-mansions.md` `MANSION.18.synthesis`). The 28 xiu are listed in full on the Zeng Hou Yi lacquer box, 433 BC.
- Antares, from the Greek anti-Ares, "rival of Ares", for its colour against Mars.
