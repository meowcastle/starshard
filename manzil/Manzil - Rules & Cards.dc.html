six laws under everything

ties flip. equal numbers claim like bigger ones, both ways. and a card taken by a tie fights on: it attacks both its own neighbours the moment it turns. a board changes hands about five times.

tied counts are yours. a level road at the count falls to you: the sky already claims ties in the fight, so the draw does not tax you twice.

the card is the card. a mansion's two numbers never change. it fights the same on its first night as its hundredth, and levels only wear away what is printed around them.

dominion. a card lodged on its own mansion glows ✦ and counts as two at the count. the ground keeps that weight whoever claims the card. her planets hold dominion where they truly stand tonight. jupiter counts two everywhere; on his own mansion, three. dominion is never hinted on the deck screen: spotting it is skill.

the seeded sky. her judgment is deterministic; where her arithmetic ties, a date seed breaks it. the same for everyone tonight, different tomorrow. nothing else is random.

the night's temper. one planet stands closest each night and flavors her even choices, printed in one header line, learnable in a board: mars strikes (tied, she picks the biggest fight) · saturn guards (she keeps her ground) · venus keeps company (she lodges beside the crowd) · mercury roams (she lodges apart) · jupiter spends big (she plays her largest, and his mercy is real: jupiter's nights run softer). tempers rest on road nights.

the table

nine mansions, two colors

the board is the road ahead of her: her next nine nights (she crosses one mansion a night; the board never claims otherwise). your ground is parchment; the sky's is amber. a card's two numbers are its two sides: left fights left, right fights right; claims flip the loser's color. one comparison, once, with two exceptions: a card taken by a tie strikes on, and the listener strikes what lands beside it.

her five planets stand open, where they truly stand tonight. nothing about her is hidden and nothing needs to be: she is deterministic, greedy arithmetic that reads your best reply before she commits, so the same night deals the same duel for everyone. the difficulty is how far ahead she reads, never what she is given.

the deck

your walking twelve

you carry twelve mansions, changed anytime, and five are dealt from them at the start of every board, face up. the deal is seeded to the night: the same for everyone tonight, different tomorrow, and tonight's own mansion always walks with you when you carry it. chance sits before the board, in the open. inside the board nothing is hidden, so reading ahead still pays.

all 28 mansions play from day one: unclaimed ones as loaners, grey and frameless at level 1, the sky's loan. winning a mansion's night claims it: your date goes on the card and its levels unlock. every owned card is a receipt of a real night.

levels, earned by the road: L2 wakes the signature move. L3 and L4 buy nothing but quiet: the face sheds the name, then the printed move, then gives itself to the art with small numbers. borders richen with level, TTA-style. hold any card to read it in full. nothing is ever sold.

first nights

the walkers' road

an unclaimed mansion's night is not the sky cold. it is a climb: eight single boards against other walkers' fives, piloted by her own judgment, each hand reading deeper than the last, then the sky herself at the top, best of five, wearing the mansion's own rule. the storm's night is storm weather: every face on the board fights at −1, never below one. the veil's night, her cards close again. the thread's night, the road wraps. the void's night, a lone card cannot be claimed.

the climb is told by altitude, never by a counter. the first board stands on the ground: warm horizon, ground haze, the stars barely out. from there the low air, the dusk, the cloud line, the high air, the cold air, the thin air, the edge of the air. she waits in open space, full stars, no horizon at all. the sky you play under is the only progress bar.

fall off a rung and the night ends there: the road-shard is given anyway, and the ladder waits where it stood for your next visit. beat her at the top and the mansion is claimed. the ladder is lifetime-per-mansion, an introduction, never a toll.

after the claim the mansion is yours and its nights go back to the walkers, best of five. the sky herself returns only when the moon crosses one of your chart's own mansions, and then she stays for the whole match. she is an occasion, not a fixture.

her nights only · in the prototype, off by default

the match as an ascent

a best of five that opens rather than repeats. the first board is small: three planets on a road of six. she holds three, you still hold your five, so the board asks which three you commit. the road is nine from board two, and by board three she holds all five. her hand fills weakest planets first, so the slow sky arrives last and the match gets heavier as it goes.

between boards you take one choice, one tap: trade your weakest card for the best in the pool · wake a sleeping signature for the rest of the match · her brightest planet sits out the next board. the choice is auto-targeted, so it costs a beat, not a menu.

the ascent belongs to the sky alone: it runs on the nights she returns, never on the walkers' road and never on a walker's night. no outer planet is ever dealt on board five, and the ascent never touches the road-shard.

expansion rule · opt-in, off by default

the eclipse fortnight

on the rare night an eclipse stands on the road ahead, the table may stretch: fourteen stations, eclipse to the answering lunation, a fortnight of road. seven cards each fill it. you bring seven; her hand is the five planets and two of the slow sky, standing where they truly stand tonight.

the outers play as themselves: uranus swaps two lodged cards · neptune turns her numbers to mist until played against · pluto removes one card from the road. best of three boards; the road is longer, the match is not.

fourteen is even, so a count can level. a level road falls to whoever holds the eclipse's own station. that slot is the board's anchor; hold it and ties are yours.

designed, not yet dealt. the nine-station road is the whole game, and a longer road adds turns rather than decisions, so before this is ever dealt the fortnight has to earn its length through pressure per placement: the outers, the level count at the anchor, never stations for their own sake.
