the moonstone

backgammon's doubling cube, called into the sky. a second game of judgment on top of the first, with zero new rules on the road. this sheet is the whole design: it gets ten paper matches before it gets a line of code.

the law
the road never changes. only what the night is worth.
what it is

before any board, either side may call the sky: this board counts two at the match. the other side answers. accept, and the board is played for two pips. let it stand, and the board falls to the caller uncontested for one, and the road moves on. nothing beyond the match is ever at stake: no shards, no levels, no claims, no time.

why it earns a place: the match length becomes chosen rather than fixed. a confident night ends in two boards. a tight night goes the distance because both sides keep answering. and calling well is a skill the road itself never tests: reading five closed cards from one open gesture.

the phases · four, not six

a first-to-three match has room for one call and one answer. the stone shows where the night stands:

new moonat rest. either side may call before a board begins.
waxing crescentyou called, she accepted. the board counts two, and the stone is hers: only she may raise again.
waning crescentshe called, you accepted. the board counts two, and the stone is yours.
full moonthe answer raise, from whoever holds the stone: the match rides on this board. accept, or let the match stand to the caller.
her rules · printed, learnable
when she calls. {{ callLine }} the number is a paper dial: tune it match by match and write down where the judgment gets interesting.
when she accepts. she accepts your call while she is behind or even at the match: a trailing sky wants the storm. ahead, she lets marginal boards stand and keeps her lead.
her call is a tell. her hand is closed but her rule is not. when the stone turns, you have learned something true about the five she hides. the one hidden thing in the game finally leaks, and only through her judgment.
calling judgment · what the hands change
the gathered stars counts two wherever it lands: the safest call in the deck rides with it.
the blaze its ground stays yours: one point guaranteed makes a call cheaper to back.
the heart burns while she holds more of the road: the board you would call from behind is the board it fights hardest.
the storm claims ties, and the hideaway makes ties likelier: a tie-prone road is a storm road. call it.
her saturn locked ground you cannot flip. when her call comes early, saturn is usually why: answer slower.
her jupiter counts two where she stands it. her jupiter nights pull her threshold closer: expect the stone.
nights
outer event nights. the stone rests. uranus swapping lodged cards under a full moon is chaos stacked on chaos: v1 keeps them apart.
the eclipse fortnight. already carries its own stake, the level count at the anchor. the stone stays home.
threshold nights. your sun's, your moon's, your rising's mansion: does the stone wake on its own those nights? open flag, decide after paper. ceremony law says threshold nights may earn it.
the ritual risk

the nightly loop is one screen and a handful of minutes, and the stone only compresses it: calls end matches in two boards, declines skip boards entirely. the real risk is guilt. a declined call must read as the road moving on, never as retreat: "you let this board stand." nothing lost, no streak language, no second ask.

paper protocol
components. the 28 at L1 values plus the five planets from the base set, nine station slips, one stone marker with four phases. one person plays her printed sheet honestly: greedy first round, best reply after, call and answer by the rules above.
ten matches minimum, two players who already know the road. record: calls per match · decline rate on each side · match lengths · after each match, one word for how the decline felt.
kill it if: the correct answer to her call is always the same (no judgment: retune the threshold, then kill) · declines read as guilt · calls per match fall under one in five by the last matches (novelty, not depth) · anyone at the table wants to stake more than the match. never.
open flags

threshold-night waking · whether the stone arrives with the game or after your first claimed mansion · the name: the moonstone is a working name and must not collide with the 28's own moon language.
