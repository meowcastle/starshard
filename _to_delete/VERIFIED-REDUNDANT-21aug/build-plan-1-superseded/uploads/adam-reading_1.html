the combination

a cord, and a manger. your sun ties the ends of the road together and your moon feeds whatever is standing there.

the twenty-eighth is the last mansion, and the only one carrying two arabic names at once: baṭn al-ḥūt, the belly of the fish, and al-rishāʾ, the cord — the well-rope, the tie. the astronomers could not settle on one image, so both were kept. it runs right up to 0° aries, where the first mansion begins again, which makes you the knot that closes the wheel and the reason it can start over. you want to tie the ends together. you are the one who connects the two groups that should have met.

your moon landed at 0° cancer, on a patch of sky that is not really a star at all — praesepe, a soft smudge of hundreds of stars too faint to separate by eye. its older latin name is the manger, and the two dim stars flanking it were the donkeys feeding there. india calls this puṣya, one of the most auspicious stations on the whole road, and gives it an emblem you cannot misread: a cow's udder.

so the cord feeds. you are the person who connects everyone and then makes sure they have eaten — and the two are not separate acts for you, they are one gesture.

what that produces is a specific kind of usefulness. groups form around you not because you lead them but because you are the reason two halves of them ever met and the reason anyone stayed long enough to find out. you can make a home out of very little, and you would rather do that than be handed a finished one.

and both mansions name the same cost from different sides. the cord's: you belong to both sides and are counted by neither — you have been the connector in rooms where nobody thought to list you as a member. the manger's is blunter. feeders eat last and call it a personality. you are not actually built to run empty. you have just gotten very good at it.

your sun is in the stretch of sky that does not appear in the indian count. your moon is on a cluster the eye cannot resolve. and the horizon climbing when you were born was the twenty-fourth mansion, which china named emptiness outright.

three placements, three names for absence. and here is what turns it: in the old election books, all three of those are among the most generous stretches of the entire road.

the void
Saʿd al-Suʿūd, the luckiest of the lucky — “one of the most fortunate mansions; favourable for all good intentions.”

india's name for that last one is śatabhiṣā, the hundred healers, and its emblem is an empty circle. a void; the greatest luck there is; a hundred physicians standing around a ring with nothing in it. put them together and you get the sentence that runs under your whole chart:

an opening is not a wound.

you enter through emptiness and something in the room opens. people tell you things within the first conversation that they have not told anyone, and are sometimes embarrassed afterward. you are read as safe and slightly bottomless — and that is not a trick of manner, it is three mansions of room, arranged in a row.

the cost is the obvious one and you already know it. space that generous does not close on command. you will keep making room long after everyone else has stopped bringing anything to put in it, and because you can always make a home out of very little, you will tolerate arrangements someone with fewer skills would have left years ago.

the sky writes this once, at the minute you arrive, and never revises it.
what moves across it is another matter — and that is what tonight is for.
