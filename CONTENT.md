**22 August 2026.** Written after the rollout plan named "someone has to make the videos" as
its biggest unfilled risk, and you said you'd make them. That closes the hole, so this is the

## The thesis

**Four civilizations, with no contact, divided the same sky into 28 rooms. Sometimes they
gave a room the same name. Sometimes they looked at the same stars and saw opposite things.**

That is the channel. It is not "here's my game" and it is not horoscopes. It is real
astronomy and comparative mythology, and the game is what it turns into at the end.

Two things make it work that most creators do not have:

**The bank is already written.** 28 mansions × 4 traditions, structured, with determinant
stars, asterisms, deities, symbols, animals and agreement flags. Most channels die of
ideation. You have a year of it in a table, plus the 784-cell corpus behind it.

**The daily hook is real, not manufactured.** The moon *is* in a mansion tonight. That is a
genuine reason to post daily that no one can accuse you of inventing, and it is the same hook
the product runs on. Content and product are the same object, which is the only sustainable
version of this.

## Ten cold opens, drawn from your own table

Each is a 30-to-60-second short. No finished art required — the sky is the visual.

1. **"Two civilizations that never met named this star the same word."** Antares. Arabic
   *al-Qalb*, "the heart." Chinese 心 *Xīn*, "heart." Same star, same word, no contact.
2. **"One patch of sky. One culture called it the luckiest thing in the heavens. Another
   called it emptiness."** Mansion 24 — Arabic *Sad al-Suʿūd*, luck of lucks; Chinese 虛 *Xū*,
   the void; the Sanskrit symbol is an empty circle. Two of them agreed it was nothing.
3. **"There is one room in the sky with no bright star in it — and it points at the centre of
   the galaxy."** Mansion 21, *al-Balda*. The only mansion in the table whose determinant star
   field reads *starless*.
4. **"This star has been chasing the Pleiades for four thousand years and it is brighter than
   all of them."** Aldebaran, *al-Dabarān*, "the follower." Mansion 4.
5. **"Four cultures looked at the same fuzzy smudge and saw a nostril, a ghost, a cow's udder,
   and a lotus."** Praesepe, mansion 8 — Arabic *al-Nathra*, Chinese 鬼 *Guǐ*, Sanskrit *Puṣya*.
   All four correctly identified the same object and told four unrelated stories about it.
6. **"The Arabs saw one scorpion across four rooms. The Chinese saw a raccoon-dog, a rabbit, a
   fox and a tiger."** Mansions 16-19: claws, crown, heart, sting — against 氐 房 心 尾 and
   four different animals. Same four rooms, one animal or four.
7. **"There are 28 animals hiding in the Chinese sky and nobody put them in order for you."**
   Dog, rooster, crow, monkey, ape, goat, horse, deer, snake, earthworm, flood-dragon, dragon,
   rabbit, fox, tiger, leopard, ox, bat, rat, swallow, pig, wolf — and three you have never
   heard of: *bian*, *xiezhi*, *yayu*. A series inside the series.
8. **"One of the 28 has no Sanskrit name. The cord that ties the wheel closed, and one
   tradition just didn't."** Mansion 28, *Batn al-Ḥūt / al-Rishāʾ*.
9. **"I couldn't put this one on a card."** Mansion 22 is literally *Luck of the Slaughterer*.
   A video about translation, taste, and what you do when the real name is unusable — this is
   the kind of honesty that builds trust with a skeptical audience.
10. **"Tonight the moon is in [mansion]. Here's what four skies called it."** The format. One
    a night, 28 nights, then it loops with the moon.

## Three veins, ranked

**1. The sky (lead with this).** Everything above. Platform: TikTok, Reels, Shorts. Audience:
astrology-curious plus the much larger "I like space facts" crowd, which is the one that
actually scales. This vein needs no finished art, no game footage and no finished product —
you could start it this week.

**2. The build (second, and nearly free).** Devlog. You are doing the work anyway, and indie
dev content converts to Steam wishlists more directly than anything else. Platform: YouTube
long-form, Bluesky/X, r/gamedev. **The sim work is unusually good material** — "I built a
robot to play my game 90,000 times and it found six openings that win every night" is a real
video, and the honesty of publishing the failures reads well to that audience.

**3. The game itself (last, and gated on art).** Board resolutions, combo chains, the flip
cascade. Genuinely satisfying to watch — but not until the art exists. Placeholder art in a
short is worse than no short.

**Do not run vein 1 and vein 2 on the same account.** They are different audiences with
different expectations. The sky account feeds the app; the devlog feeds Steam. One person can
run both, but not as one channel.

## Cadence, and the honest part

The failure mode is not a bad video, it is stopping. So set the commitment as a number before
you start, not an intention:

- **Vein 1: five a week for twelve weeks.** Sixty videos. That is the sample size at which you
  learn whether the hook works — not five, not ten. Short-form is a volume game and the first
  twenty are practice.
- **Vein 2: one devlog a fortnight.** Six over the same period.

**The metric that matters is not views, it is email signups per thousand views.** Views are
vanity here; the list is the asset every later phase consumes. Instrument that from video one.

**The gate:** if twelve weeks and sixty videos cannot produce an email list on a path to
800-1,500, the Kickstarter stays parked and the app carries the business alone. That is a real
outcome and it should be written down now, while it costs nothing to be honest about.

## What this changes upstream

The rollout plan had the Kickstarter's audience precondition — 800-1,500 warmed emails and
~40 named day-one backers — as its riskiest assumption. **Sixty sky videos is a credible path
to that number, and nothing else on the list was.** So:

- Vein 1 starts **now**, in parallel with Phase 0. It is the only item that needs no
  engineering and no money.
- Email capture in the free flow moves from "nice" to **blocking** — it is the collection
  point for everything the videos generate.
- The Kickstarter timeline stays at 4-6 months, but for the first time there is a mechanism
  behind it rather than a hope.
