## Batch A — 16·01 … 16·04

- **16·01** *work / a trade.* Both on the tools and the one who prices the
  work. The Gate puts all the speed at the front, so every customer meets the
  fastest version and then waits a season.
- **16·02** *THE OPPOSITE / farlight.* Everything in the outward life comes in
  twos and gets carried both ways; one interior thing has no counterpart. Every
  competence you own is for running two at once, so none of it reaches the
  single held item.
- **16·03** *money.* Two ventures funded from one pot, each having rescued the
  other, filed on one return. From any distance it reads as one self-employed
  person doing fine.
- **16·04** *the body.* Two seasons of genuine hunger a year apart — building
  and stripping, both wanted, neither one discipline over the other — aimed at
  a single unrevised standard taken off one man at twenty-three.

## Batch B — 16·05 … 16·08

- **16·05** *friendship.* A nineteen-year friendship pursued in two directions
  at once: the man, and the trade he came out of. Both live, neither ever the
  pretext for the other. The flat month never arrives because the two never go
  cold together.
- **16·06** *a public room.* Three minutes at a meeting in front of ninety
  people: you defended him and you named what he did, both at full weight, both
  unrecallable. The first half was permanent before the second was out of your
  mouth. **The cell is the ninety-second gap between two irrevocable
  statements.**
- **16·07** *resumption.* Two trades qualified in and alternated for thirty
  years, each absence filed as a leave by the room you left. Neither room ever
  replaced you; neither ever counts the other's years.
- **16·08** *grief.* Six years of keeping him current and six years of building
  without him, run at the same weight in the same week, with no edge on either
  — so each half becomes the accusation the other half answers for.

## Batch C — 16·09 … 16·12

- **16·09** *strangers.* Two complete, opposed, permanent readings of a person
  taken in ninety seconds, neither provisional, neither revisable because no
  second contact arrives. The currency of first impressions is one word and you
  have two.
- **16·10** *inherited rank.* A place two opposed founders built; an occupant
  trained by both and carrying both mandates whole, in a seat that descends
  from one line only and speaks in one voice.
- **16·11** *public reputation.* The doubleness itself is what you are known
  for, at a size with no low setting, so both halves and the four-year lateness
  of each are always in view and always read as a positioning move.
- **16·12** *an absolute ending.* One of two full enterprises closed in a
  season with nothing left of it, the obligation to its people surviving
  undiminished, and the remaining half running at exactly the old rate —
  because the two directions were never the two jobs.

## Batch D — 16·13 … 16·16

- **16·13** *craft.* Every commission leaves the workshop twice, one invoiced
  and one not. The output is halved, every job is late, and the unbilled second
  piece is usually the better answer.
- **16·14** *a piece of work everyone can see.* Two authorised versions of the
  one famous thing, released the same year, neither marked definitive — so a
  decade of other people have settled on your behalf which one you meant, and
  no two settled alike.
- **16·15** *not being placed.* A twenty-year institutional split in which you
  are the only name on neither faction's list. Holding both readings out loud
  reads as membership of the other camp, and nothing in you contests the
  filing.
- **16·16** *THE DIAGONAL.* The fork doubled: no single-direction part
  anywhere. The most consulted person in the building with no decision to their
  name, and thirty years of being treated for a hesitation that is not there.

## Live banned-token traps — this row is unusually bad

**Read your packet's `banned` array before you draft.**

- **The word for a curved span on two pillars is banned in every row-16 cell.**
  It comes off the portrait, and the premise's central image is built on it.
  Write *two pillars*, *carrying in two directions*.
- **`fire` and `force` are banned in every row-16 cell.** Ordinary English. The
  tokenizer is exact, so `forced`, `forces`, `fired` pass and the bare forms
  fail — the failure is one letter away and looks fine.
- **The sun portrait opens on a sign name, and the leak gate catches sign
  names.** It also catches month names, including the bare modal **`may`**.
- Per-moon: `head` (01) — `ahead`/`heads` pass · `flame`, `knife` (03) ·
  `crow`, `chariot` (04) — but `crowd` is a separate token and is safe ·

  `young`, `shoot` (15).

## The row-19 boundary

Row 19 (The Root) is being written alongside this one. **The Root goes one way,
straight down, all the way, with no stopping signal — the cost is destruction.
The Claws goes two ways at once, laterally, both real — the cost is speed.**
Grep your own output for *down / under / beneath / dug / bottom / all the way /
taken apart / the real cause*. If your subject's talent is finding what is
underneath, you have walked into row 19.
