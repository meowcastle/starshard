## Batch A — 25·01 … 25·04

- **25·01** *work / a trade.* The lone out-of-hours man at an industrial site:
  you arrive at five and the night's failure is finished and out of sight
  before the shift walks in. What is concealed is the state of the plant, so
  the case for fixing it can never be put.
- **25·02** *the family you came from.* Eleven relatives assemble because you
  assemble them, under a version of the family you edited before it reached the
  table. Naming the thing takes the roof off everyone at once.
- **25·03** *money.* Six standing arrangements, each recipient certain they are
  the only one, agreed separately at different bad hours. You know the total;
  nobody else can be shown it.
- **25·04** *the body.* Your father's course watched start to finish, and you
  are on the same road at a fixed twenty-two-year distance. The daily
  management passes for habit; the diagnosis and the schedule are both under
  the cover.

## Batch B — 25·05 … 25·08

- **25·05** *friendship.* Eleven friendships recruited by a curiosity that was
  exhausted inside a season each. **The cover has no setting for withdrawal**,
  so the interest ended and the housing did not.
- **25·06** *a public room.* You took a man apart at a meeting in view of a
  hundred people, and the reason is a woman still under your cover — so the act
  is unrecallable and its cause is unproducible.
- **25·07** *resumption.* The thing went up again in a fortnight after nine
  years down, and the nine years were filed at both ends as an interval,
  because the bad year that actually took it down was never available for
  inspection.
- **25·08** *grief.* You built the arrangement around a dead man's household in
  the first week and have run it three years. Your own share of him is the
  thing under the cover, and grief's running order gives you no place to say so.

## Batch C — 25·09 … 25·12

- **25·09** *strangers.* Read at a distance both ways: strangers admitted or
  refused on a minute's read that never loosens, while every stranger's read of
  you comes off the cover and is equally unrevisable.
- **25·10** *inherited rank.* A seat you did not build, spent as shelter, held

## Batch D — 25·13 … 25·16

- **25·13** *craft.* A workshop four people earn their living in, built and
  rebuilt by your hands at seven in the morning. Nobody in the building can
  price it, because no account of it has ever been read aloud.
- **25·14** *a piece of work everyone can see.* The work goes out complete and

  One competent copy takes the roof off.
- **25·15** *not being placed.* The house can be found by anyone at midnight

  go, and there is no seam to attach a successor to.
- **25·16** *a fight.* A two-year dispute run out of sight of the fourteen
  people it concerns, both grips closed at full weight, and the document that
  would win it outright is the one that puts their names on a desk.

## Live banned-token traps

**Banned in all 28 cells:** **`little`** · **`front`** · **`legs`** ·
**`swallow`** · the word for the portable fabric shelter the mansion is named
after · `asterism` · `fire-pillar` · `one-footed` · transliterations.

`little` and `front` are the ones that catch people. Batch A had to rebuild
three drafted lines around *in front of* and the verb *swallow*. The ban is
exact-token — `fronts`, `legged`, `swallowed` pass — **so the failure is one
letter away and looks fine.**

Per-moon found so far: `head` (01) — very easy to hit in work prose ·
`yama`/`yoni`/`pheasant` (02) · `flame`/`knife` (03) — both the obvious
metaphors for that moon · `crow`/`chariot` (04) · `hidden`/`knowledge` (09) ·
`king`/`royal`/`including` (10) · `fortune`/`allotted`/`front` (11) ·
**`back`**/`contract`/`snake` (12) · `open`/`moving`/`sets` (13) — `open` kills
the obvious *open palm* reading of The Hand · `spica`/`artisan`/`divine` (14) ·
`wind`/`young`/`shoot` (15) · `fire`/`force`/`arch`/`claw` (16).

Sign names and month names leak, including the bare modal **`may`**. Note that
*"the same day of the month"* is safe — only month *names* are caught.

## The row-26 boundary

Row 26 (The Chamber) is being written in parallel. **The Hideaway's cover
hides, and stands on one thing — it comes down in one piece without warning.
The Chamber's enclosure hides nothing and will not fall, and precisely because
it holds, nobody in a room thinks about the wall.**

Grep your output for *wall / boundary / structural / corners / unremarked / it
holds*. **In every cell the reason nobody sees the support must be stated as
concealment, not as the support being unremarkable.** Batch B caught itself
writing *"nobody outside it can see that any of it does"* — that is 26's
territory — and rewrote to *"nobody outside it has been given anything to look
at."*
