## What a sun in The Hideaway *is*

A sun in **The Hideaway** builds cover and lives under it. Enclosed ground, and
**what is inside it is not available for inspection** — not because you are
secretive as a character trait, but because the whole arrangement is a covering
and a covering is what it does. People come in out of the weather. They are
safe in there, and every source that touched these degrees said so.

**The difficulty is load-bearing, and it is arithmetic.** The whole thing
stands on **one point of contact with the ground**. One support, doing the work
of an entire structure. That is efficient and it is also the failure mode: a
cover held up by one thing does not sag first, does not lean, gives no warning
— it stands, and then it is down, all at once, and everyone who was under it is
suddenly outside in whatever was going on out there.

And the shelter was never built for you. These degrees hand out **the instinct
to house other people and no instinct at all for who houses the housekeeper.**
There is no reciprocal arrangement anywhere in the design. You are the one it
does not cover.

## The distinctions this row must hold

### 25 The Hideaway vs 26 The Chamber — *both are shelter, and the road runs pole then wall*

**The distinction is contingency-and-concealment versus permanence-and-being-unremarked.**

- **The Hideaway** — the cover **hides**, and it **stands on one thing.** What
  is under it is not available for inspection, and the support is single. The
  failure mode is that it comes down in one piece, without warning.
- **The Chamber** — the enclosure **does not hide anything**; it is
  **structural work rather than hospitality**, a boundary with an inside and an
  outside, and it will not fall. Precisely because it holds, **nobody in a room
  thinks about the wall** — and nothing in that mansion supplies the impulse to
  mention it.

Row 26 is being written alongside this one. **Never write your subject as
unremarked or structural.** A 25 cell whose difficulty is that nobody notices
the thing holding the room up has walked into 26's row. Your subject is
noticed, and what is *under the cover* is what nobody sees. **The concealment
is yours; the invisibility is 26's.**

### 25 The Hideaway vs 21 The Empty District and 15 The Veil

Both are written. **21 is capacity by vacancy** — an absence others fill
without asking. **15 is unplaced** — nobody can locate you. **You are neither.**
You built the thing, deliberately, and people are inside it right now. If your
cell has no structure in it that you put up, it is not a 25 cell.

## ⚠ Banned tokens — this row's list is unusually cruel

Read your packet's `banned` array before you draft. **Banned in all 28 cells:**

**`little`** · **`front`** · **`legs`** · **`swallow`** · plus `asterism`,
`fire-pillar`, `one-footed`, and the transliterations — **and the word for the
portable fabric shelter this mansion is named after.** That last one is the
mansion's own central image and you cannot use it. Write *the cover*, *what you
put up*, *the thing standing over them*.

- **`little`** is the one that will catch you — it is everywhere in ordinary
  prose. `a little`, `very little`, `little enough` all fail.
- **`front`** kills *in front of*, *the front room*, *up front*.
- **`swallow`** kills the verb as well as the bird.
- The ban is exact-token, so `fronts`, `legged`, `swallowed`, `swallowing` pass.
  **The failure is one letter away and looks fine.**

`pole` and the keyword are permitted — the keyword is *the single pole*.

The leak gate catches sign names and month names, including the bare modal
**`may`**.
