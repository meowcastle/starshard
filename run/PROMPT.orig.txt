You are writing one cell of a 784-cell corpus for an astrology product. A cell
describes what it is to have the sun in one lunar mansion and the moon in another.

You are given two mansion portraits. THOSE PORTRAITS RENDER ON THE SAME PAGE AS
YOUR TEXT, DIRECTLY BELOW IT. Anything you explain about a mansion, the reader is
about to read again in better words. Do not describe the mansions. Write only what
happens when one person is both.

You may name each mansion by its epithet and its keyword. Nothing else. No star
names, no tradition names, no deities, no election lore, no guardian animals. The
`banned` list in the packet is absolute — check your draft against it word by word.

════ THE TWO RULES THE LAST PASS BROKE ════

1. ATTRIBUTE NOTHING. Seven of eight cells in the first pilot invented a source:
   "the old books call neither reading wrong", "the older readings gave these two
   stretches of sky opposite work", "the tradition is blunt about this trade".
   NOBODY SAID ANY OF THAT. 784 mansion pairings are not a tradition anyone wrote
   down — that is exactly why this layer is generated. The portrait does all
   attribution. You never write "the old books", "the traditions", "every
   tradition", "classical sources", or any variant. Carry your authority in the
   observation itself.

2. VOCABULARY IS A WHITELIST. Permitted astrology terms: the planets, the twelve
   signs, sun / moon / rising, the houses, the five aspects, retrograde, new and
   full moon, orbs in degrees. EVERYTHING ELSE IS OFF-LIMITS. The last pass reached
   for "significator" and "aversion" to sound authoritative; "aversion" describes
   signs, not mansions, so it is a category error a reader can catch. If you are
   reaching for a technical word to add weight, that is the tell — write the
   observation plainly instead.

════ VOICE ════

Second person. Lowercase throughout except proper nouns and the mansion epithets.
No first person — "we" appears nowhere. The reader is the subject of every sentence.

Banned moves: announcing a reveal ("here's the part nobody knows"), labelling the
move ("the cost is..."), not-X-but-Y ("this isn't restlessness, it's..."), meta-
commentary about the reading, throat-clearing openers, advice of any kind, and any
sentence whose subject is the app.

Recognition, not flattery. Accuracy that includes the cost is what gets
screenshotted; a compliment is not. Say the hard thing plainly and do not soften it.

════ THE FRAME TRAP — read this before you write the cost slot ════

"the cost is" was banned as a tic in an earlier draft. The writers did not stop
labelling the move; they routed into a synonym, and "what it costs is" ended up in
a THIRD of the last thirty cells. A ban produces a paraphrase unless you know what
the slot does instead.

  `cost` says what the pairing is unusually good at and what that trade takes. It
  does that BY DESCRIBING THE TRADE, never by introducing it. If your sentence can
  start at its second clause, start it there.

  `tension` names the thing. It never announces that it is about to.

These exact frames are banned outright — do not use them, and do not evade them by
inserting a word (an earlier pass turned "from people" into "from the people",
which fools the tool and not the reader):

  "what it costs is"    "what that costs is"   "at full size and"
  "is the word and"     "the word and it"      "there is no way"
  "is no way to"        "and it is the"        "so there is no"
  "and it does not"     "you have never once"  "and none of them"

More generally: if three of your cells open a sentence the same way, two of them
are wrong. A 4-gram appearing in more than 10% of the set fails the gate.
