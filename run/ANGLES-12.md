# Angles already taken in row 12 — The Turning

Sixteen cells written by batches A–D. **Read them, do not repeat them.**
An angle is a life-situation, not a phrase. Go somewhere these did not go.

Full text of all sixteen is in `/tmp/ss/run/cells/12-a.json` … `12-d.json`.

## Batch A — 12·01 … 12·04

- **12·01** *work / trade.* The serial first-arriver: four trades entered
  before there was a job to apply for, good only at the opening year, each
  trade shed whole — and a permanent debt to the people who let you through
  the door. Burned sub-angle: the interview that cannot be told the true
  reason for leaving.
- **12·02** *family of origin.* Left at nineteen in one season. Because there
  is no gradient, the guilty member and the innocent ones were shed at the
  same rate. The reason is held intact and undelivered, so the family runs on
  a story in which you got above them. The surviving tie is pure
  administrative duty — money, appointments, paperwork.
- **12·03** *money / debt.* A business and a standard of living lost whole in
  one season. From outside it is one figure; up close, six creditors on six
  sets of terms. The debts are the permanent item and the assets were the
  perishable one. Burned: ranking creditors by how far each trusted you.
- **12·04** *body / appetite.* Nineteen years dry, stopped absolutely in a
  week because moderation was the only unavailable option — appetite
  undiminished, taste at full resolution. The promise was made to a wife who
  left in the eighth year and is kept for a household that no longer exists.

## Batch B — 12·05 … 12·08

- **12·05** *friendship.* The hunger is for finding a person out and it
  completes in three evenings; the friendships then run a decade on upkeep
  and all end together when the life does. The one name that crosses is the
  one that is owed, never the one you would pick.
- **12·06** *a public room.* A season that closed months earlier gets
  delivered in the only size available, in front of everyone, and is filed by
  the room as temper. You keep attending that room, on time, forever, because
  the people in it are owed individually.
- **12·07** *the mirror of 07·12.* The mourning of a friendship broke rather
  than faded, closed out inside a fortnight — and the moon's interior refuses
  to file anything as gone, reaching toward rooms you cleared yourself. The
  reaching fails, and it fails against your own past work.
- **12·08** *grief.* The mourning broke rather than faded and was over in a
  season. What did not end is what the dead man was owed, paid out with no
  edges to nine living people who never knew there was a list.

## Batch C — 12·09 … 12·12

- **12·09** *the serial newcomer.* Four complete restarts; each room takes a
  season to form a view of you and you have the whole room by week two. No
  reading ever gets its corrections. Your most exact knowledge of people is of
  near-strangers in towns you will not enter again.
- **12·10** *the inherited seat.* A molt needs something that grew on you; the
  position did not grow, so every ending takes only what carries your own name
  and the institution registers none of it. The worthiness question sharpens
  each pass, because the one thing still upright is the one you did not build.
- **12·11** *permanent host of a dead interest.* The thing you are known for
  finished absolutely, years ago. The display has one setting and no smaller
  version, so the ending cannot register outward — and what the finished thing
  left behind is a crowd that is still owed.
- **12·12** *THE DIAGONAL — the missing middle register.* Both halves doubled:
  no gradient in the ending, no expiry on the tie, leaving no third value
  anywhere. Everyone is at full weight or not present, and nothing in you does
  the sorting — the season does.

## Batch D — 12·13 … 12·16

- **12·13** *craft.* Three trades ended outright at their peak; nothing made
  was ever kept, so the only surviving body of work sits in other people's
  houses — and you still drive out to maintain it. The trade is finished; the
  guarantee is not.
- **12·14** *possessions.* Undefended ownership plus total departure: two
  households kept whole contents rather than halves, uncontested, and the one
  thing that crosses is a standing order on an object in a house you cannot
  enter. Read by everyone as being fleeced.
- **12·15** *unvouchability.* You were never in any group to be ended out of,
  so four lives leave no public record, and the only people who can testify to
  your continuity are the beneficiaries of your obligations — whose evidence
  has exactly one item in it.
- **12·16** *conflict.* Left one side of a decade-long fight in a single week,
  entirely. The grip was never on one side, so both cases still run at full
  weight with no table left to run them at, and the call you still take from a
  man on the other side is misfiled by both camps.

## Live banned-token traps — row 12 is unusually bad for these

The packet `banned` array is a union of both mansions' portrait vocabulary,
and several entries are ordinary English. **Read your own packet's `banned`
array before you draft, not after.** Known row-wide:

- **`back` is banned in every row-12 cell** (it comes from the sun side). This
  kills "comes back", "looks back", "years back", "hold back", "one length
  back". Use *behind*, *the distance*, *the reaching*.
- **`contract` is banned in every row-12 cell** — even though the premise is
  built on "the contract survives the molt". Use *promise*, *obligation*,
  *the tie*, *what is owed*.
- Also banned row-wide from the sun side: `kinship`, `legs`, `snake`,
  `phālgunī`, `denebola`, `al-ṣarfa`, `aryaman`.
- **The leak regex matches the bare word `may` as a month name.** No modal
  "may" anywhere in a cell.
