# Corpus batch 3 — stations 8–14

*Star Shard reading corpus · August 12, 2026 · The Ghost through The
Jewel — the Lion sequence and the crossing into the Azure Dragon. Same
four slots, same axis rotation, same laws as batch 2.*

**Note on this stretch:** stations 9–12 walk the length of one animal —
the lion's eye, forehead, mane, and tail — because that is genuinely how
the Arabic sky-map was built. Consecutive readings should feel like
moving along a single body, and stations 11 and 12 are a matched pair by
construction (the two ends of one bed in the Indian scheme). Written to
be read together.

---

## STATION.08 — The Ghost · 鬼宿

*0°00′–12°51′ Cancer · Praesepe (M44, the Beehive) with γ/δ Cancri ·
Arabic al-Nathra, "the nostril" · Puṣya, Bṛhaspati's sky of nourishment ·
guardian: the goat · kept by Venus · Vermilion Bird*

**STATION.08.strike**
you landed at **0° cancer** — a hinge of the whole year — on a patch of
sky that is not really a star at all. it's **praesepe**, a soft smudge of
hundreds of stars too faint to separate by eye; today we call it the
beehive. its older latin name is **the manger**, and the two dim stars
flanking it were the two donkeys feeding there. china saw something
ghostlier in the haze and named the station for spirits; the arabs called
it the lion's nostril — the breath, the soft opening.
india calls this sky **puṣya**, one of the most auspicious stations on
the whole road, and gives it an emblem you can't misread: **a cow's
udder**. so every tradition arrived at the same idea by a different door.
this is the sky of **feeding**. your hunger is to nourish the thing that
will outlast you — a person, a practice, a household, a body of work.
feeders eat last and call it a personality. you are not
actually built to run empty; you have just gotten very good at it.

**STATION.08.root**
this sky's guardian is **the goat** — the animal that thrives on the
rocky ground nothing else wants, and gets on the roof — and it is
**venus-kept**. that's an inner life of stubborn, affectionate
sufficiency: you can make a home out of very little and you would rather
do that than be given a finished one.
the room reads you as **soft**, and files you accordingly. it takes
people an embarrassingly long time to notice the goat: that you are the
one who has quietly been feeding this whole arrangement, that you have a
spine like a wall, and that the softness was never the same thing as
compliance. a ghost-rooted moon is misjudged early and relied on late.

**STATION.08.facing**
you enter gently and the temperature of the room comes up a degree.
people find themselves telling you things they hadn't planned to. the
hazy little cloud is doing its work: nobody can quite point to what you
did, only that it's easier here now.

**STATION.08.answer**
across the wheel, **the ghost** answers you — the manger, the soft cloud
of hundreds of small lights. once a year the full moon rises there and
asks what has been feeding you, and whether you have been letting it.

## STATION.09 — The Glance · 柳宿

*12°51′–25°43′ Cancer · κ Cancri and λ Leonis · Arabic al-Ṭarf, "the
glance" · Āśleṣā, the serpents' embrace · guardian: the river deer ·
kept by Saturn · Vermilion Bird*

**STATION.09.strike**
your sun landed on **the lion's eye** — that is what *al-ṭarf* means: the
glance, the look itself. china put a **willow** here, the tree that bends
and trails and never quite lets go of the water. and india named this sky
**āśleṣā**, which means *the embrace* — the coiled serpent, the sky of
the nāgas, who in that tradition keep the knowledge that isn't said out
loud.
the eye, the willow, the coil. what all three are describing is your
hunger: **you want to know the thing nobody announced.** you read rooms
at a resolution most people don't have — the pause before the answer, the
name that stopped getting mentioned, who stopped being cc'd.
the third image is the honest one. the coil holds.
what you notice, you keep hold of; a glance can become a grip. the
question that follows you is not whether you see clearly. it's when
seeing turns into not letting go.

**STATION.09.root**
this sky is guarded by **the river deer** — the small, secretive one that
lives at the water's edge and is gone before you've finished registering
it — and it is **saturn-kept**, the slow, weight-bearing planet. that's
an inner life that watches from cover and stays a long time: you don't
startle, you withdraw, and you take the whole record with you.
the room reads you as **private**, which is a nicer word for what it
usually means: unreadable. people feel *seen* by you and don't feel they
see you back, and some of them find that thrilling and some find it
lonely. a glance-rooted moon usually knows exactly how the imbalance
happened and could correct it at any time — and putting that off is the
actual choice, made daily.

**STATION.09.facing**
you enter quietly and start reading immediately. people notice being
noticed — one look from you and they feel clocked, in a way that is
either the beginning of trust or the end of the conversation.

**STATION.09.answer**
across the wheel, **the glance** answers you — the lion's eye and the
serpent's coil. once a year the full moon lands there and asks what
you've been holding onto simply because you were the one who saw it.

## STATION.10 — The Throne · 星宿

*25°43′ Cancer–8°34′ Leo · the Lion's brow — ζ/γ/η/α Leonis, including
Regulus · Arabic al-Jabha, "the forehead" · Maghā, the ancestors' seat ·
guardian: the horse · kept by the Sun · Vermilion Bird*

**STATION.10.strike**
your sun landed on **regulus** — the little king. it is one of the four
stars the ancient world called royal, it sits almost exactly on the
ecliptic so the moon and planets keep passing over it like a procession,
and the arabs read this group as the **forehead** of a lion so large it
took up a quarter of the sky.
india's name for the same stars is **maghā**, and here the tradition
turns the reading in a direction people don't expect: its deity is not a
king but **the ancestors** — the fathers, the dead who are owed
offerings. the seat is real, and **you did not carve it.** that is the
hunger of this sky: you want to be *worthy of* the chair, not merely in
it.
the cost is legitimacy, and it will follow you: a person who inherits
authority spends a long time proving it wasn't only inherited — often to
a room that already stopped asking, and occasionally to nobody but
themselves.

**STATION.10.root**
this sky is guarded by **the horse** and kept by **the sun** — the animal
that carries kings and the light everything else is measured against.
your inner life has a bearing to it. even at rest there's a carriage, a
straightened spine, an instinct not to be seen flinching.
the room reads you as **regal**, and that is a lonelier report than it
sounds. people bring you their best behavior, save their mess for
somebody else, and assume you're fine — because you look fine, because
you have always looked fine. a throne-rooted moon has to *ask* to be
taken care of, in plain words, and asking is the one thing the whole
configuration is built to avoid.

**STATION.10.facing**
you enter and the room reorganizes slightly around you, and you didn't do
anything. it is an unfair advantage and an unfair burden: people decide
you're the one in charge before you've spoken, then hold you to it.

**STATION.10.answer**
across the wheel, **the throne** answers you — the little king, and the
ancestors who set the chair there. once a year the full moon rises on it
and asks what you'd do with the authority if you stopped auditioning
for it.

## STATION.11 — The Mane · 張宿

*8°34′–21°26′ Leo · δ/θ Leonis (Zosma) · Arabic al-Zubra, "the mane" ·
Pūrva Phālgunī, the front of the bed · guardian: the stag · kept by the
Moon · Vermilion Bird*

**STATION.11.strike**
your sun landed on **the lion's mane** — *al-zubra*, the ruff, the
volume, the part of the animal that arrives before the animal does.
india's name for this sky is **pūrva phālgunī**, and its emblem is the
**front legs of a bed**: rest, pleasure, the good half of the night. its
deity is bhaga, who hands out the share of delight a life is allotted.
the old station-lists gave this sky the **release of captives**. mane
energy here is not vanity, it's *liberation*. your hunger is **to be
delighted in, and to hand delight
around**, and you have a talent for making other people feel unfastened.
the cost arrives when the delight stops coming back. this sky needs to be
enjoyed out loud by somebody, and it will keep performing to get there —
the difference between being adored and being appreciated is a
distinction you'll have to learn on purpose, usually the hard way, once.

**STATION.11.root**
this sky is guarded by **the stag** and kept by **the moon**: the animal
that carries its display on its head and sheds the whole thing every
year, under the most changeable light in the sky. your inner life grows
its crown, wears it, drops it, grows another. what moved you at twenty
embarrasses you at thirty and you will do it again.
the room reads you as **warm** — genuinely, immediately, correctly. what
it misses is how carefully you're reading it back: you know exactly who
is enjoying themselves and who is enduring the evening, and you'll adjust
the room to fix it without being asked. a mane-rooted moon does emotional
labor at a professional level and calls it having a good time.

**STATION.11.facing**
you enter warmly and loudly and the party starts. people are pleased to
see you before they've decided anything about you, which means you almost
never get a neutral first impression — and almost never get a quiet one.

**STATION.11.answer**
across the wheel, **the mane** answers you — the ruff, and the sky the
old books gave to letting captives go. once a year the full moon lands
there and asks who you'd release, including possibly yourself.

## STATION.12 — The Turning · 翼宿

*21°26′ Leo–4°17′ Virgo · Denebola, the lion's tail · Arabic al-Ṣarfa,
"the turning of the heat" · Uttara Phālgunī, the foot of the bed ·
guardian: the snake · kept by Mars · Vermilion Bird*

**STATION.12.strike**
your sun landed on **denebola**, the star at the end of the lion's tail —
and the arabs named this station for what happens when it rises:
*al-ṣarfa*, **the turning**, the point where the summer heat finally
breaks. the whole station is named after a change in the weather.
india pairs it deliberately with the sky before it: if the last station
was the front of the bed, this is the **foot of it**, and where that one
had bhaga handing out delight, this one has **aryaman** — the god of
kinship, alliance, and the promise that has to hold after the party.
that's your hunger. **you want to be the one who keeps it going once the
pleasant part is over.** you are the hinge in every group: the one
present when the mood turns, the plan changes, the season ends.
hinges take the strain from both sides. being the person
who manages the turn means you are rarely allowed to simply *have* the
feeling everyone else is having.

**STATION.12.root**
this sky is guarded by **the snake** and kept by **mars** — the animal
that grows by shedding, and the planet with no patience. your inner life
molts, on a schedule that is nobody's business but yours, and it does it
suddenly. you can be entirely finished with something you loved, and the
finish will look abrupt to everyone who wasn't watching the weeks before.
the room reads you as **decisive**, sometimes as cold. what it doesn't
see is how long you carried it before you moved. a turning-rooted moon
does its grieving in advance and its announcing at the end, which is
efficient, and which is why people say *you never said anything* — and
you didn't, and you were saying it for months.

**STATION.12.facing**
you enter and something shifts. people can't always name it; the
conversation changes direction shortly after you sit down. you are read
as someone who ends things, which is only half of it — you also start
what comes after.

**STATION.12.answer**
across the wheel, **the turning** answers you — the tail of the lion,
where the heat breaks. once a year the full moon rises there and asks
what season you've been refusing to admit has ended.

## STATION.13 — The Hand · 軫宿

*4°17′–17°09′ Virgo · β/η/γ/δ/ε Virginis · Arabic al-ʿAwwā, "the barker"
· Hasta, the open hand · guardian: the earthworm · kept by Mercury ·
Vermilion Bird · **the traditions diverge here***

**STATION.13.strike**
your sun landed on a **bend of five stars in virgo** — and this is the
other place on the road where the sky-maps flatly disagree. the arabs
called it *al-ʿAwwā*, **the barker**: dogs sounding in the dark, the
noise that tells you something is out there. india looked at the same
five and saw an **open hand** — *hasta*, whose deity is savitṛ, the one
who sets things in motion. a warning bark and a working hand. we didn't
reconcile them, because they weren't reconciled.
the hunger belongs to the hand: **you want the whole thing to pass
through your fingers.** the drawing, the repair, the meal, the sentence
that steadied someone — you don't fully believe in a thing until you have
made it or fixed it yourself.
the cost is the bark. you are the one who says the thing is not ready,
out loud, while everyone else is celebrating — and being right about that
repeatedly is a very effective way to be left off invitations.

**STATION.13.root**
this sky's guardian is the strangest on the whole road: **the earthworm**
— the animal that makes soil by passing the world through its own body,
and without which nothing anyone eats would grow. it is **mercury-kept**,
so the labor runs through language and detail as much as through hands.
this is an inner life of unglamorous, load-bearing work.
the room reads you as **useful**, and that word is doing a lot of quiet
damage. people bring you tasks, not confidences; they thank the outcome,
not you. a hand-rooted moon can go years being indispensable and
un-asked-about. the worm doesn't need the credit, but it does need the
ground to be worth the effort — and you get
to choose whose ground.

**STATION.13.facing**
you enter already noticing what needs fixing, and usually fix it before
anyone's said hello. people experience you as capable within about
ninety seconds, and start handing you things shortly after.

**STATION.13.answer**
across the wheel, **the hand** answers you — the open palm the indians
saw where the arabs heard dogs barking. once a year the full moon rises
there and asks what you would make if nobody needed anything repaired.

## STATION.14 — The Jewel · 角宿

*17°09′ Virgo–0°00′ Libra · Spica · Arabic al-Simāk al-Aʿzal, "the
unarmed high one" · Citrā, the artisan's bright stone · guardian: the
flood-dragon · kept by Jupiter · **first station of the Azure Dragon***

**STATION.14.strike**
your sun landed on **spica** — the brightest star in virgo, a single
clean white light with nothing near it. the arabic name is the one to
keep: *al-simāk al-aʿzal*, **the unarmed high one**. there are two tall
bright stars in that part of the sky, and the old astronomers
distinguished them by what they carried: the other one, arcturus, is
*al-simāk al-rāmiḥ* — the high one **with a lance**. you got the one who
came up that high carrying nothing.
india names the same star **citrā**, the brilliant one, and gives it to
tvaṣṭṛ, the divine craftsman who shapes every form. so the hunger here is
to **make the one beautiful thing that doesn't need defending.** you'd
rather build the argument than win it.
the cost is right there in the name. unarmed is a choice, and it is not
always returned. you will meet people who read the absence of a weapon as
an invitation, and you will have to decide — repeatedly, and without
becoming someone else — how to hold the ground anyway.

**STATION.14.root**
this sky begins a new banner: the white tiger's country ends, and your
moon took root in the first station of the **azure dragon**, the sky of
spring and beginnings. its guardian is the **flood-dragon** — the
water-serpent that lives in the deep pool and, in the old stories, is
what a dragon is before it has learned to fly. and it is
**jupiter-kept**: the largest and most generous of the seven.
so your inner life is coiled potential in deep water, and it knows it.
the room reads you as **impressive and unfinished** — talented, obviously
so, and somehow still arriving. that reading is accurate and it has an
expiry date nobody will announce. a jewel-rooted moon spends its twenties
being called promising and has to decide, at some point, to simply be the
thing instead. the pool is not the problem. staying in it is.

**STATION.14.facing**
you enter clean and unarmed and people notice the absence of edge before
they notice anything else. it reads as poise. it is also, in certain
rooms, read as an opening — and you find out early which room you're in.

**STATION.14.answer**
across the wheel, **the jewel** answers you — the tall bright star that
carries no weapon. once a year the full moon lands there and asks what
you would build if you never had to defend it afterward.

## Production notes

**~3,100 words; stations 8–14 complete.** Fourteen of twenty-eight done —
the road is half written. Batch 4 picks up at station 15, The Veil.

**The lion sequence works.** Stations 9–12 read as a walk down one body
(eye → forehead → mane → tail) because that's how the Arabic map was
actually built, and 11/12 are a true matched pair in the Indian scheme —
the front and foot of the same bed, delight and the promise that outlasts
it. Anyone whose sun and moon land in that pair gets a reading that
writes itself.

**Best material in this batch:** Regulus paired with Maghā's *ancestors*
turns the throne from a power fantasy into an inheritance question
("the seat is real, and you did not carve it"); Spica's al-Simāk
al-Aʿzal, *the unarmed high one*, is genuinely distinguished in the
Arabic from Arcturus, *the one with a lance* — a real astronomical
naming convention that hands us a whole character; and Praesepe as
**the manger** flanked by two donkey-stars converges with Puṣya's
cow's-udder emblem on the same idea from opposite ends of the world.

**Divergence handled honestly again:** station 13 is `DIVERGENT` in the
table — the Arabs heard dogs barking where India saw an open hand — and
the reading says so rather than picking a winner. That is now the house
pattern for the flagged stations (6 and 13 so far).

**The banner change lands at 14**, the first station of the Azure Dragon,
and the root section uses it: the flood-dragon is what a dragon is before
it flies. Later batches should keep marking banner changes this way — at
21 (Black Tortoise) and 28 (back to the White Tiger).

**VERIFY PASS — August 12, 2026.** Confirmed: Praesepe was "the manger"
