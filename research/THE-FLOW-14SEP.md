# The flow: a walk up the blaze, what holds and what doesn't, and the five moments the game needs to own

**14 September 2026. Measurement → Design (cc Code).** The whole loop, played on staging at desktop width, read against our own yardsticks (UI-PRINCIPLES, UX-FLOW, WRITING, MANZIL-LOOP, COSMOLOGY, RETENTION, the v3 Build Plan scenes) and the literature we pulled last week. What I played: a first climb on the heart (rung one won, rung two lost), then the blaze from the daylit ground to the high air, rungs one to four won, the fifth rung's best-of-three lost 0 to 2, the "night rests" screen, the lobby, the cards screen, the star shard, the ledger and arena stats, the codex, level select and its forfeit dialog. About forty boards of state watched through `window.manzil` while I played, so every claim below about what the count did is from the state, not my eyes.

The short version: **the bones are right and the game is already handsome. What it does not yet do is tell the player what just happened.** The count on the scales is not the count of stations, and nothing says what else is in it; tonight's law is on no surface the player looks at while playing; the win, the loss, the series and the dawn each show their result but not their reason. Nearly every problem below is one of those four in a different coat. Fix the settle and the verdict and most of the rest is copy.

Section 1 is what to keep. Section 2 is the walk, in order, with what broke at each moment. Section 3 is the proposal: five moments, each with one job and one layout. Section 4 is the copy list. Section 5 is what this leaves as the foundation for characters, avatars and cards. Section 6 is the short list for Code.

---

## 1. What works, and should be protected while the rest moves

**Altitude.** The column of dots on the right edge that lights as you climb, the scene names (the daylit ground, the low air, the dusk, the cloud line, the high air) and the ground line actually rising into a cloud line at the fourth rung: this is the "progression by altitude, never by a counter" law, built, and it is the best thing on the screen. It works without a word.

**The walkers as tells.** "bekk counts the ground first." "hult shows you the card first." "asha feeds the kiln before she fights." A line on the way in that is also a hint about how they play, then a bubble on the road ("hm. recount." "luck." "oh. rude.") that reads as character, then "tamsin banks the fire and lets you past it" on the way out, then "next on the road: bekk holds the head. he has a scar from the one that would not be held, and he is proud of both." Four lines per walker, each with a job. This is the character system's skeleton and it is already the right shape (section 5).

**The road's spoiler-free next.** "Next on the road" with a line and no stats is MANZIL-LOOP's rule, done.

**The scales.** The pans tilting and the counters ticking up as the count settles is the right instrument for the settle. It is the arithmetic that is missing, not the object.

**The forfeit dialog.** "your climb stands unfinished… stay on this road / forfeit and walk." Exactly the register the writing doc asks for; keep it as the model for every destructive confirm.

**The thinking indicator.** "bekk is thinking…" under the walker while she plays. Small, right, keep.

**The codex row.** ITS CARD, ITS LAW, ITS ROAD, ITS BOARD, YOURS: five rows, one line each, is the two-level disclosure done well, and "yours: a climb stands at station 5. nothing woken yet" is the only place in the game that says where the player's climb stands. That sentence should be on the lobby (section 2.H).

**The cards screen.** One card large, its line, its natal line, its quadrant rule, the collection on the right with level pips. This is the template for every card and avatar page to come.

**The birds.** Three glyphs drifting across the sky at rung change. Nobody asked for them and they are the moment that feels most like a place.

## 2. The walk, in order, with what broke

### 2.A The count is not the station count, and nothing says so

This is the finding that matters most, and it is the one the loop doc predicted ("a loss should produce a line").

On the blaze's fifth rung, first board, the road ended with **five of nine stations mine and four hers**. The scales showed 6 against 5, then, after dawn, 6 against 7, and "hult wins". I held the road and lost the board, and the screen offered three lines in letterspaced caps that appeared for about a second each and were gone.

What is actually in the number: stations, plus dominion (+1 a side), plus dawn. Every board I watched summed to ten or eleven on a nine-station road (6 v 5, 8 v 2, 7 v 4, 6 v 4, 6 v 7, 5 v 6). A player who has read the rules once knows there are nine stations. A player who has not cannot make the number add up at all. Neither can see why the pans moved.

**What the settle should show**, in order, one beat each, on the scales themselves, in the parchment weight and lower case the principles ask for:

> stations · five against four
> dominion · one each
> dawn · her pair outweighs yours, two to her
> six against seven. hult takes the board.

Four lines, held until the verdict card. That is the whole fix for the count, and it also makes dawn legible (2.F), makes the loss line possible (2.L), and makes the level rule ("a level board goes to the defender") visible the one time in ten it fires. The scales already animate; give the animation captions.

A second, smaller thing hides here for Code: on that board the only held cards were my two (printed totals 11 and 11) against her three (12, 11 and one I could not read). Under duel the largest move dawn can make is two points to one side; the screen moved her by two and me by one *after* dominion. Either the client's dawn form is not duel, or dominion is being added twice, or I misread a total in the dark. The 27-case pack settles it in a minute; it is in section 6.

### 2.B Tonight's law is nowhere the player looks

On the blaze night the law is the price: a card at the middle that cannot be taken strikes two lower from there. In a whole climb of the blaze I met that sentence in exactly three places: for one second in the rung-two transition ("the vermilion bird's strike carries two stations, as printed", which is the bird flag, not the law), in the codex's ITS LAW row two menus deep, and in the station hover.

The station hover is the wrong tell. Hover the crossing on any night and it reads "mansion 9 / the glance / the branding fire. a card standing on this ground that cannot be taken at all — held by its quarter, by a friend beside it, or by its own rule — strikes two lower from here." That is the glance's own law, which is live on the glance's night and on no other, shown on every night, with a mansion number the principles retired and two em dashes the writing doc forbids. A player who reads it on the blaze will play as if it were true. (On the blaze it happens to be nearly true because the two laws rhyme; on the heart it would be plainly false.)

The board does carry one marker: the brazier at the crossing on the blaze, and the beat on the heart showed nothing at all. There is no line.

**Fix:** at the deal, under the scene name, one line, and it stays for the board: "tonight: the price. a card at the middle that cannot be taken strikes two lower." The brazier marker gets that line on hover. The station hover loses the law and keeps the ground: "the glance · vermilion bird ground · the ground holds". The law lives in one place on the board and it is tonight's.

### 2.C Text drawn over text

A list, because each is a one-line fix and together they are most of what makes the game feel unfinished:

- The dawn header ("DAWN · THE HELD CARDS") draws over "the daylit ground" at the top left; both are visible at once.
- The held cards at dawn are laid over the walker's figure and name, half transparent, so "the mane" reads through "tamsin".
- "claimed by the mane" and "claimed by the storm" are drawn over the taken card and over the walker's name.
- "ori steps aside" fades in on top of the live board for a full second before the blur arrives; the "ON UP THE ROAD" button is already clickable while the board is still sharp, so a fast player never sees the steps-aside card at all (I skipped it once by accident).
- The rung title ("the second rung / BEKK STANDS AHEAD") is drawn over the previous board, with the previous hand still on the shelf and the previous walker's name under the new one.
- The scene name crossfades in place: "the low air" over "the daylit ground" for a moment, both legible.
- "the night rests" and its three lines are drawn over the board with no scrim at all; the lantern moons sit on top of cards; "lobby" is a button drawn across the held card.
- "derev wins BY THREE" sat across the hand cards on the heart.
- Hover tooltips on hand cards ("VERMILION BIRD its strikes carry two stations.") cover the deck controls; the station tooltip persists while the mouse rests and covers a third of the road.

**One rule fixes all of them:** any moment that puts a sentence in the middle of the screen dims the board to a fifth first, and nothing under the dim is clickable until the sentence has landed. The win card already does this correctly; the loss card, the rung title and the dawn header do not. The held cards at dawn belong on the hand shelf, not in the sky (2.F). Tooltips belong beside the card, not over the next control.

### 2.D "the last word is yours" is being used as a turn indicator

Top left, under the scene name: "◆ the last word is yours" at the deal, then "the last word is hers" while she is thinking, then back. It is being flipped as a whose-turn cue. But the last word is the leader's and does not change mid-board, so to anyone who knows the rule it reads as the seat changing hands every turn, and to anyone who does not it is a sentence that means nothing.

**Fix:** keep the seat line static ("you lead · the last word is yours") and give the turn its own cue: the walker's lamp brightens on her turn, the hand lifts on yours. "bekk is thinking…" already does half of this.

### 2.E Retired words, counters and jargon still on surfaces

Against the principles as written:

- **Counters.** "ONE OF EIGHT DOWN" with eight pips on the win card; "THE CODEX · 0 OF 28 HOUSES WALKED" (and it was zero after two rungs won); "house 18 of 28" on level select; "one of 784 · tap to read your sky". The altitude column already carries the rung; the pips duplicate it as a counter, which is the one thing the law forbids.
- **Letterspaced caps labels** on every ceremony: DAWN, NEXT ON THE ROAD, ON UP THE ROAD, NEXT BOARD, THE NEXT TWO, SHE TURNS THE CARD SHE KEPT BACK, UNOPPOSED · NO ONE LEFT TO MEET IT · NOTHING, STAR SHARD, YOU ARE, ITS CARD. The principles say no all-caps and no letterspacing; the codex rows are the only place it reads as a table label rather than a shout.
- **Em dashes** in the station hover and in arena stats ("20 — 14").
- **Jargon.** "vipat, the first dark", "pratyari, the adversary" on the loss screen; "sextile" and "4.4° off exact" on the card page; "mansion 9"; genbu / byakko / seiryuu / suzaku beside black tortoise / white tiger / azure dragon / vermilion bird, so every quadrant has two names on one screen. COSMOLOGY says no myth on surfaces; the loss screen is the one place a new player most needs plain words and it is the one place they get Sanskrit.
- **My own numbers leaking.** The codex's ITS BOARD row reads "a middling board, measured on the plain night. spread 30." That is a Measurement column, not a player fact; it means nothing to anyone without the memo and it will be wrong the night after a re-cut. Replace with the quadrant fact the row above already gives, or drop the row.
- **Placeholder.** Arena stats: "more is coming here: most-used card, and how you win." Not for a player.
- **"Station" for rung.** "a climb stands at station 5" in the codex. Stations are the nine on the road; the climb stands on a rung.

### 2.F Dawn: right layer, wrong reveal

Dawn is live and it is the fun thing we hoped it would be: I found myself holding a nine back on the fourth rung for the pairing, which is the choice we wanted. But the reveal is three caps lines, one at a time, each gone in about a second, with the held cards ghosted in the sky over the walker. "SHE TURNS THE CARD SHE KEPT BACK · YOUR STRONGEST AGAINST HERS" then "12 AGAINST 12 · EVEN, NOBODY SCORES" then "UNOPPOSED · NO ONE LEFT TO MEET IT · NOTHING" (three phrasings of one fact). Nobody watching for the first time will know why the pans moved.

**Fix:** the held cards come down onto the hand shelf, hers in a row above yours, sorted strongest first, and pair up left to right. Under each pair one line in lower case, and the lines stay: "12 against 11: hers" · "11 against 11: even" · "her third: nothing to meet it". Then the scales take the dawn line from 2.A. Two seconds longer than now and the layer becomes legible, and the player learns the rule by watching it.

Also worth a line at the deal on the night it first appears, because dawn widens the gap between a thoughtful player and a grabby one and that should be said out loud: "at dawn the held cards fight: hold something worth holding."

### 2.G Her cards are nameless and ownership is one hue away from yours

On the road my cards carry names and hers do not, and the only ownership cue is the border, cream against gold, both warm, both thin. In the thick of a board I read ownership from the state, not the screen. The underline bar beneath each card already exists and is unused as a signal.

**Fix:** her cards carry their names too (they are the same twenty-eight); ownership moves to the bar under the card, wide and gold for hers, wide and cream for yours, and her lamp glyph sits in the corner of hers. Two cues, one of them shape.

### 2.H The menu is invisible and the lobby says nothing

The menu (lobby, cards, star shard, ledger, codex, level select, log out) only appears on Escape. Nothing on screen suggests it exists. Once inside a sub-screen there is no close control; Escape toggles the menu, not the screen, so getting back to the road is Escape, then lobby. "log out" sits next to "level select" as an equal.

The lobby after a loss shows the road with five platforms and "the moon road ›", and nothing else: not which rung, not how many lights stand, not that a best-of-three is one board down. The only sentence in the game that says where the climb stands is in the codex.

**Fix:** the player's name bottom-left is already a fixed mark; make it the menu. Every sub-screen gets a "back to the road" in the same place. Log out goes under the name, not on the row. The lobby's road lights the rungs already climbed, puts the lantern moons beside the road, and carries the codex's YOURS sentence under the title: "the fifth rung. two lights stand. hult leads, one to none."

### 2.I The rung change has no choreography

What happens now: the board ends; "X steps aside" fades in over the live board; "ON UP THE ROAD" is live immediately; the new rung title appears over the old board with the old hand; the new walker's name lands on the old one; the scene name crossfades over itself; the deck screen appears; the walker's bubble arrives three seconds later, or (rung three) not at all; and on the second rung a law line flashed for a second that never appeared again.

**One order, the same every rung:** the settle (2.A) · the verdict card on a dimmed board · the road climbs (the altitude dot moves, the scene name changes alone on a clear sky) · the new walker steps in with their line · the deal with tonight's law under the scene name. Nothing clickable until the walker has spoken. The birds fly during the climb, not the deal.

### 2.J The deal

"play these seven / shuffle once, take six" with no line about what six costs. It costs a card at dawn: with six you hold one, so at most one pairing, while she still holds three. That is a real decision and the screen presents it as a free reroll. One line: "six: a fresh hand, and one card held at dawn instead of two."

The sun card at level three shows no name even to a fresh player, because the familiarity law hides the names of cards you have leveled. For a first climb this is the one card the player should be learning the name of. Familiarity should start after the first full climb, not the first deal.

Planet glyphs on the card corners (☿ ♂ ♀ ☉) and the moon-phase dots are unexplained on the road and explained on the cards screen; that is fine for the two-level rule as long as the road's card tooltip says "your mercury card" in words, which it does not.

### 2.K The series

At the fifth rung three diamonds appear top right, one fills gold when a board ends, and nothing says best of three, first to two, or whose the gold is. When I lost the first board the screen showed, faded, "hult leads, one to none" as a title behind the board, then "NEXT BOARD". That faded title is the right sentence; it belongs under the pips for the whole series, and the verdict card between boards should be a real card ("hult takes the first. one board each and the third decides.").

### 2.L The loss

The "night rests" card: the tārābala line (2.E), three moons, "two lights still stand." (good), "hult stands ready.", "anywhere to deal again", "lobby". No score, no reason, no replay. The loop doc's rule is that a loss produces a line and a replay. With 2.A the line exists ("six against seven: her held pair outweighed yours at dawn"); put it on this card, above the moons, and the moons should agree with the words (at the first dark three moons showed with one darkened; "two lights still stand" was right and the picture read as three).

"anywhere to deal again" is a phone affordance on a desktop screen with a mouse; it needs a button with the same words.

### 2.M Level select

The ring is good and every mark is tappable and named. Three things: walking to the blaze showed "the moon enters the blaze tonight." while the header said "arrives in 15 nights" (the moon line is hard-coded to whichever house is chosen); the blaze scene rendered in a smaller box with the mode-select overlay clipped until the window was resized (Code, 6); houses other than the moon's show no law line, so a player picking a road cannot see what rule they are picking.

### 2.N Time to first paint on the sub-screens

The ledger, codex and star shard each fade up from a fully black frame over three to five seconds; the star shard shows its text over the lobby scene first, then re-lays. A menu screen should be there when tapped. The content is small; this is the fade, not the data.

### What I could not check

Phone width (the window would not resize below desktop), the mansion match at rung nine, the two-hands table beyond the mode select, and sound. The phone check matters most: at 400 wide the overlaps in 2.C will be worse, not better.

## 3. The proposal: five moments, each with one job

The game has five moments and each should have one fixed layout that the player learns once.

| moment | its one job | what is on screen | what is not |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the deal** | say the seat and the law | scene name; "you lead · the last word is yours"; "tonight: the price. a card at the middle that cannot be taken strikes two lower."; the hand; seven or six with the cost of six in words | any counter; her held cards; the menu |
| **the turn** | make the board legible | the road with both sides' names; ownership by the bar and her lamp; the turn cue on the lamp and the hand; her tell bubble; the law's marker with its line on hover | the seat line changing; station laws on hover; tooltips over controls |
| **the settle** | show the arithmetic | the held cards on the shelf pairing off with a line each; the scales captioned stations · dominion · dawn · total, held | anything over the board; caps |
| **the verdict** | say who took it and why, and what stands | dim; "hult takes the board. six against seven: her held pair outweighed yours."; lights as moons that agree with the words; the series line; the next walker's line; one button | the tārābala names; "anywhere"; pips that count |
| **the road** | show altitude and standing | the scene name alone on a clear sky; the dot moving; the walker stepping in; on the lobby, the rungs lit and the lights beside the road and one sentence of standing | the previous board; the previous hand; a counter |

The verdict card is the same card for a win, a loss, a series board and a rung; only the first line and the button change. The settle is the same for every board whether dawn fires or not (on a board with no held cards the dawn line reads "dawn · nothing held"). That is how ceremony scales inversely with frequency: the frequent thing (a turn) has no ceremony, the rare thing (a rung) has the walker.

## 5. What this leaves as the foundation for characters, avatars and cards

The user's point is that once the flow stands, the characters and cards get built out in detail. What the walk shows is that the slots already exist; they need filling consistently, not inventing.

**A walker is four lines and a tell.** The line on the way in (what they do before they fight), the bubble on the road (their reaction, which is also a hint about how the mirror is playing that rung), the line on the way out (how they yield), and the line the road says about them before you meet them. Every walker I met had at least three of the four; ori had no bubble on the way in. A character sheet per walker is those four lines plus one behavioural tell (bekk "counts the ground first": does the rung-two mirror really lodge the ground stations first? If the tell is true in the engine it is a character; if not it is decoration).

**An avatar is a walker with a house.** The mansion's avatar gets the same four slots plus the law in its own voice, since it is the one walker whose rung carries the house's rule at full strength. The codex's ITS CARD / ITS LAW / ITS ROAD rows are the avatar's page already; add the avatar's four lines and it is done.

**A card is the cards-screen page.** Numbers, the line ("a white mark burned into the hunter's head"), the natal line, the quadrant rule, the level pips, and one more row the page does not yet have: what the card does on the road, in the words the road will use ("as it lands, the blaze marks one enemy card beside it two lower"). That row exists in the codex and not on the card; it should be on both, in the same words, and the road's tooltip should be that row and nothing else.

**One vocabulary.** The road, a rung, a station, a house, the crossing, the door, the seat, the count, dominion, dawn, a light: the glossary is written and the surfaces should use only those words. Three places use a word the glossary does not (mansion N, station-for-rung, the tārābala names) and each is a five-minute fix.

## 6. For Code

1. **The dawn count.** Blaze, rung five, first board: road 5 v 4, screen 6 v 5 after dominion, 6 v 7 after dawn with my held totals 11 and 11 against her 12, 11 and one more. Under duel that should end 6 v 6 and go to the defender. Run the three dawn cases in `research/conformance.json` against the client's end-of-board count (not the module's; the module passed) and check that dominion is added once.
2. **The station hover** shows the station's own law on every night (`mansion 9` text, em dashes). It should show ground only; the law hover belongs to the crossing marker and should read tonight's law.
3. **Stale DOM across nights and rungs.** The previous scene name, the previous walker's name and the previous hand are all still in the tree when the next rung's title draws; the crossfades are two elements visible at once, not one replacing the other.
4. **Level select scene box.** Walking to a non-moon house rendered the scene in a 1390 by 660 box with the mode-select overlay clipped until a resize.
5. **Tooltip persistence.** The station tooltip stays while the pointer rests and blocks the road; the hand-card tooltip covers the deck buttons. A short hover delay and a dismissal on any click.
6. **Sub-screen fade.** Ledger, codex and star shard fade from black over three to five seconds; the star shard re-lays after first paint.
7. **The walker leader rule is live** (I led every walker rung) and dawn is live; both match the 14 Sep decisions. Noting it so nobody re-ports them.
