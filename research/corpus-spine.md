# Corpus batch 1 — the spine

*Star Shard reading corpus · August 12, 2026 · the chart-independent
half of the Full Reading. Written to `INSTRUMENT.md` (the ten jobs, the
Becoming), `ANCHORS.md` (shared sky leads), `SIGIL-READING.md` §7 (the
depth layer). **This is the voice bible**: the 28 station bodies in later
batches imitate this register, and the generative transit layer is
constrained by it.*

**Why the spine first:** every passage here fires for *every* chart, no
matter which stations a traveler landed in. Porting this alone lifts all
readings; the station bodies then deepen them.

## OPEN.address — the opening (pick 1, seeded)

**OPEN.address.1**
at the minute you arrived, the sky over {birthPlace} stood one way and
no other. the sun was somewhere in it. the moon was somewhere else. a
third stretch of sky was climbing over the eastern horizon. and one of
those three had nearly finished where it stood, and was already
*leaning into the next*. read it slowly. all of it is yours.

**OPEN.address.2**
here is your shard. everyone knows their sign; almost nobody knows
their sky within it. a sign is thirty degrees wide and the sun takes a
month to cross it. below is where inside yours you actually landed —
and the one place your chart was already moving when you got here.

**OPEN.address.3**
four sky-cultures divided this same band of stars and none of them
agreed on where the borders fell. arab, chinese, indian, and the old
european zodiac each named the stretch you were born under, and each
named it something different. below is what all four saw standing
there when you arrived.

## STEP — where in its sky each light stood

*Steps are never named as jargon on the surface (ANCHORS §2). These are
the plain-language inflections. Sun-role and moon-role differ in
temperature.*

**STEP.sun.entering** — the sun had just crossed into this sky when you
arrived: a beginner's position, and it stays with you. you meet most
things at the start, before anyone's decided what they are, and you like
it there.

**STEP.sun.dwelling** — the sun stood deep in the middle of this sky,
furthest from either door. this is the most settled place a light can be:
whatever this ground means, you got the full-strength version of it, with
no other sky bleeding in.

**STEP.sun.turning** — the sun had passed the middle and started to lean.
you carry a slight forward tilt in everything: half of you is always
already looking at what comes after this.

**STEP.sun.leaving** — the sun stood in the last stretch of this sky, near
the door. you inherited a deadline nobody handed you. whatever this ground
gives you, some part of you knows it isn't the last stop.

**STEP.moon.entering** — the moon had only just arrived here: your inner
weather still has the newness on it. you feel things early, sometimes
before there's anything to feel them about.

**STEP.moon.dwelling** — the moon sat in the deep middle. your inner life
runs at full saturation of this one thing — steady, un-mixed, and harder
to talk yourself out of than most people's.

**STEP.moon.turning** — the moon had begun its lean. you change your mind
by drifting, not by deciding, and then you're surprised to find you've
already moved.

**STEP.moon.leaving** — the moon stood near the far door. your inner life
is already packing. you grieve things while they're still happening, which
is either the most annoying thing about you or the most useful.

### The honest-fallback slots

*Fire when the birth time is unknown or the moon changed station that
day. Phrased as depth, never as apology (SIGIL-READING §3 rule 5).*

**STEP.moon.unknown** — you didn't give the clock. the moon crosses
one of these skies roughly every day, so the sky it stood in is
certain and its exact place inside that sky is the one thing only the
hour knows. birth certificates usually carry it. if yours turns up,
bring it back here.

**STEP.moon.crossing** — and your moon arrived on a **crossing day**: it
changed skies within hours of your birth. so your inner life was written
at a door, in the passage itself. people born on a crossing tend to hold
two weathers at once and be asked, all their lives, to pick one. you
don't have to. the sky didn't.

**FACING.unknown** — the horizon needs a clock. without a birth time
there is no way to say which sky was rising when you arrived —
it changes completely every four minutes — a different door for
someone born ten minutes after you, in the same room. nothing else on
your shard depends on it.

## LIGHT — the moon phase you were born under

*Anchor first, always: everyone believes the full moon does something.*

**LIGHT.new** — you were born at a **new moon**: the one night a month the
sky offers no moon at all. the sun and the moon were in the same stretch
of sky, so close the light was all on the far side. new-moon people start
things in the dark — not secretly, just early, before there is anything to
show. you have almost certainly been accused of having nothing to show. it
was never true; it was just unlit.

**LIGHT.waxing_crescent** — a **waxing crescent**: the first sliver, a few
days old. barely enough to see by, and already growing. you build in
increments so small other people don't register them as progress, and then
one day the thing exists. you are worse than most at explaining what
you're working on and better than most at finishing it.

**LIGHT.first_quarter** — a **half moon, filling**: the sky's exact
halfway point, lit on one side, dark on the other, no ambiguity about
which is which. you arrived already knowing that choosing something means
un-choosing something else. it makes you decisive and it makes you
occasionally ruthless, and you have known both those things about yourself
for a long time.

**LIGHT.waxing_gibbous** — a **fat, almost-full moon**, still filling. the
most impatient light in the sky: nearly there, not there. you are the one
who finishes other people's projects, and the one who cannot leave a thing
at 90%. rest is a skill you had to learn on purpose, if you've learned it.

**LIGHT.full** — a **full moon**. a moon is only full when it stands
opposite the sun across the whole width of the sky — so the distance
between your two lights, the very thing that can make you feel split,
is the same geometry that made the night you arrived bright. full-moon
people don't arrive subtle. people read light off you when you're
saying nothing at all. you were never hiding as well as you thought
you were.

**LIGHT.waning_gibbous** — a **full moon just past full**, beginning to
let go. you are the one who knows when a thing is finished, usually before
the room does. saying so out loud is your whole difficulty: you can see the
ending clearly and still have to wait for everyone to catch up.

**LIGHT.last_quarter** — a **half moon, emptying**: lit on one side, and
this time the dark side is the one that's growing. you are a natural
editor of your own life. what you keep, you keep on purpose. you
sometimes cut early, and call it clarity.

**LIGHT.waning_crescent** — a **thin old moon**, the last light before the
dark. you arrived good at endings — the packing-up, the last conversation,
the part everyone else avoids. it can look like sadness from outside. it
isn't. it's competence at the thing most people never get good at.

## KEEPER — the day that carried you

*The weekday's planet, anchored in the word itself. English keeps the
Norse gods; the Romance languages keep the Roman ones — the same figures,
twice, which is a free and genuinely delightful fact. **Scope, verified:**
the symmetry holds cleanly for tuesday–friday only. sunday and monday are
Germanic *translations* of the Latin (not god-swaps), saturday kept its
Roman god outright, and French turned sunday and saturday Christian
(*dimanche*, *samedi*) — so never write the parallel as absolute across
all seven.*

**KEEPER.sunday** — you arrived on a **sunday — the sun's own day; it's
right there in the word.** of the seven day-rulers the sun is the plainest
and the least strategic: it doesn't scheme, it doesn't hedge, it rises.
sunday-born get trusted fast and forgiven fast, and reliably underestimate
how much a room rearranges itself around their mood. yours does.

**KEEPER.monday** — you arrived on a **monday — moon-day, and again, it's
in the word.** the moon rules the day you landed on, and the moon rules
nothing steadily; it rules in tides. monday-born run on a weather system
rather than a schedule. you are not inconsistent. you are *periodic*, and
the people who learn your period stop taking it personally.

**KEEPER.tuesday** — you arrived on **tuesday — tyr's day in english,
mars's day in french** (*mardi*): the same war-god, named twice, because
two different peoples looked at the same red planet and agreed. tuesday
gives its children a working edge — the ability to start the
confrontation rather than wait for it. tyr lost his hand in the wolf's
mouth. he put it there as a pledge, and the other gods broke their
word. it was never a day about winning. it was a day about being
the one who pays what was promised, in a room where not everyone will.

**KEEPER.wednesday** — you arrived on **wednesday — odin's day, and
mercury's** (*mercredi*): the messenger and the wanderer, the two gods of
words and roads. wednesday-born think in transit. you work things out by
telling someone, which means you sometimes find out what you believe at
the same moment they do.

**KEEPER.thursday** — you arrived on **thursday — thor's day, and
jupiter's** (*jeudi*): the biggest planet, the loudest god, both of them
the same idea. thursday gives room. things you touch have a way of getting
larger — plans, rooms, arguments, generosity. the discipline you'll spend
your life learning is which of those you actually wanted to grow.

**KEEPER.friday** — you arrived on **friday — frigg's day, and venus's**
(*vendredi*): love in both languages. friday-born are unreasonably good at
making things pleasant, and that skill hides how much they notice. people
tell you things. you have probably never once had to ask.

**KEEPER.saturday** — you arrived on **saturday — saturn's day, spelled
out plainly, the only weekday in english that kept its roman god.** saturn
is the slowest planet the eye can catch: it keeps time, and it keeps
score. saturday-born get handed weight early and get *good* at it. the
lifelong work is telling the difference between what's genuinely yours to
carry and what you simply picked up first.

## MOVING — which of your lights stood at a door

*The Becoming's opening move. `INSTRUMENT.md` §3.5.*

**MOVING.sun** — the light standing closest to a door was **your sun** —
the part of your chart that says who you are. of all the ways a chart can
be in motion, this is the loudest: the person people already know is not
the final draft. this is not a warning. it means the description everyone
has of you was accurate once, and you have been quietly out of date for a
while now.

**MOVING.moon** — the light standing closest to a door was **your moon** —
the inner one, the part nobody gets shown. what feeds you is changing. the
outside of your life may look completely settled while this is happening,
which is exactly why it's disorienting: nothing is wrong, and it isn't
working the way it used to.

**MOVING.rising** — the light standing closest to a door was **your rising
sign** — the horizon, the meeting-place, the version of you that arrives
in a room before you do. how you enter is not settled. people who met you
five years ago met a different doorway. you may have already noticed that
your first impressions have stopped matching your reflection.

## BECOMING — the second figure

*Register set by distance to the edge (`INSTRUMENT.md` §3.3). All four are
real results; "rooted" is not a null state.*

**BECOMING.door** *(< 1°)* — and it stood **right at the threshold** —
less than a degree of sky from the next station. in the oldest changes-
oracle we borrowed this from, a line that close doesn't describe a
situation, it describes a *hinge*: read what's here, then read what it's
turning into, almost as loudly. yours is **{becomingEpithet}**. this is
not a someday. by the arithmetic of your own chart, you are already
standing in the doorway of it, and have been for some time.

**BECOMING.ripening** *(1–3.2°)* — and it stood in the last stretch of its
sky, a few degrees from the edge. that's a real tendency with a direction,
not a vague pull: what you are is ripening toward **{becomingEpithet}**.
things that ripen can't be hurried and can't be stopped, which is the most
irritating pair of facts about them.

**BECOMING.leaning** *(3.2–6.4°)* — and it had passed the middle of its
sky and begun to lean toward the next. so there's a pull in you, quieter
than a decision: **{becomingEpithet}** is the direction of it. it's worth
asking as a question rather than answering as a fact — the sky offers it,
it doesn't insist.

**BECOMING.rooted** *(> 6.4°)* — and none of your three lights stood
anywhere near a door. every one of them sat deep in its own sky. that is
its own result, and a rarer kind of luck than it sounds: nothing in your
chart is halfway out the window. you were not built to be in transition.
you were built to be *here* — to go deep on ground you already stand on
while other people spend their twenties changing shape. the tendency your
chart points at is the one you're already in.

## ECHO — the backward case

**ECHO.body** — one of your lights had only just crossed into its sky when
you arrived — still close enough to the door behind it to be carrying the
last one. that's an echo: **{echoEpithet}** came with you. it shows up as a
habit that doesn't match the rest of you, a taste you can't account for,
a competence you never trained for. you didn't pick it up along the way.
you walked in with it.

## DOUBLE_DOOR — the rare configuration

**DOUBLE_DOOR.body** — and here your chart does something uncommon: **two
of your lights stood at doors at once.** not one part of you in motion —
two, at the same time, in different skies. this is the configuration the
old changes-oracles gave their own special text to, because it doesn't
read like a tendency, it reads like a season. the ordinary version of this
is a year where the outside of your life and the inside of it both
insist on rearranging, refuse to do it in sync, and you conclude something
is wrong with you. nothing is wrong with you. you have two doors. they were
never going to open on the same schedule.

## GAIT — the synthesis, and the climax of the reading

*The one section that must name a cost. Each opens by restating the
geometry in plain sign language, shows the light-derivation, names the
tension with both accusations, then reframes.*

### GAIT.seedborn *(sun and moon in the same station — ~3.4%)*

your {sunSign} sun and your {moonSign} moon landed in the *same* sky —
the same twelve-and-a-half degrees out of three hundred and sixty. about
three travelers in a hundred arrive that way, and it has a consequence you
can check against any calendar: **you were born at a new moon.** the two
lights were too close together for the sky to hold any moon at all that
night. that's not a coincidence layered on top of your chart; it's the
same fact said twice.

on the road, that walk is called **seedborn**. what it means practically:
there is no gap in you between what you want and what you're like. most
people run a negotiation between their outside and their inside. you
don't have the seam, and that's why you're hard to talk out of things.

the same fact runs the other way. you have no second opinion in the
building. when you're right you're unstoppable, and when you're wrong
there is nobody inside your own chart to say so — you have to go out and
find that person, deliberately, and then actually listen. and you arrived
unlit: you begin things in the dark, and you will keep meeting people who
mistake *unlit* for *nothing there yet*. they're wrong, and correcting
them is not your job. finishing is.

### GAIT.homebound *(sun and moon under the same banner)*

your {sunSign} sun and your {moonSign} moon landed under the same
banner — **{sky}** — different skies, one country. your lights are
neighbors, which means they mostly agree.

on the road, that walk is called **homebound**, and the gift is coherence.
you are recognizably the same person in every room, your inside and your
outside tell the same story, and people find you restful in a way most of
them can't articulate. depth over distance was always the assignment.

agreement is comfortable, and comfort is not the same as truth. with
both lights facing the same direction, you can go a long way
without ever being interrupted by yourself — and the travelers who have to
argue with their own charts every morning get a kind of practice you don't.
the ordinary version of this is realizing at thirty that a belief you'd
held since childhood had simply never been challenged, by anyone, including
you. go and find your disagreement on purpose. your chart won't hand
it to you.

### GAIT.outbound *(the moon one banner ahead of the sun)*

your {moonSign} moon stood one banner *ahead* of your {sunSign} sun — out
in front, on ground the sun hadn't reached yet. that also tells you the
sky you were born under: your moon was **waxing**, filling toward full,
because a moon ahead of its sun is a moon on its way up.

on the road, that walk is called **outbound**. your inner life runs
scouting missions. you know how things are going to feel before you know
what they are, you get bored at the exact moment other people get
comfortable, and you have almost certainly been told you're restless by
someone who meant it as a diagnosis.

it isn't restlessness. it's that your inner light genuinely arrived at the
next place first, and the rest of you is doing the honest work of catching
up. **you are chronically early,
and being early looks identical to being wrong** — for a while. you'll
leave rooms before anyone understands why, name problems before there's
evidence, and lose arguments you were going to win in eighteen months.
learning to say *"i can't prove this yet"* out loud buys you the room
to be early without being alone.

### GAIT.emberwake *(the moon one banner behind the sun)*

your {moonSign} moon stood one banner *behind* your {sunSign} sun — on
ground the sun had already crossed. which means the moon you were born
under was **waning**: past its fullness, letting go of light. a moon behind
its sun is a moon in the long exhale.

on the road, that walk is called **emberwake** — the one who carries the
coal in the wake of the fire. what it means: you keep things. not
hoarding — *keeping*. you remember how people take their coffee and what
they said they were afraid of in 2019. you are the reason certain
friendships still exist. institutions, families, and group chats all
quietly run on someone with your chart, and they rarely notice.

a keeper is always slightly out of step with a room that's already
moved on. you'll be accused of living in the past. some of
the time the accusation will even be fair — there is a version of this
walk that turns into a museum of things that stopped mattering. the
question is *what the fire is for.* a coal
carried without a hearth in mind is just a burn. carried toward one, it's
the reason anyone gets warm.

### GAIT.farbank *(sun and moon in opposite banners)*

your {sunSign} sun stood on one bank; your {moonSign} moon on the far one —
opposite banners, the full width of the wheel between them. and that
distance is why the sky was bright the night you arrived: **a moon only
stands full when it faces its sun across everything.**

on the road, that walk is called **farbank**. you were born whole and
*wide*. the two halves of you are not missing each other — they're holding
opposite ends of the same river, and everything you love ends up strung
across it like a bridge.

what your sun wants and what your moon needs were never going to be
satisfied by the same afternoon. on bad weeks that lands as two
opposite accusations at once —
too much and too withholding, too quick to move on and too slow to let go.
both are wrong, and for the same reason: each one is only reading one
bank. **everything worth keeping gets built on the crossing** —
decided in the open, in full light, with both banks in view. it is the
only place your two halves were ever going to meet.

## CLOSE — the last lines

**CLOSE.1** — **{typeLabel} of {sunEpithet}.** that's the mark the sky
wrote down before anyone asked your name. the ring above holds all of
it — your {sunSign} sun, your {moonSign} moon, the door, the star that
answers. it's mostly dark. that isn't a lack; it's room. the road fills it
one crossing at a time, and the first crossing is tonight's.

**CLOSE.2** — **{typeLabel} of {sunEpithet}** — and one sky ahead,
{becomingEpithet}, waiting. nothing in here is a prediction; the sky
doesn't do those. it's a description of the ground you're standing on and
which way it slopes. the walking is yours. the first night of it is
tonight.

**CLOSE.3** *(rooted charts)* — **{typeLabel} of {sunEpithet}**, and no
part of it halfway out the door. you got the rarer thing: ground to stand
on. the ring above is mostly dark, which is only to say most of it is
still ahead of you. tonight's crossing is the first.
