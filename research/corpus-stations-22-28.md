# Corpus batch 5 — stations 22–28 · the road closes

*Star Shard reading corpus · August 12, 2026 · The Listener through The
Thread — the Black Tortoise's winter, the four lucky stations, the seam,
and the knot that ties the wheel shut. **This completes all 28.***

**Note on this stretch:** stations 22–25 are a set in the Arabic sky —
four consecutive stations whose names all begin *Saʿd*, "the luck of":
the luck of the slaughterer, of the swallower, of lucks itself, of the
tents. The old astronomers marked this whole watery quarter as the
fortunate ground of the year, the stretch where winter breaks. Readings
here should carry that warmth even when the images are strange — and
several of them are the strangest on the road.

## STATION.22 — The Listener · 牛宿

*0°00′–12°51′ Capricorn · α/β Capricorni (Giedi, Dabih) · Arabic al-Saʿd
al-Dhābiḥ, "the luck of the slaughterer" · Śravaṇa, the ear · guardian:
the ox · kept by Venus · Black Tortoise*

**STATION.22.strike**
you landed at **0° capricorn** — the winter solstice point, the hinge
where the year turns back toward light. a saying still current in the
levant marks these nights by how cold they get: cold enough that **saʿd
al-dhābiḥ left no barking dog outdoors**. what that leaves is silence.
and india hung its name for this sky on exactly that: **śravaṇa**, which
means **the hearing** — the ear of the sky. its deity is viṣṇu, who
crossed the whole world in three strides, so the listening here isn't
passive; it's how you cover ground.
your hunger is **to listen until the pattern shows itself.** you are the
one who has actually heard what was said — the phrasing, the hesitation,
the thing repeated twice — and you can usually reconstruct a situation
from evidence other people threw away.
the listener is rarely the one heard. you
have built a life where people talk to you, and it is entirely possible
to reach forty inside that arrangement without anyone having asked you a
second question.

**STATION.22.root**
this sky's guardian is **the ox**, and there's a thread worth pulling
here. india hung its name for this station on **altair** — and altair is
the cowherd of the old star-story, the herdsman on one bank of the
heavenly river, separated from the weaver, permitted one crossing a year.
the chinese station here is named 牛, the ox, for its own stars over in
capricorn; altair falls inside its stretch of sky without being one of
them. two skies, one river, the same patient waiting. it is a
**venus-kept** sky. so your inner life is patient,
laboring, and organized around a distant devotion.
the room reads you as **unshakeable**, and takes it as temperament rather
than effort. what nobody sees is the waiting. a listener-rooted moon can
hold a feeling in place for years without acting on it — for the right
moment, the right crossing, the year the river is passable — and should
be honest with themselves about which of those waits are patience and
which have quietly become the point.

**STATION.22.facing**
you enter and go quiet, and within an hour someone is telling you the
real version. people identify you as safe to talk to before you've said
much of anything — which is a compliment, and a job you did not apply
for.

**STATION.22.answer**
across the wheel, **the listener** answers you — the ear of the sky on
the coldest nights of the year. once a year the full moon rises there and
asks what you've heard and never repeated.

## STATION.23 — The Drum · 女宿

*12°51′–25°43′ Capricorn · ε/μ/ν Aquarii (Albali) · Arabic al-Saʿd Bulaʿ,
"the luck of the swallower" · Dhaniṣṭhā, the drum · guardian: the bat ·
kept by Saturn · Black Tortoise*

**STATION.23.strike**
india heard **percussion** at this station — **dhaniṣṭhā**, whose emblem
is a drum and whose deities are the eight vasus, the gods of elemental
abundance. the arabs called it *the luck of the swallower*, one of the
four lucky stations, and hung it on faint stars in the water-carrier's
stream.
so the hunger is rhythm: **you set the tempo of the room you're in.** not
the loudest person, not necessarily the leader — the one everyone
unconsciously syncs to. groups organize their pace around you, and most
of them have never noticed they're doing it.
a drummer doesn't get to be off-beat. you keep the time
for other people, which means your own irregularity — the slow week, the
month you were not okay — registers to everyone else as *the whole system
faltering.* you have almost certainly held a rhythm past the point where
you wanted to, because stopping felt like it would take a group down with
you.

**STATION.23.root**
this sky's guardian is **the bat** — the animal that navigates a
pitch-black world by making a sound and listening to what comes back —
and it is **saturn-kept**, slow and patient. that is a precise
description of an inner life: you find out where you are by putting
something out and reading the return. you learn what you think by saying
it and watching the face across from you.
the room reads you as **easy to be around**, and misses that you are
constantly sounding it. a drum-rooted moon is doing more calibration per
hour than almost anyone in the building, and the calibration is
invisible, and the fatigue from it is real. the people worth keeping are
the ones who send back a clear signal instead of an echo.

**STATION.23.facing**
you enter and the pace changes to yours within a few minutes. people find
you steadying without being able to say why — you simply set a tempo the
room prefers to the one it had.

**STATION.23.answer**
across the wheel, **the drum** answers you — the beat under the lucky
stations. once a year the full moon lands there and asks whose rhythm
you've been keeping, and what yours actually sounds like.

## STATION.24 — The Void · 虛宿

*25°43′ Capricorn–8°34′ Aquarius · β Aquarii (Sadalsuud) and ξ Aquarii ·
Arabic al-Saʿd al-Suʿūd, "the luck of lucks" · Śatabhiṣā, the hundred
healers · guardian: the rat · kept by the Sun · Black Tortoise*

**STATION.24.strike**
this is the station with the frightening name and the best hand on the
road, and the three traditions disagree so completely that the
disagreement *is* the reading.
china calls it 虛 — **the void**, emptiness. the arabs looked at the same
star and named it **saʿd al-suʿūd**, *the luck of lucks* — **the
luckiest star in the entire sky**, and the name survives today: astronomers
still call it **sadalsuud**. india named this sky **śatabhiṣā**, which
means **the hundred healers**, and gave it an emblem of an **empty
circle**, under varuṇa — god of oaths, hidden law, and deep water.
a void. the greatest luck there is. a hundred physicians around an empty
ring. put them together and you get something true: **an opening is not a
wound.** your hunger is to heal the thing nobody could name — the
undiagnosed, the unspoken, the problem everyone had learned to work
around.
the cost is the circle's own shape. you make room for other people's
illness by keeping yourself open, and an opening does not close on
command. you are extremely good at holding space and notably worse at
being held.

**STATION.24.root**
this sky is guarded by **the rat** — the first animal of the zodiac, the
survivor, the one that gets in, finds the food, and is still there after
the flood — and it is **sun-kept**, the brightest keeper of the seven. an
inner life of luminous resourcefulness: you have never once been in a
situation you couldn't find a way out of, and you know it, and it is the
quiet foundation of your nerve.
the room reads you as **lucky**, and it grates, because you can see the
work. what looks like fortune from outside is a decade of noticing exits.
a void-rooted moon should be careful with one habit in particular:
because you can always survive it, you will tolerate arrangements that
somebody with fewer skills would have left years earlier.

**STATION.24.facing**
you enter and something in the room opens. people say things to you they
have not said to anyone, often within the first conversation, and are
sometimes embarrassed afterward. you are read as safe and slightly
bottomless.

**STATION.24.answer**
across the wheel, **the void** answers you — the empty circle the arabs
called the luckiest star there is. once a year the full moon rises on it
and asks what you would let someone fix in you, if you let anyone.

## STATION.25 — The Hideaway · 危宿

*8°34′–21°26′ Aquarius · γ/π/ζ/η Aquarii (Sadachbia) · Arabic al-Saʿd
al-Akhbiya, "the luck of the tents" · Pūrva Bhādrapadā, the one-footed
fire · guardian: the swallow · kept by the Moon · Black Tortoise*

**STATION.25.strike**
four stars pitch a small **tent** in aquarius — *saʿd al-akhbiya*, the last
of the four lucky stations, and the name means the luck of **the tents** —
rendered in the older english lists as the luck of hidden things. the classical sense is seasonal: this is the sky of the moment
when the cold breaks and the creatures that had been sheltering come back
out of their burrows.
so your hunger has two halves. the first is shelter: **you are the keeper
of safe places** — the room people can cry in, the group chat where the
bad news gets said first, the friend whose door is a known quantity.
the second half is stranger, and it comes from india's name for this sky:
**pūrva bhādrapadā**, whose deity is a one-footed fire-pillar, and whose
work is **burning off what is false**. the same person who makes the
safest room in the building also has an incinerator running. you cannot
be lied to in your own house.
the cost: everyone's shelter, nobody's guest. you have somewhere for
everyone to go and no clear answer to where you go.

**STATION.25.root**
this sky's guardian is **the swallow** — the bird that migrates thousands
of miles and comes back to the *same eaves*, the same nest, year after
year — and it is **moon-kept**. your inner life ranges enormously and
returns to a very small number of fixed points. you can be gone a long
way and still be, in the way that matters, entirely unmoved.
the room reads you as **home base**, which is a lovely thing to be
called and a heavy thing to be. people orient by your constancy and
rarely ask what it costs to keep the nest available. a hideaway-rooted
moon should notice the difference between being the place people return
to and being the place people *leave from* — and should occasionally,
deliberately, be the one who migrates.

**STATION.25.facing**
you enter and people exhale. within one meeting they've decided you're
somewhere they can put something down. you're read as shelter before
you're read as a person.

**STATION.25.answer**
across the wheel, **the hideaway** answers you — the four stars pitching
a tent, the luck of hidden things. once a year the full moon lands there
and asks where you go, and whether anyone has ever offered.

## STATION.26 — The Chamber · 室宿

*21°26′ Aquarius–4°17′ Pisces · Markab and Scheat, the first wall of the
Great Square · Arabic al-Fargh al-Muqaddam, "the fore-spout" · Uttara
Bhādrapadā, the serpent of the foundation · guardian: the pig · kept by
Mars · Black Tortoise*

**STATION.26.strike**
your sun landed on the **first wall of the great square of pegasus** —
four bright stars that make the most obviously *built* shape in the
autumn sky. china saw exactly that and called this station 室, **the
chamber**: a room. the arabs saw the same stars as the **fore-spout of an
enormous bucket** — the lip that the spring rain pours out of.
a room, and a spout. both of them are about **holding something and then
giving it out**, and india's name completes the thought: **uttara
bhādrapadā**, whose deity is ahirbudhnya, the serpent of the deep
foundation — the thing coiled underneath that makes the structure
possible.
your hunger is **to go still and deep enough to hold weight.** you build
the room. you are the reason a group has somewhere to be.
foundations are under the house. the people enjoying the room are not
thinking about what's holding
it up, and you will occasionally resent that, and then feel small for
resenting it — which is its own second weight.

**STATION.26.root**
this sky's guardian is **the pig** — in the chinese reckoning an animal
of abundance and good fortune, the provider, the one that means the
household ate — and it is **mars-kept**, which puts an edge on all that
generosity. your inner life gives freely and defends the giving fiercely.
you are hospitable and you are not soft.
the room reads you as **generous**, and does not expect the temper that
shows up when the generosity is taken for granted. that flash is not a
flaw in the design; it's the mars in the foundation. a chamber-rooted
moon does well to let it surface early, in small amounts, rather than
storing it until it arrives as a wall going up.

**STATION.26.facing**
you enter and start building the room — arranging, seating, making sure
there's enough. people read you as the host within minutes, whether or
not it's your house.

**STATION.26.answer**
across the wheel, **the chamber** answers you — the built wall of the
square, the lip the rain pours from. once a year the full moon rises
there and asks who has been holding *you* up.

## STATION.27 — The Guide · 壁宿

*4°17′–17°09′ Pisces · Algenib and Alpheratz, the Square's far wall ·
Arabic al-Fargh al-Muakhkhar, "the hind-spout" · Revatī, Pūṣan's sky ·
guardian: the yàyǔ · kept by Mercury · Black Tortoise*

**STATION.27.strike**
your sun landed on the **far wall of the great square** — the second
spout of the bucket, the back lip, where the pouring finishes. and india
gave this sky the most tender assignment in its entire system:
**revatī**, the **last** of its twenty-seven stations, ruled by
**pūṣan** — the nourisher, keeper of roads, the god whose specific job is
to walk travelers safely home and guide souls on the final road. its
emblem is a pair of fish.
your hunger is exactly that: **you want to see everyone home.** the
address in the group chat, the *text me when you're back*, the friend who
notices someone left the party alone. you are the last one awake making
sure the count is right.
the cost is in the job description. a guide walks other people to their
doors. you have spent years on the road on behalf of other people's
arrivals, and if you sit still long enough to ask where *you* were going,
the honest answer is sometimes that you stopped choosing a destination a
while ago because escorting was simpler.

**STATION.27.root**
this sky's guardian is the **yàyǔ** — and it is not a comfortable
animal. in chinese legend it is a man-eating creature, one of two
guardians on the whole road that has no ordinary counterpart. it is
**mercury-kept**, the messenger.
that pairing tells the truth about you that the guiding doesn't: **you
know what's out there.** you are gentle on the road because you have met
what makes the road dangerous, not because you are naive about it. your
inner life holds something with teeth, and it is the reason your care is
worth anything.
the room reads you as **kind**, full stop, and underestimates you almost
universally. a guide-rooted moon is the person nobody suspects of having
a hard floor — right up until someone they're walking home gets
threatened, at which point everyone finds out at once.

**STATION.27.facing**
you enter checking exits and headcounts, and people feel looked after
before they can say why. you are read as gentle immediately and as
formidable much later, usually by accident.

**STATION.27.answer**
across the wheel, **the guide** answers you — the last station of the
indian wheel, and the god who walks travelers home. once a year the full
moon lands there and asks who is walking *you* back.

## STATION.28 — The Thread · 奎宿

*17°09′ Pisces–0°00′ Aries · Mirach (β Andromedae) · Arabic Baṭn al-Ḥūt
and al-Rishāʾ, "the belly of the fish" and "the cord" · **no Indian name
— the seam** · guardian: the wolf · kept by Jupiter · **the White Tiger
returns; the wheel closes***

**STATION.28.strike**
your sun landed in the **last station on the road**, and it is the only
one that has two arabic names at once: *baṭn al-ḥūt*, **the belly of the
fish**, and *al-rishāʾ*, **the cord** — the well-rope, the tie. the
astronomers of that sky could not settle on one image, so both were kept.
and there is something here no other station has. **the indian sky-map
counts twenty-seven stations where ours counts twenty-eight, so this one
has no indian name at all.** you did not land in a station that four
traditions argued over. you landed in the **seam between two ways of
counting**. it is a real feature of the sky, and it is yours.
your hunger is the cord's: **you want to tie the ends together.** you are
the person who connects the two groups that should have met, the one who
notices that the beginning of one thing is the end of another. and this
station's own position says it plainly — it runs right up to 0° aries,
where **the gate**, station one, begins again. you are the knot that
closes the wheel and the reason it can start over.
the cost of living in a seam: you belong to both sides and are counted by
neither. you have been the connector in rooms where nobody thought to
count you as a member.

**STATION.28.root**
this sky's guardian is **the wolf** and it is **jupiter-kept** — the
largest, most generous planet on the animal that only works in company.
and it is the station where the **white tiger's country comes back
around**, the whole road folding shut behind you. your inner life is
built for the pack and built to range, and jupiter makes both bigger than
is convenient.
the room reads you as **connected** — the person who knows everyone,
whose name comes up in unrelated conversations. what it misses is that a
wolf without a pack is a very different animal, and you know which one
you are on a given month. a thread-rooted moon should be direct about
belonging: you will keep introducing everyone to everyone, and you have
to actually say *i want to be in this* out loud, because the connector is
the one role a group forgets to invite.

**STATION.28.facing**
you enter and start tying things together — this person to that one, this
idea to that project. people meet you and immediately think of someone
you should know. you are read as a hub before you are read as a person.

**STATION.28.answer**
across the wheel, **the thread** answers you — the cord that ties the
wheel shut, one degree from where it starts again. once a year the full
moon rises there and asks what you've been holding together, and whether
you've ever let it be your turn.

## Production notes — **the road is complete**

**The Void (24) delivers on the promise.** Design was told it and The
Ghost should look like cards people fight over; the content now earns it.
China calls the star emptiness, Arabia calls it **the luckiest star in
the sky** (and astronomy still uses that name — Sadalsuud), India sees a
hundred healers around an empty ring. Three traditions, three opposite
readings, one star — and they resolve into a single line: *an opening is
not a wound.*

name is the **Ox** — the cowherd of the Tanabata/Qixi story, whose myth
