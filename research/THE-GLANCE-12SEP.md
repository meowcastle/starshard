# The glance: the two stars arm the leader, the ill road is a seat law that widens, and the door is a leader's square

**12 September 2026. For Design.** 1a on both dials crossed (four rows), 1b at both depths, 1c as
control, standard window, with m9 plain at 896 on both windows, the far-strike rate and the
door-order row. 138 vectors in the station-law suite. 896 boards a cell, two seeds, walker
boards; the two telemetry rows at 448.

**Nothing on the sheet ships.** Every one of the two stars' four rows fails the same two bounds
in the same direction, and the ill road passes the bound it was built for and fails the one it
was not. But the sheet bought two facts the calendar did not have, and both are worth more than
the laws that surfaced them.

### The two telemetry rows

**The door-order row.** On m9's night, plain, the leader lodges the door first in **71.2% of
boards fresh and 70.3% deep**, the answerer in the rest, and the door is never left empty. **The
door is a leader's square**, seven boards in ten, at every depth. That is the tempo fact Design
asked for and it is the key to everything else on the sheet.

**The far-strike rate**, the two stars at both/printed. Both ends of the road fill every board
(2.0 pair lodges a board; fourteen cards for nine stations means the road always fills), so the
second arrival always finds the first: **the law fires once a board, 1.01 looks**, which is the
ceiling Design named. The look takes 36% of the time fresh and 48% deep. Fresh, the door is the
second arrival 62% of the time (the end fills first), and **the leader is the one firing it 60% of
the time fresh**, 46% deep.

## 1a the two stars: a free strike that the leader gets to take

All four rows fail on the same two columns, and the dials only change how badly. Seat fresh
**+8.2 to +11.4**, over the bound on every row. Fresh skill **−6.3 to −11.9**, which is a third to
two-thirds of the calendar's best skill gap spent on one law. Spread barely moves. Deep, the law is
close to inert (seat +0.3 to +2.0).

The far-strike rate says why. The two stars are one free strike a board, awarded to whoever
arrives at the pair second, and fresh the leader arrives second three times in five. A strike
nobody had to earn, handed to the side that already has the last word, is a seat law pointed the
wrong way. And it is a careless player's law: the free strike is an immediate gain that a
depth-zero search sees in full, while the careful player's advantage lives in the replies, which
the strike does not touch. That is what −11.9 skill looks like from inside the sim: the law makes
the board simpler, and simpler pays the player who was not thinking.

**The dials answer their questions, and neither answer helps.** Door-only halves the damage
(seat +8.2, skill −6.3) because it removes the far end's look, and the far end is where the fresh
leader most often lands second; it does not remove enough. So the pairing does not narrow because
it is symmetric or because it hands the answerer a strike; **it does not narrow at all**, on either
notch. Printed against unprinted is within a point on every column: on an eight-station look the
raised face was never the compounding, because on this night the raised face is rare. The throne's
clause was borrowed for a problem this road does not have.

**The card's own risk paragraph was right about the mechanism and wrong about the victim.** It
worried the law would arm the bird, and the bird moves +3 to +5. It arms the *leader*, which no
quadrant row could have shown and the door-order row shows in one line.

## 1b the ill road: the seat law with a fiction, and it widens

Design's framing was exact: if the door is a leader's square, 1b is a seat law with a fiction. The
door is a leader's square at 71%, and the ill road cools seat **−7.2 fresh, −4.6 deep at 1**,
inside the bound, with the skill gap giving up 3.5 of its 16.9 and staying the calendar's best.
**As a seat instrument it is the eye's equal on this night, and its fiction is better than the
eye's.**

It fails on spread: **25.2 → 38.5, the bird +10.9, the dragon −5.5**. At 2 the seat goes over the
bound (−9.0) and the widening is a little less (+9.4), so the second notch is a wall, not a nudge,
and it is the wrong wall.

I do not have the bird's +10.9 explained and I would rather say so than guess. The door on this
window is the bird's own mansion, where dominion doubles, and the law taxes whoever takes it
first; one reading is that the tax turns the other three quarters away from the door while the
bird, with its home count there, keeps taking it and keeps the doubled station. That is a
hypothesis. A door-order row split by quadrant would settle it in one run if Design wants the ill
road pursued, and I would want it settled before anything with this shape ships anywhere.

**On the class question.** "Stored on the card" is a real column, and the ill road is not its
first row: the storm's brand (`scarAt`, the mark a taken card carries off the crossing) was the
first, measured inert on both of m6's windows. So the column has two rows, one inert and one that
moves seat 7 and spread 13 in opposite directions. What the two share is the thing that matters
for the scorecard: **the condition is on the station and the effect is on the traveller, so the
law's size is set by who takes the station first, which is a tempo fact about the night rather
than a card fact.** The ill road is portable in shape only to nights whose door is a leader's
square, and we know that for exactly one night. It should be scored as a tempo law with a stored
effect, not as a fight law; the fight-class 3/3 does not transfer to it.

## 1c the price: flat here, and that was the point

Inert on the standard window (spread +1.9, seat −0.1). On m5's night the same station, the
glance's own, took 19.2 off the tiger with the price standing on it. So the price's second use on
this sheet delivered: **the crossing is doing the work, not the ground.** On m5's road the middle
is m9 and the tiger's sheltered cards go there to strike; on m9's road the middle is m13 the hand
and nothing sheltered wants it. Design's rule follows: neither 1a nor 1b is a shelter law and
neither should be read as one. Nine nights, nine passes, and the yardstick's most useful reading
yet was a zero.

## Recommendation

**Nothing ships from this sheet.** The two stars are retired on all four notches: one free strike
a board, taken by the leader. The ill road is held, not retired, pending the quadrant split on the
door-order row; it is the best seat fiction on the calendar and the wrong spread.

**What the sheet leaves behind is the door-order row**, and I would carry it to every remaining
night before a law is drafted: 71% is a large number and it will not be the same on every road.
A night whose door is an answerer's square inverts every door law we have seen, which is what the
card predicted; the row costs one run and tells Design which family a night's door belongs to
before a sentence is written. It joins the deep seat on both windows as the second thing to read
about a night before its sheet.

**And a note on m9 itself.** Plain, it is the best skill board on the calendar by a distance and
inside every bound with a tortoise floor of 28.3. If the glance's night ends up shipping as "the
night with no law", it will be the strongest plain board a player meets, and there are worse
fictions for a house whose card was sent back for seeing nothing than a night on which nothing
is added.
