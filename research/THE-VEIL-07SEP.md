control, all on both windows. 125 vectors in the station-law suite. 896 boards a cell, two seeds,
except the two deck-blind rows at 448 (the belief costs 5×; still inside the convention's floor).
Walker boards.

**Nothing on the sheet ships clean.** The cover fails on both windows, for a reason that adds a
footnote to the portability rule. The hidden face is exactly what Design said it was against the
mirror, and I can now say so with a blinded mirror rather than an unblinded one. The hush narrows
the slid window to the narrowest board we have measured and widens the door-first one. So by the
sheet's own order of preference, **the veil's night is the hush on the slid window, as "a quiet

## 1a the cover: a swap is a tax on whichever quarter holds the lopsided cards

Door-first the cover moves the tortoise **−14.7** and the tiger +3.1, and the board goes from the
narrowest on the sheet to 31.3 wide. The dial makes no difference (both is a little worse).

Design's card already had the mechanism: *"the mirrored cards swing most, the nine symmetric ones
cannot be turned at all."* What the card did not have is that the mirrored cards are not evenly
dealt. Summing |left − right| across each quarter's seven cards:

| quarter | lopsidedness | cards that can be turned |
|---|---|---|
| byakko | 4 | 3 |
| suzaku | 7 | 5 |
| seiryuu | 13 | 6 |
| **genbu** | **16** | 5 (the district 3/8, the void 9/3, the drum 5/7, the listener 7/5) |

So a swap conserves both numbers *per card* and is nothing like neutral *per quarter*. The
tortoise's two best cards, the district and the void, are the two most lopsided in the deck, and
the cover turns the void into a 3 facing the road. That is the same thing that happened on the
turning's night in the other direction (suzaku +11.5, the bird's grant reaching two stations):
**the turn family reads the deck's asymmetry, and the deck's asymmetry is a quarter property.**

Which is a footnote to Friday's rule. *Condition on what a card is, not on how two cards compare*
still holds; the cover does condition on what a card is. But **a card property that is dealt
unevenly across the quarters is a quarter condition wearing a card's clothes**, and a swap law is
the clearest case, because it does nothing at all to the symmetric cards and the most to the
lopsided ones. Shelter is dealt evenly (every quarter has a grant); lopsidedness is not.

On the slid window the cover is nearly flat on spread but takes 5.6 to 7.3 off the deep skill gap,
which is the bound that exists to catch a law that makes careful play worth less. Design's card
named exactly that hazard, "the level has to make the cover read as hiding a face, not flipping
one", and the number says the flip is what the board feels. The neighbouring signatures (the
jewel turning to face, the claws shoving) partly undo the turn on this window, which is why the
spread holds; they do not give the careful player anything back.

**1a does not ship on either window.**

## 1b the hidden face: the question, answered with a row

Design asked whether the harness could blind her reply to a covered face, and what it costs.

**It can, it costs 5×, and here is the thing I found on the way in that matters more than the
cost: the reference search is full-information on both sides, hands included.** The sky reads the
player's hand when she weighs a reply and the player reads hers. Every law measured to date was
measured under that. So "covered from her" is not a flag; it needs a stated belief about what is
under the cover. I built two:

- **blind/deck** — she sees the art and not the numbers; the covered pair could be any of the 28
  printed pairs. This is the sentence exactly ("its numbers show to nobody").
- **blind/hand** — she knows the hand and not which card was lodged; the pair is one of the cards
  the player was holding. This is the PvP bluff on the card ("you know what you lodged, they do not").

The real board keeps its real faces. Only the score is blinded, at every leaf where a covered enemy
card stands, averaged over the belief. `hideN` is both dials: until struck (any strike, landed or
not, shows the numbers) and until the count (never shown; the count does not read faces).

**Three results.**

1. **Unblinded, the hidden face is plain to the tenth on both windows.** That is Design's "against
   the mirror it is nothing" as a measured fact rather than a prediction, and it is also the
   conformance check: the rule changes no arithmetic, so a harness that shows any difference here
   has a bug. (Mine did, for an hour: the reveal replaced the slot object and the take landed on a
   dead copy, which made covered cards untakeable and read as seat +21. Fixed in place; the vector
   that catches it is now in the suite.)
2. **Blinded, it is still nothing.** Hand belief moves no cell more than 2.3. Deck belief moves no
   cell more than 2.7 at 448 boards, where the noise floor is about that. Until-the-count is the
   same as until-struck. Every row is flat.
3. **So the cost of blinding her is not the compute. It is that blinding her does not produce a
   law.** A search that averages over what it cannot see makes nearly the same reply as one that
   can see, because on a 9-station road the reply that is best against the expected card is
   almost always the reply that is best against the real one. A bluff has no bite against a player
   who does not flinch, and the mirror never flinches.

That is not a failure of the rule and it is not a pass. **It is the sim saying it cannot see this
law**, which is the honest answer to a law about what a player knows. Whatever the hidden face is
worth, it is worth it against a human, and the reference has no human in it. Design's own test was
"send it only if Measurement can give the blind row." Here is the row, and it says the mirror
walks through the veil without noticing it.

**Hold it for a PvP-only night, as the card already proposed.** Not on the strength of these rows,
which say nothing either way about PvP, but because the single-player road cannot be the place
where the first information law is judged, and the reference cannot be the instrument.

## 1c the hush: travels to the slid window, not the door-first one

**Slid: seat +7.7 → −0.4 fresh, spread 20.0 → 6.1, tiger +12.3, tortoise +8.8, bird −7.4,
dragon −5.1.** That is a true NARROWS, top down and floor up on both ends, to the narrowest board
we have measured on any night. It is also the hush doing precisely what it did on m21: the deep
seat drops (−6.6 here, −17.4 there) with the leader row barely touched.

**One bound is a tenth over.** Deep skill falls 5.6, against a rule of "not more than 5". Fresh
skill rises (+2.5), and the careful player still wins 22.7 more than the careless one deep. I am
reporting it as a pass with the tenth shown rather than rounding it either way; Design can decide
whether the narrowest board on the calendar is worth a tenth of a point on a bound we wrote before
we had a board this narrow.

**Door-first: fails.** Deep seat −9.9 (over ±8), spread widens 6.2, the bird −12.9. Same clause,
same index, the root at the middle instead of the veil. The hush counts two stations for nobody,
and door-first those two are m18 the heart and m20 the flock, the bird's reach-two grant making
both of them bird ground for a card that should be scoring there; slid they are m14 the jewel and
m16 the claws, dragon ground, and silencing dragon ground on a board the dragon tops at 52.0 is
what narrowing looks like.

So the hush is **1/2 on windows**, which is the same shape as the shell's 1/2 on nights: **a count
law reads what its stations are worth, and which mansion stands on the station is what the window
changes.** It is portable in the sense that its verb never breaks a board it was built for; it is
not portable in the sense of being safe to move without a row. Same conclusion as the shell,
reached from the other side.

## Recommendation

**The hush, on the slid window, as "a quiet night."** `lawAt {15:{station:5, kind:"hush"}}` in
Design's numbering, **index 4** in the reference and in Code's map, and the window for m15 set so
that the veil stands at the middle (`_boardOff` 15:4, the same slide as m2, m19, m21, m23, m25,
m28). One dial flip in the reference, one entry in the client, nothing new to build; the district's
verb at a second address, which is the thing Design said it would ship if 1a failed and 1b could
not be measured. Both of those happened.

**The cover is retired**, on both windows, and takes the turn family with it as a portable shape:
swap laws are quarter laws, and the quarter they tax is whichever holds the lopsided cards.

**The hidden face waits for a human opponent**, which is where the card already wanted it. The
harness can now blind the mirror at two beliefs if a later sheet needs it; nothing about this
sheet suggests one will.

**Two notes for the loop bookmark.** The door-first m15 board is the fairest plain ground in the
tested set (10.8 wide) and it has the second-thinnest fresh skill gap (+1.5): the same pattern as
m1, fairness and thinness arriving together. And this is the second law in a week whose only fail
was the deep-skill bound by a point or so; when the loop work revisits the bound policy, the
deep-skill floor is the one to look at first.
