# Corpus batch 2 — stations 1–7

*Star Shard reading corpus · August 12, 2026 · The Gate through The
Return. Written to `corpus-spine.md`'s voice, `INSTRUMENT.md` §4's
four-axis grammar, `ANCHORS.md`'s anchor law, and the verified data in
`research/hunger-axis.md`.*

**Four slots per station.** `strike` (~150w, the Sun's section — leads
with the *image*, lands on the *hunger*) · `root` (~140w, the Moon's
section — leads with the *guardian*, lands on the *mirror*) · `facing`
(~40w, the rising sign) · `answer` (~30w, when this station is someone's
Farlight). The strike and root never repeat each other's material; a
traveler with both lights here gets two different readings of one sky.

**Not reusable:** the existing `birthEntry` card-back prose. That is the
kawaii card voice, it stays on the cards, and these are written fresh.

## STATION.01 — The Gate · 婁宿

*0°00′–12°51′ Aries · β/γ Arietis (Sheratan) · Arabic al-Sharaṭān, "the
two signs" · Aśvinī, the twin physicians · guardian: the dog · kept by
Venus · White Tiger*

**STATION.01.strike**
you landed at the very front of **aries** — and not just early aries: the
first stretch of the first station, the point the entire wheel counts
from. every degree of sky anyone has ever measured is measured from
somewhere near where your sun stood. the old desert astronomers called
these two stars *the two signs*: the pair of horns that opened the year,
the marker you watched for to know the season had turned. india put the
twin horse-headed physicians here — the gods' first responders, the ones
who arrive at the accident before anyone has finished calling for help.
that is your hunger, and it isn't ambition: **you want to be there
first**, before you're asked, before it's officially anyone's problem.
the cost is exact. arriving first and being ready are different skills,
and this sky only gave you the one. you will get to things you cannot yet
finish, and the hardest habit you'll ever build is staying past the
arrival.

**STATION.01.root**
the sky your moon took root in is kept by **the dog** — the one who hears
it at the gate before the house does, and says so, every time, including
the nine times it was nothing. and it is a **venus-kept** sky, which
changes the whole character of that alarm: your warning system runs on
affection. you don't notice trouble because you're suspicious. you notice
because you were already watching the people you love.
here's how the room reads you: **early**. you'll name the thing before
there's evidence, and be called dramatic, and then be right, and the
apology never quite arrives. the quiet cost of a gate-rooted moon is that
you learn to doubt your own alarm to keep the peace — and yours is
better calibrated than almost anyone's in the building.

**STATION.01.facing**
you enter through **the gate itself** — the opening mark of the whole
wheel. people meet a beginning when they meet you: you make rooms feel
like something is about to start. it's a genuine gift and it is
occasionally exhausting to be the weather that arrives.

**STATION.01.answer**
across the wheel, **the gate** answers you — the sky where the year turns
over. once a year the full moon nearest your birthday lights the place
everything starts counting from, and asks what you'd begin if beginning
were free.

## STATION.02 — The Bearer · 胃宿

*12°51′–25°43′ Aries · ε/δ/ρ Arietis (Botein) · Arabic al-Buṭayn, "the
little belly" · Bharaṇī, Yama's station of passage · guardian: the
pheasant · kept by Saturn · White Tiger*

**STATION.02.strike**
you landed in **late aries**, and here the ram stops charging. the arabs
looked at these three dim stars and saw *the little belly* — the soft
underside where anything valuable gets carried, close and warm and out of
sight. india named the same sky for the one who **bears**: its deity is
yama, who meets people at the threshold and carries them across the one
crossing nobody makes alone. so your hunger isn't conquest, whatever your
sign's reputation promised. **you want to get the precious thing to the
other side.** you have been handed responsibilities early and repeatedly,
by people who could not have explained why they trusted you.
the cost is the obvious one and it is worse than people think: a bearer
is measured by what they carry, so you keep accepting weight as proof.
some of what you've been handed was never yours. you've known that about
one or two things for years.

**STATION.02.root**
this sky's guardian is **the pheasant** — bright enough to be spotted
across a field, and a bird that nests on the ground and would rather run
than fly. and it is **saturn-kept**: the slowest, heaviest, most
patient of the seven. that pairing is your inner weather exactly —
conspicuous and earthbound, noticed and unhurried, and old for your age
in a way that arrived without permission.
the room reads you as **steady**, which is true and is also the trap.
people bring you their weight because you don't visibly buckle, and the
not-buckling gets mistaken for not-feeling. a bearer-rooted moon grieves
in private and hosts in public. the discipline of your life is learning
to say *this one is too heavy* out loud, to someone, before it becomes
a fact you carry alone.

**STATION.02.facing**
you enter carrying something. people can't always see what, but they
feel it — the sense that you arrived from somewhere with a purpose,
already holding a thing that matters. rooms hand you responsibility
within minutes of meeting you.

**STATION.02.answer**
across the wheel, **the bearer** answers you — the soft place where
valuable things travel. once a year the full moon lands there and asks
the only question that sky knows: *what are you carrying, and where were
you taking it?*

## STATION.03 — The Gathered Stars · 昴宿

*25°43′ Aries–8°34′ Taurus · the Pleiades (M45), determinant Alcyone ·
Arabic al-Thurayyā, "the many little ones" · Kṛttikā, Agni's blade and
flame · guardian: the rooster · kept by the Sun · White Tiger*

**STATION.03.strike**
your sun landed on the most famous handful of stars in the sky: the
**pleiades**. japan calls them **subaru** — the gathered ones; that is
where the car logo comes from, and the stars in the badge are these. the
arabs called them *the many little ones* and made them so central that
you could say simply "the star" and everyone knew which. six or seven
separate lights that everyone's eye reads as **one shine**.
india names this sky **kṛttikā**, and its deity is agni, fire — its
emblem a blade. that is the hunger under all that prettiness: **you want
to cut a thing down to the part that's actually true.** you are not the
soft light people expect from the prettiest station on the road.
the cost lives inside the gift: a cluster is loved as a cluster. you
have spent real time unsure whether you were being seen or whether the
group you were part of was, and the blade in you knows the difference
even when you're too polite to say it.

**STATION.03.root**
this sky is guarded by **the rooster** and kept by **the sun** — the
announcer and the announced, which is a loud pair to have running your
inner life. the rooster's whole job is to be the first sound of the day,
whether or not the day asked. something in you is always on the verge of
declaring.
the room reads you as **a signal**. people orient by you, take your mood
as the room's temperature, and check your face before deciding how to
feel. flattering, and a genuine weight: you rarely get to be quietly
unwell. a gathered-rooted moon needs at least one person who doesn't
read them for weather — and usually has to go find that person on
purpose, because everyone else has already assigned you the job.

**STATION.03.facing**
you enter as a group even when you're alone — a bright, plural first
impression that people remember before they remember details. it takes
them a while to notice that under the shine there is a blade with an
opinion.

**STATION.03.answer**
across the wheel, **the gathered stars** answer you — the little heap of
lights everyone knows. once a year the full moon rises there and asks
what you'd cut away if being loved as part of the cluster weren't the
price.

## STATION.04 — The Follower · 畢宿

*8°34′–21°26′ Taurus · Aldebaran and the Hyades · Arabic al-Dabarān,
"the follower" · Rohiṇī, Prajāpati's station of growth · guardian: the
crow · kept by the Moon · White Tiger*

**STATION.04.strike**
your sun landed on **aldebaran** — the red eye of the bull, one of the
brightest stars in the sky, and named for what it does rather than what
it is. *al-dabarān* means **the follower**: it rises just behind the
pleiades and chases them across the sky every night, forever, without
catching them.
and here is the fact the name never mentions. **aldebaran is far brighter
than any star in the cluster it follows.** that is your entire reading.
you are behind something on purpose — a person, a standard, a version of
this you saw once and can't unsee — and you outshine it, and the arrangement
suits you, and you'd rather not discuss it. india's name for this sky is
**rohiṇī**: *growing*, the station of things that thicken and take root
where they stand. your hunger is not to arrive; it's to make the thing
you're tending get **bigger**.
the cost: following is only a strategy while you're still learning. some
people never notice the day it turned into a hiding place.

**STATION.04.root**
this sky's guardian is **the crow** — memory with wings, the animal that
recognizes faces years later, uses tools, and keeps a ledger. and it's
**moon-kept**, so that ledger is emotional. your inner life records
everything, precisely, with the feeling still attached.
the room reads you as **unbothered**, right up until it discovers you
remember exactly what was said in 2019 and how it landed. that's the
gift and the risk in one: a follower-rooted moon can carry a grievance
the way other people carry keys, without effort, for years. what you
choose to file and what you choose to release is not a small question
for you. it is, quietly, the whole shape of your life.

**STATION.04.facing**
you enter a step behind the front of the room, and end up being the
person everyone checks with. people mistake it for shyness for about a
month, until they notice that nothing gets past you and the quiet was
attention.

**STATION.04.answer**
across the wheel, **the follower** answers you — the bright red eye that
chases the cluster and never closes the gap. once a year the full moon
lands there and asks what you're still following, and whether you've
noticed you're brighter than it.

## STATION.05 — The Blaze · 觜宿

*21°26′ Taurus–4°17′ Gemini · λ/φ¹/φ² Orionis, the hunter's head ·
Arabic al-Haq'a, "the white blaze" · Mṛgaśīrṣa, the deer's head ·
guardian: the monkey · kept by Mars · White Tiger*

**STATION.05.strike**
your sun landed on a **small triangle of faint stars on orion's head** —
and both of the traditions that named it saw the same thing, which almost
never happens. the arabs called it *al-haq'a*: **the white mark** — the
same word they used for a pale whorl on a horse's coat, the mark that
makes one animal identifiable in a herd. india called this sky **mṛgaśīrṣa**, the **deer's
head**. one tradition marked the hunter's brow, the other put a deer
there; between them sits the reading — you are the mark on the hunter and
the thing being hunted, at once.
the old station-lists gave this sky travel and study, and the hunger fits
both: **you follow a faint trail because it's faint.** you are the person
who leaves to learn something and comes back changed and slightly
unbearable about it.
the cost is that the search is genuinely more pleasurable than the
finding. you can leave in order to keep leaving, and call it curiosity
for a decade.

**STATION.05.root**
this sky is guarded by **the monkey** and kept by **mars** — quick hands
and a short fuse. the monkey solves the latch nobody thought was a
puzzle; mars makes sure it does it now. your inner life runs fast, gets
bored faster, and does its best thinking while provoking something.
the room reads you as **clever**, which is a compliment with a lid on
it. people enjoy you, quote you, and are slightly slow to trust you with
anything solemn — not because you aren't deep, but because you show them
the quick thing first, every time, as a reflex. a blaze-rooted moon
usually has one or two people who've seen the other register, and would
be startled how much those people matter to you.

**STATION.05.facing**
you enter fast, mark yourself instantly, and are remembered as *the one
who* — the blaze on the forehead, impossible to confuse with anyone else
in the herd. people know exactly which one you are; whether they know you
is a separate question.

**STATION.05.answer**
across the wheel, **the blaze** answers you — the white mark on the
hunter's brow. once a year the full moon rises there and asks what you'd
go and learn if nobody needed you to come back with a result.

## STATION.06 — The Storm · 參宿

*4°17′–17°09′ Gemini · γ/ξ Geminorum (Alhena) · Arabic al-Han'a, "the
brand" · associated in India with Ārdrā, Rudra's storm-star · guardian:
the gibbon · kept by Mercury · White Tiger*

**STATION.06.strike**
this is one of the places where **the sky-maps genuinely disagree**, and
that turns out to be the reading. the arabs looked at these stars in
gemini and saw *al-han'a* — **the brand**, the mark burned into a camel's
neck to say whose it is. india's storm-star, **ārdrā** — rudra's sky,
the howler, whose emblem is a teardrop — sits nearby in orion, and the
traditions never fully reconciled the two. a mark of belonging, and a
storm. we didn't pick one, because your sun landed in the seam.
the hunger of this sky is to **break the surface of a thing and find out
what it actually cost** — the question nobody at the table wants asked,
asked anyway, usually by you, usually correctly.
the cost is amplitude. you feel at full volume, and full volume is
routinely misfiled as too much. it isn't damage. it's voltage, and
voltage needs somewhere to go or it goes everywhere.

**STATION.06.root**
this sky is guarded by **the gibbon** — the long-armed caller of the
forest canopy, heard across miles before anything sees it — and kept by
**mercury**, the messenger. so your inner life is a voice that carries,
whether or not you meant to broadcast. you work things out by saying
them, and discover what you feel at the same moment the room does.
the room reads you as **intense**, and reaches for that word early. what
it misses is that the intensity is *processing*, not a verdict — you're
not delivering a position, you're thinking out loud at full volume. a
storm-rooted moon spends years learning one sentence — *"i'm not upset,
i'm working"* — and the people who learn to hear the difference tend to
stay for life.

**STATION.06.facing**
you enter at full amplitude and the room adjusts around you within
seconds. some people brace. the ones who don't brace become important to
you very fast.

**STATION.06.answer**
across the wheel, **the storm** answers you — the brand, and the
rain-star nobody could agree about. once a year the full moon lands there
and asks what you've been keeping at a polite volume.

## STATION.07 — The Return · 井宿

*17°09′ Gemini–0°00′ Cancer · Castor and Pollux · Arabic al-Dhirā', "the
forearm" · Punarvasu, "the light that comes back" · guardian: the wild
dog · kept by Jupiter · first station of the Vermilion Bird*

**STATION.07.strike**
your sun landed on **castor and pollux** — the twins, the two bright
heads of gemini, which the arabs read as the outstretched **forearm** of
a much larger sky-figure. the greek story of those two stars is the one
worth knowing: the brothers were half-mortal, and when the mortal one
died the immortal one refused to take eternity alone, so they split it —
alternating, one up while the other is under, forever.
india's name for this sky is **punarvasu** — literally *the goods
returned*, the restoration of what was lost, and usually rendered as the
light that comes back — and its deity is aditi, the boundless mother — the sky of
restoration, of second arrivals. that is your hunger: **you want the
return.** friends who drift back. projects that revive the moment you
touch them. the door you left open on purpose.
the cost is exactly that door. if everything can come back, nothing has
to be finished now — and a life can quietly become a hallway of things
still technically open.

**STATION.07.root**
this sky is guarded by **the wild dog** and kept by **jupiter**, the
biggest planet and the most generous of the seven — and it is the first
station under a new banner, where the white tiger's sky hands over to the
**vermilion bird**. so your inner life runs on pack logic and expansion:
you don't do things alone, and whatever you touch gets larger.
the room reads you as **safe to come back to**, which is the most
undersold thing a person can be. people return to you after silences they
assume they've ruined. the cost is that being the reliable door means you
rarely get to be the one who leaves — and a return-rooted moon should
check, once in a while, that they're staying because they chose to and
not because they're the one everyone else navigates by.

**STATION.07.facing**
you enter like someone coming back — even the first time. people
routinely feel they've met you before, and relax faster around you than
they can explain to themselves.

**STATION.07.answer**
across the wheel, **the return** answers you — the twins who split
eternity so neither would be alone. once a year the full moon rises there
and asks who you'd let back in, if you stopped counting who left.

## Production notes

**~3,000 words; stations 1–7 complete** (strike · root · facing · answer).
Batch 3 picks up at station 8, The Nest.

**Facts used, and where they came from.** Aldebaran genuinely outshines
every star in the Pleiades (the "follower brighter than what it follows"
reading is astronomy, not metaphor); Subaru is the everyday Japanese word
for the Pleiades and the carmaker's badge shows them; al-Thurayyā was
famous enough that unqualified *al-najm*, "the star," meant this cluster;
Castor and Pollux's alternating immortality is the standard Greek myth;
al-Haq'a and Mṛgaśīrṣa independently mark the same stars on Orion's head
(a STRONG match, and rare enough to be worth saying). Station 6's
Arabic/Indian divergence is written *as* the content per the match-flag
rule rather than smoothed over — `mansions-table.json` carries no match
for it, and pretending otherwise would be the dishonest fix.

**Axis rotation held:** strike leads on the Arabic image and lands on the
nakṣatra hunger; root leads on the guardian animal + Keeper luminary and
lands on the mirror (how the room reads you). No station repeats its own
material across the two slots, so a traveler with sun and moon in the
same sky gets two genuinely different readings — which is exactly the
Seedborn case, and it now reads well.

**Verify-pass queue** (before these ship, per house practice): the
Aldebaran-vs-Pleiades magnitude claim, the Subaru badge detail, the
al-najm epithet, the Dioscuri alternation, and the gibbon's call range.
None are load-bearing enough to block drafting; all get checked with the
batch.

**Keeper luminaries used here** come from the now-resolved table
(`hunger-axis.md` §0): Venus, Saturn, Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter —
stations 1–7 run the seven-cycle exactly once, which is a nice
coincidence for the first batch and not true of later ones.
