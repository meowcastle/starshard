## PART 1 — THE JUNGIAN "MIRROR SHARD"

### 1.1 What Jung actually said (and didn't)

Jung never proposed a closed list of 12 archetypes — archetypes were unquantifiable structuring patterns of the collective unconscious, and he warned against fixed lists ([Scott Jeffrey, "Jungian Archetypes"](https://scottjeffrey.com/classic-jungian-archetypes/)). Figures Jung actually named, with Collected Works anchors: Persona (CW 7) · Shadow (Aion, CW 9ii) · Anima/Animus ("The Syzygy," CW 9ii) · Self (CW 9ii; CW 12) · Great Mother (CW 9i) · Father (CW 9i §396) · Child/puer aeternus (CW 9i) · Maiden/Kore (CW 9i) · Hero (CW 9i; CW 5) · Wise Old Man (CW 9i) · Trickster (CW 9i §472). Sources: [Jeffrey's guide PDF](https://scottjeffrey.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Jungian-Archetypes-Guide-Scott-Jeffrey.pdf), [Pacifica Jung LibGuide](https://pacifica.libguides.com/Jung/essays), [Eternalised overview](https://eternalisedofficial.com/2020/09/05/jungian-archetypes-explained/).

### 1.2 Where "the 12 Jungian archetypes" actually comes from

Lineage is **Jung → Carol S. Pearson → Mark & Pearson**; the 12 is entirely Pearson's:

- 1986, *The Hero Within*: six archetypes (Innocent, Orphan, Wanderer, Warrior, Martyr, Magician) ([carolspearson.com](https://carolspearson.com/books-page/the-hero-within-six-archetypes-we-live-by)).
- 1991, *Awakening the Heroes Within*: the canonical 12 — Innocent, Orphan, Warrior, Caregiver, Seeker, Lover, Destroyer, Creator, Ruler, Magician, Sage, Fool — in six chapter pairs across three journey phases ([Spirituality & Practice review](https://www.spiritualityandpractice.com/book-reviews/view/7073/awakening-the-heroes-within), [BookRags guide](https://www.bookrags.com/studyguide-awakening-the-heroes-within/)).
- 2001, Mark & Pearson, *The Hero and the Outlaw*: the brand 12 with renames (Seeker→Explorer, Warrior→Hero, Destroyer→Outlaw, Fool→Jester, Orphan→Everyman) on two axes (Belonging↔Independence, Stability↔Mastery) ([The Brand Archetypes history](https://thebrandarchetypes.com/history/pearson-mark.html), [Jeffrey's wheel](https://scottjeffrey.com/12-brand-archetype-wheel/)).
- 2002–06, **PMAI (Pearson–Marr Archetype Indicator)**: 72-item instrument measuring the 12; **now owned/administered by the Myers & Briggs Foundation via Storywell; PMAI® is a registered trademark — proprietary, paid, licensed** ([Storywell](https://www.storywell.com/about-the-pmai/pearson-and-heroic-archetypes.htm), [Pearson — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_S._Pearson)).

**Copy rule:** "twelve archetypes *in the tradition of* Jungian psychology, as popularized by Carol S. Pearson" — never "Jung's 12 archetypes."

### 1.3 Jung and astrology — record vs. overclaim

Documented ([Astro*Synthesis PDF](https://www.astrosynthesis.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Jung-and-the-Astrological-Timepiece.pdf)): 1911 letter to Freud ("My evenings are taken up very largely with astrology…"); the marriage experiment in *Synchronicity* (CW 8) — 483 couples' charts vs. randomized controls counting traditional marriage aspects; striking first-batch frequencies **leveled to chance** as batches were added; Jung's own conclusion was a synchronistic artifact of the researchers' engaged psychic state, "a secret, mutual connivance" ([Jungian Alchemist account](https://jungianalchemist.substack.com/p/carl-jungs-curious-experiment-to)); 1947 letter to B.V. Raman — astrology as "projected" psychological experience; Pauli objected to the experiments ([Wikipedia, Synchronicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synchronicity)). **Overclaimed in pop sources:** that Jung "proved" astrology or endorsed natal determinism — he did neither. Honest framing: "Jung was fascinated by astrology as a mirror for the psyche — he read charts as symbol, not fate." Scholarly context: [Brill IJJS 2024](https://brill.com/view/journals/ijjs/16/2/article-p175_3.xml).

### 1.4 Keying options — honesty and IP

**IP baseline:** 17 U.S.C. §102(b) — no copyright in systems/methods; names and short phrases unprotectable ([Circular 33 PDF](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf)). Protected: Pearson's descriptive prose (paraphrase, never lift); **PMAI®** trademark and instrument (never reproduce items/scoring/name); passing-off (don't imply endorsement).

| Option | Honesty | IP | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Keep moon-sign→archetype, relabeled | Weak–fair: mapping is invented | fine with own names/prose | acceptable floor only |
| (b) **Sun-element × Moon-element (16 combos)** | Better: elements are load-bearing in astrology; sun/moon rhymes with persona/anima; 16 ≠ 12 forces original names | strongest — an original 16-cell system | **recommended primary key** |
| (c) Moon's house (12) | honest as astrology but requires birth time; house-system disputes | fine | optional overlay for time-known charts |
| (d) Short original quiz | most honest psychologically (self-report is Pearson's own method) | safe if fully original; never shadow the PMAI | **recommended secondary** — "check the mirror" |

Recommended architecture: default from (b), quiz as (d), with disagreement as charm: *"your stars say Hearthkeeper, your heart says Wayfinder — Jung would say the mirror you argue with is the interesting one."*

### 1.5 Depth schema: Light / Shadow / Growth

Every entry: **Light** (the gift), **Shadow** (the same energy over-rotated — the unlived side, not evil), **Growth** (individuation: integrate, don't delete). Pearson's chapters model this (goal, fear, task, gift, shadow, development levels). **Family-friendly shadow language:** the VIA "golden mean" / strength-overuse framing — struggles as imbalanced strengths, non-pathologizing ([VIA overuse article](https://www.viacharacter.org/topics/articles/the-overuse-underuse-and-optimal-use-of-character-strengths), [Niemiec 2019](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09515070.2019.1617674)). Product term: the **"flicker"** — the gift turned up too far, always followed by the growth line.

### 1.6 Worked examples

**CAREGIVER** (Pearson: goal = help others; fear = selfishness; gift = compassion). Light: giving from abundance and choice. Flicker: the martyr-shadow — "giving compulsively while secretly resenting it" ([Selfgazer/Pearson](https://www.selfgazer.com/blog/pearson-archetype-the-caregiver)); kid-safe: *"Sometimes you give away your whole sandwich and then feel grumbly. That grumble isn't mean — it's your inner Caregiver asking to be cared for too."* Growth: put your own name on the list; one small honest "no" a week.

**SEEKER** (goal = the truer life; fear = entrapment; gift = authenticity). Light: "naturally independent, authentic, curious" ([carolspearson.com](https://carolspearson.com/archetype-pages/seeker-archetype)). Flicker: "always searching, never finding — exploration as avoidance of commitment" ([Selfgazer](https://www.selfgazer.com/blog/pearson-archetype-the-seeker)); kid-safe: *"Ten new hobbies, zero finished projects? Some treasure only appears if you stay long enough to dig."* Growth: pick one quest this season; bring a friend.

## PART 2 — THE FOLK "HEARTH SHARD"

### 2.1 Monday's Child — real history

First printed in A.E. Bray's *Traditions of Devonshire* — 1838 per the Opie tradition ([Interesting Literature](https://interestingliterature.com/2023/05/mondays-child-fair-of-face-poem-summary-analysis/)); Wikipedia renders 1836 — safest copy: "first written down in Devon in the 1830s" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monday%27s_Child)). Tradition older: Thomas Nashe recalled 1570s Suffolk birthday-luck telling. **Variants:** an 1887 Harper's Weekly version makes *Friday's* child full of woe and the Sabbath child "happy and wise"; an 1893 novel makes Wednesday loving; a NE-England variant is savage to Friday. **Product move: never doom Wednesday kids — show the variant map and let users pick their lineage.** Folklore's plasticity IS the content.

### 2.2 Planetary weekday rulers — mechanics + multilingual table

### 2.3 Rokuyō (六曜) — verified

Six days in cycle order ([Unseen Japan](https://unseen-japan.com/rokuyo-lunar-calendar-marriage-days/), [SNG](https://www.sng.ac.jp/en/sng-news/the-rokuyo-%E5%85%AD%E6%9B%9C-traditional-calendar-of-fortune/)): 先勝 Senshō (lucky morning) · 友引 Tomobiki (good day; funerals avoided — "pulls friends along") · 先負 Senbu (lucky afternoon) · 仏滅 Butsumetsu (unluckiest; originally 物滅 — also read as "clean slate") · 大安 Taian (luckiest; the wedding day) · 赤口 Shakkō (unlucky except 11–1).

**Computation:** `(lunar month + lunar day) mod 6` → 0 Taian, 1 Shakkō, 2 Senshō, 3 Tomobiki, 4 Senbu, 5 Butsumetsu; sequence resets each lunar month (hence irregular jumps on Gregorian calendars). Needs a Gregorian→lunisolar conversion ([open-source example](https://github.com/massn/taian)).

**Live practice:** 2015 poll — 52.5% consider rokuyō for events; weddings cluster on Taian (22.8%); venues discount Butsumetsu; crematoria close on Tomobiki; Sony launches on Taian. **Include as "the weather of your arrival day," never birth-fate** — rokuyō is a day-quality system, not a natal one; note it's living practice; never play Butsumetsu for doom.

### 2.4 Folk inventory (expansion priorities)

1. **Planetary day-ruler + multilingual names + Monday's Child variant map** — deepest documentation, upgrades existing shard in place.
2. **Chinese zodiac + East Asian variants** — biggest audience win: Vietnam swaps rabbit→**cat** (likely 卯 mǎo / *mèo* homophony), Japan reads pig as boar; lunar vs. Feb-4 solar-term New Year boundary affects Jan/Feb birthdays ([Vietnamese zodiac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_zodiac), [regional variations](https://chinesezodiac.com/learn/regional-variations)).
3. **Rokuyō** (above) + **birthstones** — the layered true history (Aaron's breastplate → Josephus/Jerome → Polish/German custom → **standardized by the American National Jewelers' Association, Kansas City, 1912**; tanzanite 2002, spinel 2016) is delightful honesty ([Gemporia history](https://www.gemporia.com/en-gb/article-hub/article/630/a-history-of-birthstones-and-the-breastplate-of-aaron/)).
4. **Name days** — living tradition rivaling birthdays in Greece/Hungary/Poland/Latvia ([CalendarZ](https://www.calendarz.com/blog/name-days-around-the-world)).
5. **Birth flowers** (Victorian backfill — garnish only) + **golden birthday** (coined by Joan Bramsch, 1955 — charming one-liner) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Bramsch)).

### 2.5 Presenting living traditions without flattening

- **UNESCO ICH framework** (2003 Convention; 2015 Ethical Principles): the four failure modes — over-commercialization, decontextualization, misappropriation, misrepresentation ([UNESCO guidance PDF](https://ich.unesco.org/doc/src/68297-EN.pdf), [Ethical Principles](https://ich.unesco.org/en/background-of-the-ethical-principles-00867)).
- Folklore-in-media ethics: research thoroughly, attribute and contextualize, represent **diversity within traditions** rather than a flattened standard version ([Fiveable ethics guide](https://fiveable.me/folklore-and-visual-culture/unit-11/ethical-considerations-folklore-media/study-guide/EkNa3ztyaHIBMYsq)).

**Operating rules:** (1) label every entry with where/when/whether still practiced — "still printed on Japanese calendars today" does more respect-work than any disclaimer; (2) show variation as a feature; (3) never grade cultures' beliefs; (4) culture-native review for living systems; (5) "tradition says…" reports, not claims — which matches the product's Jung framing: mirrors and stories, not fate.
