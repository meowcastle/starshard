# Manzil: how to play, and what the words mean

**14 September 2026. Measurement → Design, a draft to ship from.** Everything below is the game as
it stands on the reference and in Code's 13 Sep copy plus the two decisions of 14 Sep (dawn/duel,
the walker leader rule). Player-facing names are in **bold**; names that are ours and should not
reach a player are marked *(internal)*. Design owns the voice; this is the content.

---

## 1. The game in one paragraph

Manzil is a card game played on a **road** of nine **stations** under one of the twenty-eight
**mansions** of the moon. Two players, seven cards each, take turns placing a card on an empty
station. A card has two numbers, one facing each way along the road; when it lands it fights its
neighbours, and a bigger or equal number **takes** the neighbour for its side. When the road is
full, the **cards each side still holds** are shown and fight in the sky. Whoever holds more
stations, counting the sky, wins the board. Each night the mansion the moon stands in adds one
**law** to the road, and each night is a climb of nine matches to that mansion's door.

## 2. The board

- **The road.** Nine stations in a line. The left end is the **door** (the first station, where
  the night's mansion usually stands); the right end is the **far end**; the fifth station is the
  **crossing** (the middle). The two beside the crossing are its **flanks**.
- **Ground.** Each station stands on a mansion of the sky, nine in a row. Tonight's mansion is
  the door unless the night **slides** so that it stands on the crossing; either way the road
  reads left to right along the calendar. The ground matters for two things: a card standing on
  its own mansion has **dominion** and counts one more; and a few laws read the ground's quarter.
- **Hands.** Seven cards each, dealt from your twenty-eight. The road takes nine, so the leader
  places five and holds two; the follower places four and holds three.

## 3. A turn

1. **Lodge** a card from your hand on any empty station. A **two-faced** card may be lodged either
   way round.
2. The card **strikes** its neighbours, both sides at once. Your number facing a neighbour against
   its number facing you: **bigger or equal takes**. A taken card changes sides and stays where
   it is, faces unchanged.
3. Some cards do something extra when they land or when they are struck (their **signature**, see
   the table). The night's law may add a step. Then the other player goes.

There is no chain: a taken card does not strike onward unless a signature or law says so.

## 4. The count

When the ninth station fills, the board ends.

- Every station counts one for whoever holds it. **Dominion** (your card on its own mansion) counts
  one more. A few signatures and laws change what a station is worth.
- **Dawn.** Both players show the cards they still hold. Strongest against strongest, by the two
  printed numbers added together, each pairing is worth one station to the higher card; equal
  cards score nobody; a card with no opponent scores nothing. The follower always holds one more
  card than the leader, so the follower always has one card that does not fight.
- Higher total wins. **A level board goes to the answerer**, the player who did not lead it.

## 5. The night's law

Every mansion has one **law**, a sentence that changes one station on its own night, usually the
crossing. Some nights carry no law: the road is played as printed. The law is shown at the start
of the night and is the same for both players. The laws are listed in section 9.

## 6. The climb

A night is a **climb** of nine **rungs** up the road to the mansion.

- **Rungs one to four** are single boards against **walkers**. **Five to eight** are best of three.
  The ninth is the **mansion match**, best of five, against the mansion itself.
- You carry **three lanterns**. Losing a rung puts one out; the rung is played again. When the
  third goes out the climb ends. Clear the mansion match and the night is yours.
- **Who leads.** You lead the first board of every walker rung; after that, the loser of a board
  leads the next. The mansion leads its own first board.
- **The storm (mansion 6)** is the hardest road: two lanterns, every rung best of three, no
  handicap, the walkers more careful than usual.

The moon stands in one mansion each day. The game opens on that mansion; the others are reachable
from the ring while we test.

## 7. Cards, levels, quarters

Twenty-eight cards, one for each mansion, seven in each of the four **quarters** of the sky. A
card grows through four **levels** as you walk its road:

| level | what wakes |
|---|---|
| 1 | its two numbers only |
| 2 | its **signature** (the card's own verb) |
| 3 | its quarter's **grant** |
| 4 | one number one higher |

A card at level two or above is **awake**.

**The four grants**

| quarter | animal | cards | grant |
|---|---|---|---|
| byakko | the white tiger | 1–6, 28 | **cannot be taken** |
| suzaku | the vermilion bird | 7–13 | its strikes **carry two stations** (at the far one it fights with its printed number) |
| seiryuu | the azure dragon | 14–20 | **two-faced**: may be lodged either way round |
| genbu | the black tortoise | 21–27 | once taken, its station **counts for nobody**, both sides, to the end of the board |

## 10. Glossary

**answerer** · the player who did not lead the board; wins a level board. Also *the defender*
*(internal)*.
**awake** · a card at level two or higher.
**bigger or equal takes** · the one rule of striking.
**climb** · a night's nine rungs to the mansion.
**count** · the tally at the end of a board: stations held, plus dawn.
**crossing** · the fifth station, the middle of the road. Most laws stand here.
**dawn** · when the road fills, the held cards are shown and fight in the sky.
**dominion** · a card standing on its own mansion counts one more.
**door** · the first station, the left end. Where tonight's mansion usually stands.
**face** · one of a card's two numbers. *Printed* means as on the card, before any mark or turn.
**far end** · the ninth station.
**flanks** · the two stations beside the crossing.
**grant** · the power every card of a quarter shares, waking at level three.
**ground** · the mansion a station stands on tonight.
**handicap** *(internal)* · on walker boards the opponent's mirror has some of the player's awake
cards asleep; fewer as the collection grows.
**held cards** · the cards still in hand when the road fills; they fight at dawn.
**held / cannot be taken** · sheltered by a grant, a signature, or a law; the strike misses.
**lantern** · a life on the climb. Three a night; two on the storm.
**law** · the one sentence a mansion adds to the road on its own night.
**leader** · the player who places the first card of a board. *Loser leads* the next board of a
series; the player leads the first board of every walker rung.
**level** · a card's growth, one to four; one level per road walked.
**lodge** · to place a card on a station.
**mansion** · one of the twenty-eight houses of the moon; also the card that bears its name, and
the night that belongs to it.
**mansion match** · the ninth rung, best of five, against the mansion.
**mark** · a permanent change to a card's numbers (the blaze, the hand, the throne).
**mirror** *(internal)* · the walker opponent: a copy of the player's own collection.
**planets** · the mansion's own hand at the ninth rung: the seven wanderers of the sky. Quarterless;
no grant, no dominion.
**quarter** · one of the four animals of the sky and its seven cards.
**road** · the nine stations.
**rung** · one match of the climb.
**signature** · a card's own verb, waking at level two.
**slide** *(internal: "slid")* · a night whose road is set so the mansion stands on the crossing
rather than the door.
**station** · one of the nine places on the road.
**strike** · what a card does to its neighbours when it lands.
**take** · to win a neighbour to your side with a bigger or equal number.
**the sky / she** · the opponent: walkers on the way up, the mansion at the top.
**turn (a card)** · to swap its two faces.
**two-faced** · a dragon card at level three: may be lodged either way round.
**walker** · the opponent at rungs one to eight.
**window** *(internal)* · which nine mansions the road stands on tonight.

*Not in the game, in the app:* **tārābala** (the day's standing between the moon's mansion and the
player's birth mansion), **the hungers**, and **the reading** belong to the astrology side and are
defined there.
