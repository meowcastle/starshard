# PART 1 — Manāzil al-Qamar: The Classical Arabic Tradition

## 1.1 Primary sources and what each contains

**Ibn Qutaybah (828–884/889 CE), Kitāb al-Anwāʾ.** A Baghdad philologist (not an astronomer), Ibn Qutaybah compiled the single most important record of *native* Arab star lore, deliberately excluding foreign (Greek/Indian) science. The book contains: an 84-page description of the 28 lunar mansions with adjacent stars; the meteorological traditions of each mansion; Bedouin astrometeorological knowledge of desert seasons; zodiac signs, Milky Way, planets; risings/settings and stellar navigation; and rain-prediction lore — all documented through pre-Islamic poetry citations ([Encyclopedia.com — Ibn Qutayba](https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/ibn-qutayba-abu-mu)). The technical term *nawʾ* (pl. *anwāʾ*) means the dawn setting of one mansion while its opposite rises heliacally — the moment believed to govern that period's rain.

**Al-Bīrūnī (973–1048), Kitāb al-Tafhīm ("Book of Instruction in the Elements of the Art of Astrology," 1029, trans. R. Ramsay Wright 1934).** The standard reference list of the 28 mansions with their determining stars. Critically for your product: al-Bīrūnī's presentation anchors the mansion cycle to the **vernal equinox** (a tropical anchor), even while defining mansions by stars — an internal tension that later writers (Ibn ʿArabī tropical; Abraham ibn Ezra sidereal) resolved in different directions ([Şira Nur Uysal, "Lunar Mansions"](https://sirauysal.com/en/blog/lunar-mansions); [Wright translation, Cambridge review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/44978F5B82155F08EAB3E539BAC9ECA8/S0035869X00084446a.pdf/elements-of-astrology-by-abul-rayhan-al-biruni-the-book-of-instruction-in-the-elements-of-the-art-of-astrology-reproduced-from-brit-mus-ms-or-8349-with-a-translation-by-r-ramsay-wright-8-6-pp-xviii-33.pdf); [full scan at skyscript](https://www.skyscript.co.uk/albiruni_elements.html)). Al-Bīrūnī gives astronomical positions, not fortune-telling meanings. The Tafhīm is inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World-adjacent Silk Road registry ([UNESCO](https://en.unesco.org/silkroad/silk-road-themes/documentary-heritage/al-tafhim-li-awail-sanaat-al-tanjim)).

**The anwāʾ literature generally.** A whole genre: Abū Ḥanīfa al-Dīnawarī and al-Naḍr ibn Shumayl wrote Kitāb al-Anwāʾ works; ʿArīb ibn Saʿīd's calendar (973, the "Calendar of Córdoba," Latinized as *Liber anoe*) merged anwāʾ lore with agronomy ([Wikipedia — Kitab al-Anwa'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitab_al-Anwa%27)); Aḥmad ibn Fāris wrote a *Mukhtaṣar min al-anwāʾ* ([academia.edu](https://academia.edu/9697425/Astrology_and_Folk_Astronomy_the_Mukhtasar_min_al-anwa_of_Ahmad_ibn_Faris)). The key modern scholars are **Daniel Martin Varisco** (*Medieval Folk Astronomy and Agriculture in Arabia and the Yemen*; "Islamic Folk Astronomy") ([Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/Medieval_Folk_Astronomy_and_Agriculture.html?id=b08SAQAAIAAJ); [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-94-011-4179-6_21); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/279378884_Islamic_Folk_Astronomy); [his blog](https://tabsir.net/?p=1075)) and **Danielle Adams**, whose 2018 Arizona dissertation *Rain Stars Set, Lunar Stations Rise* argues that the neat 28-mansion grid was a later systematization that "flattened" a richer, messier indigenous star lore of rain stars and rhymed sayings ([repository.arizona.edu](https://repository.arizona.edu/handle/10150/630173?show=full); her public-facing project is [Two Deserts, One Sky](http://onesky.arizona.edu/about-two-deserts-one-sky/) — note: this site was unreachable through my proxy; cited from search records). Also relevant: al-Būnī's *Shams al-Maʿārif* treats the mansions in letter-mysticism ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342151641_Illuminating_the_Lunar_Stations_manazil_al-qamar_in_al-Buni's_Shams_al-ma'arif)), and Ibn ʿArabī's *Futūḥāt* maps the 28 mansions to the 28 Arabic letters and Divine Names on a tropical "starless sphere" ([Uysal](https://sirauysal.com/en/blog/lunar-mansions)).

**Picatrix (Ghāyat al-Ḥakīm, ~10th–11th c.).** Book IV ch. 9 (drawing on an "Indian" source) gives each mansion a talismanic purpose, image, materials, suffumigation, invocation, and ruling spirit name ([Renaissance Astrology — Picatrix mansion list](https://www.renaissanceastrology.com/mansionslistpicatrix.html); spirit-name detail also at [Moonlit Hermit](http://moonlithermit.blogspot.com/2015/10/the-picatrix-spirits-of-mansions-of-moon.html)). **Product flag, stated plainly: Picatrix is a manual of astral magic.** Its mansion indications include cursing, binding, separating spouses, and destroying enemies. In a family-friendly fandom product this material must be presented as *history of ideas* ("medieval magicians believed…"), never as usable practice — this is both an ethics/brand-safety issue and a religious-sensitivity issue (talismanic magic is *siḥr*, categorically condemned in Islam). Agrippa's *Three Books of Occult Philosophy* II.33 (1533) transmitted a cleaned-up version of the same list to Europe ([Esoteric Archives — Agrippa Book II](https://www.esotericarchives.com/agrippa/agripp2c.htm)).

**Electional source:** ʿAlī ibn Abī al-Rijāl ("Abenragel," c. 1000) adapted Dorothean sign-based lunar elections to the mansions, preserving "Indian" per-mansion judgments (good/bad for marriage, travel, trade…) — tabulated at [yeatsvision.com/Mansions.html](https://www.yeatsvision.com/Mansions.html) ([Scribd transcription](https://www.scribd.com/document/842721787/The-Mansions-of-the-Moon-according-to-Abenragel)).

## 1.2 How the mansions were actually used

Four distinct practitioner modes — worth keeping separate in your content:

1. **Solar/agricultural calendar (the anwāʾ proper).** Not about the Moon at all: the *Sun* passes through one mansion every ~13 days (al-Jabha gets 14, making 27×13+14=365), and the pre-dawn *heliacal rising* of a mansion's stars — with the *dawn setting* (nawʾ) of the opposite mansion — marked seasons, rain periods, planting, jam-making, even perfume-making. At any moment 14 mansions are visible, 14 hidden ([Muslim Heritage](https://muslimheritage.com/stellar-lunar-keys-medieval-mus-agriculture/)). Knowledge circulated as rhymed sayings (sajʿ) — see table below ([Al Jazeera Arabic, "The lunar mansions and their rhymed prose"](https://www.aljazeera.net/tech/2016/2/28/%D9%85%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%B2%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D9%85%D8%B1-%D9%88%D9%85%D8%A7-%D9%82%D9%8A%D9%84-%D9%81%D9%8A%D9%87%D8%A7-%D9%85%D9%86-%D8%B3%D8%AC%D8%B9)). This tradition is *alive* in the Gulf: Saudi, Omani and Qatari media still run seasonal anwāʾ commentary ([al-Marsd on Saʿd al-Suʿūd weather](https://al-marsd.com/article/23692/%D8%A7%D8%AE%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A9/%22%D8%A5%D8%B0%D8%A7-%D8%B7%D9%84%D8%B9-%D8%B3%D8%B9%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%B9%D9%88%D8%AF-%D9%83%D9%8F%D8%B1%D9%87-%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D9%85%D8%B3-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%B9%D9%88%D8%AF%22..-%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%81%D9%8A%D8%AF%D9%8A%D9%88:-%22%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B2%D8%B9%D8%A7%D9%82%22-%D9%8A%D9%83%D8%B4%D9%81-%D8%B9%D9%86-%D8%A3%D8%AC%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%85%D9%84%D9%83%D8%A9-%D8%AE%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D9%8A%D8%A7%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%AF%D9%85%D8%A9); [Oman Daily on Saʿd al-Suʿūd](https://www.omandaily.om/%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B6%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%A6%D9%85/na/%D9%86%D8%AC%D9%85-%D8%B3%D8%B9%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%B9%D9%88%D8%AF)).
2. **Electional astrology (the Moon's daily station).** The Moon moves ~13°/day, i.e., one mansion per day. "The Moon is in mansion X today" told a practitioner *what today is good for*: journeys under mansions 1, 5, 11, 13, 21, 28; avoid travel under 9, 15, 16; marry under 3, 14, 27 but not 1; trade under 2, 3, 10, 14; build under 10, 20, 21 ([Uysal](https://sirauysal.com/en/blog/lunar-mansions); [Renaissance Astrology](https://www.renaissanceastrology.com/mansionsmoon.html)). This — not natal reading — was the mansions' core astrological use; natal mansion interpretation is rare (the 13th-c. Alfonsine *Astromagia* is the noted exception; [Uysal](https://sirauysal.com/en/blog/lunar-mansions)). Your product's "birth mansion" concept is therefore a modern extension, closer to Indian/Japanese practice than to Arabic.
3. **Talismanic election (Picatrix):** wait for the Moon in the right mansion, in good condition (not afflicted by Saturn/Mars, not combust), then make the talisman ([Renaissance Astrology](https://www.renaissanceastrology.com/mansionslistpicatrix.html)).
4. **Timekeeping:** counting mansions crossing the meridian gave the hour of night; al-Judhāmī's 13th-c. poem timed the dawn prayer by mansion culminations to within ±6 minutes ([Suhayl 8 (2008), "Lunar mansions and Timekeeping in Western Islam"](https://www.ub.edu/arab/suhayl/volums/volum8/paper%204.pdf)). This paper also confirms the mansions were treated by astronomers as **unequal star groups** (1–3 stars each), not equal segments.

## 1.4 Cultural sensitivity

Findings, in order of importance:

1. **The sharpest live sensitivity is religious, not ethnic.** Mainstream Islamic theology explicitly condemns two things your product touches: attributing rain/fortune to stars — the Prophet's ḥadīth qudsī declares that saying "we were given rain by such-and-such a star" is a form of disbelief ([Sunnah.com, Hadith Qudsi 3](https://sunnah.com/qudsi40:3)) — and astrology as divination generally ([hadith "whoever has faith in the stars…"](https://www.abuaminaelias.com/dailyhadithonline/2012/07/14/astrology-is-kufr/); [IslamWeb on the sharia stance](https://www.islamweb.net/en/article/213193/sharia-stance-on-astrology-ii)). Ibn Qutaybah himself was a religious scholar documenting the lore *as language and heritage*, not endorsing star-causation. So do not label the feature "Islamic astrology," and do not imply Muslims practice or endorse mansion divination.
2. **Your current disclaimer is a good start but incomplete.** "Shared as cultural scholarship with love… not as religious guidance" should be extended to state affirmatively: (a) this is *historical* material from the Arabic-Islamic scientific and poetic heritage; (b) mainstream Islamic scholarship rejects astrological divination, and the presentation does not represent Islam or Muslim practice; (c) Picatrix material is described as history of medieval magic, not offered for use; (d) name your sources (Ibn Qutaybah, al-Bīrūnī, modern scholars). Scholarly and heritage institutions (Muslim Heritage Foundation, UNESCO's listing of al-Tafhīm) model exactly this "science-and-heritage" framing ([Muslim Heritage](https://muslimheritage.com/stellar-lunar-keys-medieval-mus-agriculture/); [UNESCO](https://en.unesco.org/silkroad/silk-road-themes/documentary-heritage/al-tafhim-li-awail-sanaat-al-tanjim)).
3. **This is a living tradition, not a dead one.** Gulf almanac culture still tracks the manāzil for weather and agriculture ([al-Marsd](https://al-marsd.com/article/23692/%D8%A7%D8%AE%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A9/%22%D8%A5%D8%B0%D8%A7-%D8%B7%D9%84%D8%B9-%D8%B3%D8%B9%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%B9%D9%88%D8%AF-%D9%83%D9%8F%D8%B1%D9%87-%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D9%85%D8%B3-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%B9%D9%88%D8%AF%22..-%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%81%D9%8A%D8%AF%D9%8A%D9%88:-%22%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B2%D8%B9%D8%A7%D9%82%22-%D9%8A%D9%83%D8%B4%D9%81-%D8%B9%D9%86-%D8%A3%D8%AC%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%85%D9%84%D9%83%D8%A9-%D8%AE%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D9%8A%D8%A7%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%AF%D9%85%D8%A9); [Oman Daily](https://www.omandaily.om/%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B6%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%A6%D9%85/na/%D9%86%D8%AC%D9%85-%D8%B3%D8%B9%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%B9%D9%88%D8%AF)). Presenting the sayings with dates and agricultural meaning (rather than as "ancient mystic secrets") is the respectful register, and Adams' scholarship warns against flattening the folk material into a tidy fortune-wheel ([Adams dissertation](https://repository.arizona.edu/handle/10150/630173?show=full)) — worth acknowledging in an "about" page.
4. **No specific controversy found** about fandom/commercial commodification of the manāzil (I searched; nothing surfaced beyond general appropriation debates in Western occultism). The realistic risk is mislabeling (calling it "Islamic," implying religious endorsement, or serving Picatrix curses as content), not the use of the star lore itself, which has always circulated across cultures (Latin Europe, Yeats, etc. — [yeatsvision](https://www.yeatsvision.com/Mansions.html)). Practical steps: correct transliteration with ʿayn/macron handling, Arabic script alongside, credit named scholars, and route "is this religious?" questions to the disclaimer.

# PART 2 — The Cross-Cultural Hypothesis

## 2.1 The Chinese 28 xiù — VERIFIED, with caveats

Structure confirmed: four quadrant-spirits × seven mansions — Azure Dragon (East/Spring), Black Tortoise (North/Winter), White Tiger (West/Autumn), Vermilion Bird (South/Summer) ([Wikipedia — Twenty-Eight Mansions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-Eight_Mansions)). Full list with determinative stars ([Calendar Wiki table](https://calendars.fandom.com/wiki/Twenty-Eight_Mansions)); each xiù also carries an animal and one of the seven luminaries (Sun, Moon, and the five planets — so the 28 = 4×7 cycle meshes with the 7-day week), used "to calculate a person's fortune and luck" ([HandWiki mirror with tables](https://handwiki.org/wiki/Astronomy:Twenty-Eight_Mansions)). The 28 animals (二十八禽): East — flood-dragon, dragon, raccoon-dog, rabbit, fox, tiger, leopard; North — xiezhi, ox, bat, rat, swallow, pig, yayu; West — wolf, dog, pheasant, rooster, crow, monkey, ape; South — bian, goat, river-deer, horse, deer, snake, earthworm ([HandWiki](https://handwiki.org/wiki/Astronomy:Twenty-Eight_Mansions)). Day-selection use is alive in the Tong Shu almanac tradition: each date carries a presiding mansion with an "activity register" (e.g., Jiǎo days favorable for inaugurations and marriages), integrated with the 60-jiazi cycle and BaZi ([Master Sean Chan](https://www.masterseanchan.com/auspicious-28-mansions/); personality-oriented modern treatment: [Benebell Wen](https://benebellwen.com/2023/05/14/chinese-lunar-mansions-oracle/), [her reference PDF](https://benebellwen.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/the-28-lunar-mansions-chinese-astrology.pdf)). Note the animal iconography standardized only ~11th century CE — it is medieval Daoist elaboration, not ancient astronomy ([Duke UP, "Zoomorphizing the Asterisms"](https://read.dukeupress.edu/sungkyun-journal-of-east-asian-studies/article/22/1/1/299045/Zoomorphizing-the-Asterisms-Indigenous)).

**Origin debate, honestly stated:** the xiù are attested in China before the mid-5th century BCE ([Duke UP](https://read.dukeupress.edu/sungkyun-journal-of-east-asian-studies/article/22/1/1/299045/Zoomorphizing-the-Asterisms-Indigenous)); nakshatras appear in Vedic texts of comparable or greater antiquity; 19th-century scholars fought a three-way war (Biot: China; Weber: India; Hommel: Babylon) that remains unresolved. Wikipedia's sourced summary: lunar groupings existed in Babylon by the 6th c. BCE, but the specific 28-scheme derivation is contested, and surviving nakshatra lists "seem to betray transmission through Greek sources" (Tester) ([Wikipedia — Lunar station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_station)); recent sinology argues for independent Chinese development "based on its notable idiosyncrasies," against the older Babylonian-diffusion consensus ([Duke UP](https://read.dukeupress.edu/sungkyun-journal-of-east-asian-studies/article/22/1/1/299045/Zoomorphizing-the-Asterisms-Indigenous)). The *Arabic* system, by contrast, is generally agreed to be the youngest of the three and shaped by contact (the pre-Islamic anwāʾ lore is native; the tidy 28-mansion grid shows Indian influence — [Adams](https://repository.arizona.edu/handle/10150/630173?show=full); [Uysal](https://sirauysal.com/en/blog/lunar-mansions)). **Product copy should say "three ancient cultures each mapped the Moon's path through 27–28 star-stations — whether by shared inheritance or parallel invention is still debated," and no stronger.**

## 2.2 Indian nakshatras and tārābala — VERIFIED; mechanics exact

**27 vs 28:** the working system is 27 equal sidereal divisions of 13°20′ from 0° sidereal Aries (Ashvinī), 4 pādas of 3°20′ each (27×4=108). The 28th, **Abhijit** (Vega), is the older intercalary station, squeezed between Uttara Āṣāḍhā and the first quarter of Śravaṇa and dropped from the equal scheme for mathematical clarity ([Wikipedia — Nakshatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakshatra)). Full deity/symbol/star table extracted (all 27: Ashvinī/Aśvins/horse's head/β-γ Ari; Bharaṇī/Yama/yoni; Kṛttikā/Agni/blade/Pleiades; Rohiṇī/Brahmā/chariot/Aldebaran; … Revatī/Pūṣan/fish/ζ Psc) — same URL; I hold the complete table for the DB build.

**Tārābala — the exact algorithm** (cross-verified against two independent sources; they agree):

```
birth_star  = janma nakshatra index (1–27, from sidereal Moon at birth)
today_star  = today's nakshatra index (1–27, from sidereal Moon now)
count       = ((today_star - birth_star) mod 27) + 1   // inclusive count, janma = 1
tara        = count mod 9;  if tara == 0 then tara = 9
```

The nine tārās, in fixed order, with verdicts ([VedAstro/Raman's Muhurtha](https://vedastro.org/blog/Muhurtha-Chapter-3-The-Birth-Star-and-the-Birth-Moon.html); [PanchangBodh](https://panchangbodh.com/tara-bala)):

| tara | Name | Meaning | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Janma | one's own birth-star | Mixed: fine for foundational acts (marriage rites, ritual, agriculture, education); avoid for conflict, travel, medical treatment |
| 2 | Sampat | wealth | Favorable |
| 3 | Vipat | loss, danger | Unfavorable |
| 4 | Kṣema | well-being, security | Favorable |
| 5 | Pratyak (Pratyari) | obstacles, the adversary | Unfavorable |
| 6 | Sādhana (Sādhaka) | accomplishment | Favorable |
| 7 | Naidhana (Vadha) | "death-like" — worst | Unfavorable (strongest) |
| 8 | Mitra | friend | Favorable |
| 9 | Parama Mitra | best friend | Most favorable |

Refinements you must implement for correctness: the **three paryāya (cycle) rule** — the nine repeat three times around the 27 wheel (counts 1–9, 10–18, 19–27), and the malefic intensity *decreases* in the second and third cycles; Raman still advises avoiding Vipat and Naidhana in any cycle for major undertakings. Remedial tradition also allows skipping only the first ghaṭīs of a bad tārā for urgent matters ([VedAstro](https://vedastro.org/blog/Muhurtha-Chapter-3-The-Birth-Star-and-the-Birth-Moon.html)). Tārābala is traditionally weighed together with **Chandrabala** (Moon not in the 6th/8th/12th sign from natal Moon) — cheap to compute and a good second signal for a daily card ([VedAstro](https://vedastro.org/blog/Muhurtha-Chapter-3-The-Birth-Star-and-the-Birth-Moon.html)). Practitioners consult it daily via panchāṅga apps for travel, purchases, and launches ([PanchangBodh](https://panchangbodh.com/tara-bala); [tpanchang](http://tpanchang.blogspot.com/2016/04/taarabalm-how-to-calculate-tarabalam.html)).

**Engine warning:** tārābala's arithmetic *requires 27* (27 = 3×9; the wheel closes exactly). It cannot be run on your 28 tropical mansions without breaking the cycle (28 mod 9 ≠ 0 — the same tara would drift every revolution). If the daily card uses tārābala, compute it on a separate sidereal-27 track (standard Lahiri ayanāṁśa) behind the scenes, and keep the 28-mansion display layer separate. This is also more honest: it keeps each tradition's mechanics intact.

## 2.3 Japanese Sukuyōdō — VERIFIED, with one correction to the hypothesis

**History (scholarly).** Amoghavajra compiled the *Xiuyao jing* (宿曜経, "Sutra of Lodges and Luminaries," 759/764 CE) in Tang China from Indian Buddhist astrology; Kūkai carried it to Japan in 806, bringing the seven-day week with it; from the late 10th century a dedicated lineage of monk-astrologers (sukuyōshi) emerged — first documented: Hōzō (905–969), who innovated by determining the natal mansion from the Moon's *actual observed* nakshatra at birth rather than nominal calendar tables. Sukuyōshi cast full horoscopes (with Hellenistic-derived techniques — triplicities, profections), performed astral magic to planetary and mansion deities (e.g., the Hokuto/Big Dipper rite), and competed with Onmyōdō court diviners — the 961/963 dispute between Hōzō and the onmyōji Kamo no Yasunori over Emperor Murakami's natal star is emblematic. The tradition collapsed after its head temple (Hokutokōrin-in, Kyoto, est. 1165) burned in 1417: "Sukuyōdō as an identifiable community vanished around this time" ([Kotyk, *Japanese Buddhist Astrology and Astral Magic: Mikkyō and Sukuyōdō*, JJRS 45/1](https://archive.org/stream/JJRS451Kotyk/JJRS%2045-1%20Kotyk_djvu.txt); [Japanese Wiki Corpus — Sukuyōdō](https://www.japanesewiki.com/Buddhism/Sukuyodo%20(a%20type%20of%20astrology).html); further: [Kotyk's Tang dissertation](https://www.academia.edu/35484261/Buddhist_Astrology_and_Astral_Magic_in_the_Tang_Dynasty_PhD_Dissertation_), [MDPI Religions on early Mikkyō astronomy](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/13/5/458), [Buddhistdoor interview](https://www.buddhistdoor.net/features/understanding-buddhist-astrology-an-interview-with-dr-jeffrey-kotyk/)). Heian-lit anchor: in the *Tale of Genji*'s first chapter a sukuyō practitioner divines the newborn Genji's destiny ([Japanese Wiki Corpus](https://www.japanesewiki.com/Buddhism/Sukuyodo%20(a%20type%20of%20astrology).html)). The 28 mansions also live in Japanese Buddhist art as deities in the Womb World Mandala (seven celestial maidens per quarter) and Hokuto mandalas — with the Ox mansion (牛宿, = Abhijit) standing apart, the fossil of the 27/28 problem ([JAANUS — nijūhasshuku](https://projects.mcah.columbia.edu/jaanus/node/2129)).

**Correction:** the popular story that Tokugawa Ieyasu *banned* sukuyō "because it was too accurate" circulates uncritically in Japanese pop-fortune media ([example blog](https://sudatiremonn.hatenablog.com/entry/2018/04/21/173015)) but is contradicted by the scholarship — the lineage died out ~1417, with no documented Edo suppression or continuous transmission ([Kotyk](https://archive.org/stream/JJRS451Kotyk/JJRS%2045-1%20Kotyk_djvu.txt)). Your content should treat "sealed by Ieyasu" as legend.

**Modern sukuyō fortune-telling — VERIFIED as popular, and yes, compatibility is its signature.** Present-day 宿曜占星術 is a modern reconstruction from the Sukuyōkyō using **27 mansions**; your *honmyōshuku* (本命宿) is the mansion of the Moon on your birth date, and it is marketed heavily for personality, daily fortune (日運), and above all **compatibility (相性)** ([to-manabi overview](https://to-manabi.com/what-is-syukuyo-astrology); associations and apps: [宿曜秘宝協会](https://syukuyo.jp/about-syukuyo/), [Google Play app](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=jp.co.reiji.kouyou&hl=en_US), commercial readings [unkoi](https://unkoi.com/fortune-premium/contents/telsys/koyorian/about.html), mass-market books [Suruga-ya](https://www.suruga-ya.com/en/product/BO163238)). The compatibility engine classifies the *distance between two people's mansions on the 27-wheel* into six relation types — 栄親 (prosperity/parent — best), 友衰 (friend/decline), 危成 (danger/achievement), 安壊 (ease/destruction — the famous "fatal attraction" pairing), 業胎 (karma/womb), and 命 (same mansion) — each except 命/業/胎 further split by three distances (近/中/遠), and readings are **asymmetric** (you may be 栄 to them while they are 親 to you) ([Yakumoin explanation](https://yakumoin.net/about/aisyou); type overviews: [uranai.blog](https://uranai.blog/shukuyo-aisho/), [JAAMP](https://www.domap.net/uranai/syukuyo/syukuyo-column08/)). Structurally this is the same 27÷9 arithmetic family as tārābala — a genuine, honest bridge for your daily-card + compatibility features.

**Pop-culture footprint — real and citable.** The definitive fandom anchor: *Fushigi Yūgi*, where all 28 celestial warriors are literally named after the xiù — Tamahome = 鬼宿 (Ghost mansion), Hotohori = 星宿 (Star mansion), Nuriko = 柳宿 (Willow mansion), seven warriors per god matching the four quadrants ([Fushigi Yūgi wiki](https://fushigiyuugi.fandom.com/wiki/Celestial_Warriors); kanji confirmed at [AcademicKids](https://academickids.com/encyclopedia/index.php/Fushigi_Yugi)). The mansions also permeate Onmyōji-boom media settings (Heian court diviner rivalry is exactly the Sukuyōdō-vs-Onmyōdō history above) and Japanese temple/shrine calendars; sukuyō titles are a steady commercial fortune-telling genre in Japan ([Suruga-ya listings](https://www.suruga-ya.com/en/product/BO718150)). For a Vocaloid/anime audience, "your Fushigi Yūgi mansion" is an immediately legible hook.

Match: STRONG — identical asterism in all four. Fandom hook: the single best mansion for the "everyone's mansion agrees" story.

### Example B — #24 Saʿd al-Suʿūd ("Luck of Lucks")

### Example C — #10 al-Jabha ("the Forehead")

- **Name:** al-Jabha, "the Forehead (of the Lion)"; variants el-Cebhe, Algebhal/Algelioche (Latin) ([Uysal](https://sirauysal.com/en/blog/lunar-mansions); [Renaissance Astrology](https://www.renaissanceastrology.com/mansionslistpicatrix.html); [Agrippa](https://www.esotericarchives.com/agrippa/agripp2c.htm)). - **Stars:** the Lion's forehead group ζ, γ, η, α Leo — including Regulus; determinant η Leo (Al Jabhah) in al-Bīrūnī's convention ([Uysal](https://sirauysal.com/en/blog/lunar-mansions); multi-star group per the astronomer tables in [Suhayl 8](https://www.ub.edu/arab/suhayl/volums/volum8/paper%204.pdf)).
- **Position:** 25°43′ Cancer – 8°34′ Leo ([Uysal](https://sirauysal.com/en/blog/lunar-mansions)).
- **Classical election:** favorable for trade and construction ([Uysal](https://sirauysal.com/en/blog/lunar-mansions)); Agrippa: strengthens buildings, promotes "love and benevolence, and help against enemies" ([Esoteric Archives](https://www.esotericarchives.com/agrippa/agripp2c.htm)).

- **anwāʾ:** the calendar's oddity — the only mansion allotted **14 days** of the solar year instead of 13 ([Muslim Heritage](https://muslimheritage.com/stellar-lunar-keys-medieval-mus-agriculture/)); its midsummer naw' was a notable rain marker (detail thin in accessible translation — Ibn Qutaybah is the source to mine).
