# Print-Once Cards and the Mistakes of Hasbro/Wizards

Research brief for Manzil (Triple Triad-descended, 28 lunar mansions). Compiled 6 October 2026. Figures are dated inline; anything I could not verify against a primary or reputable secondary source is marked **[unverified]**.

---

## 0. The one-paragraph version

"Never reprinted" is the most expensive promise a card game can make. Magic made it in 1996 under duress, tightened it in 2010 under collector pressure, and thirty years later it is a 572-card legal/financial fossil that prices players out of two formats, constrains design, breeds counterfeits, and produced the $999 proxy fiasco of 2022. Meanwhile the publishers who did *not* make that promise (Pokémon, Lorcana, Vanguard) weathered shortages by printing more, and the publisher that actually printed everything once (KeyForge) did so as a *game mechanic*, not a value proposition. Hasbro's 2020-2026 record is the mirror image: not scarcity but *flood*. Both failure modes come from the same root: letting secondary-market price become the product. A print-once model for Manzil can work only if the scarcity is **a fact about an event, not a promise about a market**.

## 1. The Reserved List

### Origin (1996) and revisions (2002, 2010)

- **Trigger.** *Fourth Edition* and *Chronicles* (1995) reprinted roughly 500 early cards; collector values collapsed and shops that had bought in protested. Wizards published the Official Reprint Policy on **4 March 1996**, promising a set of cards would never be reprinted "in black or white border in game-functionally identical form." ([Misprint explainer, Feb 2026](https://www.misprint.com/posts/magic-reserved-list-explained); [MTG Wiki](https://mtg.wiki/page/Reserved_List))
- **Scope.** The list closed in June 1999 (*Urza's Destiny* the last contributing set); 2002 removed Limited Edition commons/uncommons and declared no card from *Mercadian Masques* onward would be reserved. Today: **572 cards**. ([Misprint](https://www.misprint.com/posts/magic-reserved-list-explained); Hoardgate also cites 572)
- **2010 tightening.** The 2002 wording left a "premium" loophole: foil versions could be reprinted (*Duel Decks: Phyrexia vs. the Coalition*, *From the Vault: Relics*). Collector reaction was "predominantly negative" and from 2011 the policy bans Reserved cards "in either premium or non-premium form." ([MTG Wiki](https://mtg.wiki/page/Reserved_List)) Note the direction of travel: the policy was *tightened*, not loosened, because the people it protects were loud.

### Why Wizards says it can't go: legal vs. "just policy"

- **The legal view.** Bill Stark (ex-WotC senior product manager, ~14 years) in July 2022: "you can't abolish it for legal reasons that would bankrupt the game... Lawsuits aren't about whether you win or lose, it's about the cost to doing so." Commentary frames the exposure as **promissory estoppel**: a public promise, reasonably relied on by buyers who paid thousands, with detriment if broken. ([Wargamer, Jul 2022](https://www.wargamer.com/magic-the-gathering/reserved-list-legal); [MTG Rocks, Jul 2022](https://mtgrocks.com/this-is-why-wizards-wont-remove-the-mtg-reserved-list/))
- **The "just policy" view.** The list is "technically corporate policy rather than legally binding" ([Misprint](https://www.misprint.com/posts/magic-reserved-list-explained)); Wizards has unilaterally amended it twice (2002, 2010). No court has tested it. Whether a mass tort by card-holders would survive is genuinely unknown; the practical point Stark makes is that *defending* it, against thousands of plaintiffs with real losses and a 30-year paper trail, would be ruinous regardless of merit.
- **Rosewater's position.** 2015: the list's supporters "stay mostly silent on social media, but it's a much bigger number than I assume most of you guess." 2018: "If I could snap my fingers and have it never have existed, I would." 2021: "I spent years trying. I don't think it's going away." 2022: "We know the majority of players would like it gone... that doesn't make any of the other obstacles go away." ([Wargamer](https://www.wargamer.com/magic-the-gathering/reserved-list-mark-rosewater))
- **The structural reading.** Hipsters of the Coast (Jan 2023) argued the community lacks "means, motive, and opportunity" to force repeal the way D&D fans forced the OGL reversal: no boycott, no unified outrage, no triggering event. ([Hipsters](https://www.hipstersofthecoast.com/2023/01/could-the-magic-community-force-wizards-of-the-coast-to-repeal-magics-reserved-list/))

### What "never reprinted" did to the game over 30 years

Hoardgate's April 2026 survey of prices and consequences ([link](https://www.hoardgate.com/news/mtg-reserved-list-dangers-2026-dual-lands-legacy-commander)):

| Card | Approx. price (Apr 2026) |
|---|---|
| Underground Sea | ~$930 |
| Volcanic Island | ~$747 |
| Tundra | ~$562 |
| Tropical Island | ~$530 |
| The Tabernacle at Pendrell Vale | ~$2,900 |
| Gaea's Cradle | ~$1,579 (mid-2026, per [Misprint](https://www.misprint.com/posts/magic-reserved-list-explained)) |

A competitive 4-colour Legacy mana base is **$2,000-$3,000 in dual lands alone**. Five documented effects:

1. **Legacy attrition.** Entry cost drives shrinking attendance; the format survives largely on proxies at the local level.
2. **Commander two-tier.** The game's most popular format has a class of cards most players will never own, so proxy culture becomes normal and the official product is routed around.
3. **Design constraint.** Wizards cannot print functional reprints or mechanically identical alternatives. The 2023 "playtest" Counterspell with official art/name but "not tournament legal" is read as a probe of how to sell Reserved-List-adjacent product without reprinting. ([AetherHub](https://Aetherhub.com/Article/Do-New-Playtest-Cards-Point-To-Reserved-List-Reprints-In-The-Future))
4. **Counterfeiting.** High fixed prices make duals and Power the primary counterfeit targets.
5. **Community fracture.** Collectors protecting asset value vs. players demanding access, a permanent fault line.

### The 30th Anniversary Edition (Oct 2022)

Announced 4 Oct 2022: **$999** for four 15-card boosters of *Beta*-style reproductions (Black Lotus included), **not tournament legal**, random contents. Wizards' framing: a collector product that avoids "gatekeeping actual playable cards" and sidesteps the Reserved List because the cards aren't legal. Reaction: a 2,000-comment Reddit thread; The Professor (Tolarian Community College) noted Wizards was selling proxies players can already make. The sale was shut down early. ([Wargamer](https://www.wargamer.com/magic-the-gathering/fans-angry-30th-anniversary-edition); [Dot Esports](https://dotesports.com/mtg/news/magic-30th-anniversary-edition-sale-mysteriously-shuts-down-early-potentially-to-avoid-more-backlash)) The lesson: once a promise forbids you from making the thing players want, you end up selling a simulacrum of it at a price that insults them.

### State of the debate, 2025-2026

No policy change. Misprint (Feb 2026) calls it "untouchable" and "repeatedly reaffirmed." The live threads are: prices still rising (above), proxy normalisation in Commander, and the 2026 "playtest card" hints. There is no public indication Wizards plans repeal; there is public indication they are exploring non-legal reproductions as a revenue line. ([Misprint](https://www.misprint.com/posts/magic-reserved-list-explained); [Hoardgate](https://www.hoardgate.com/news/mtg-reserved-list-dangers-2026-dual-lands-legacy-commander))

**Takeaway for Manzil:** the Reserved List is the canonical case of a promise made to the *secondary market*, which then outlived the people who made it and now constrains the game's design and the players' access. Whatever you promise, promise it to **players about the game**, not to **holders about price**.

## 2. Hasbro/Wizards' documented mistakes, 2020-2026

### The numbers first

| Year | MTG revenue | Wizards & Digital segment | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | >$1.0bn (first billion-dollar brand) | segment rev +3%; tabletop +12%, digital -23% | ([Magic Untapped, Feb 2023](https://magicuntapped.com/index.php/news/magic-the-gathering-sees-record-year-exceeds-1b-in-revenue)) |
| 2023 | ~$1.09bn (record, LOTR-driven) | op. profit ~$530m | ([Draftsim, Feb 2025](https://draftsim.com/mtg-revenue-drop/)) |
| 2024 | $1.08bn (-1%; Q4 -19%) | rev +4%; op. profit ~$630m | ([Draftsim](https://draftsim.com/mtg-revenue-drop/)) |
| 2025 | **$1.7bn (+59%)**; Q4 $502m vs $208m (+141%) | **~$2.2bn rev (+45%)**, op. profit **>$1bn (+46%)** | Hasbro total $4.7bn (+14%). ([BoardGameWire, 11 Feb 2026](https://boardgamewire.com/index.php/2026/02/11/record-magic-the-gathering-success-powered-hasbro-to-4-7bn-revenue-for-2025-remains-its-primary-growth-engine/); [Wargamer, 10 Feb 2026](https://www.wargamer.com/magic-the-gathering/2025-financial-results-best-year)) |
| H1 2026 | $1,014.9m YTD (+34%); **Q2 $545.3m (+32%)**, first $500m quarter | Q2 segment $663.8m (+27%), op. profit $270m, 40.7% margin | Drivers: *Secrets of Strixhaven*, *Marvel Super Heroes* ("record-breaking debut"). Other 2025 data points: organised play participation +22%, WPN stores +20%, "a record year for Secret Lair" (Cocks). ([Wargamer](https://www.wargamer.com/magic-the-gathering/2025-financial-results-best-year))

Note what this table says: **the mistakes below did not hurt revenue.** Magic has roughly doubled since the "killing the golden goose" downgrade. That is exactly why they are worth studying: they are mistakes in trust, design health and community, which a solo designer cannot absorb the way a $4.7bn company can.

### 2.1 Product fatigue and the "overprinting" charge

- **Nov 2022: Bank of America downgrade.** Analyst Jason Haas cut Hasbro to Underperform, projecting ~34% downside, saying Wizards was "killing the golden goose" by flooding the market with releases and crushing secondary prices. Wizards president Cynthia Williams replied there was "no evidence that Magic is overprinted" and blamed the October 2022 double-release on supply chain. ([CNBC, 8 Dec 2022](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/12/08/hasbro-defends-magic-the-gathering-strategy.html))
- **Release cadence.** 2024: **9** products (8 with new cards). 2025: **7** (6 draftable). Rosewater framed 2025 as a deliberate reduction after fatigue complaints. ([MTG Rocks, 4 Nov 2024](https://mtgrocks.com/mtg-designer-confirms-that-only-7-sets-are-releasing-in-2025/); [Commander's Herald](https://commandersherald.com/rosewater-2025-to-feature-fewer-main-releases/)) 2023 is widely cited as the peak-fatigue year (LOTR, Aftermath, Doctor Who, Commander Masters, Wilds of Eldraine, Lost Caverns, Phyrexia, March of the Machine, plus Secret Lairs) **[exact 2023 count unverified]**. Draftsim's 2025 retrospective: 2025 had "one of the lowest output of total cards" yet **Standard got 50% more new cards than in 2023** because UB sets became Standard-legal. ([Draftsim, Dec 2025](https://draftsim.com/the-biggest-magic-the-gathering-news-stories-from-2025/))
- **The 2026 Standard change.** Announced Oct 2024: Standard expands from four to **six sets a year**, three in-universe and three Universes Beyond, all Standard-legal. Critics called it "live service" Magic, devaluing individual releases. ([In Between Drafts, Oct 2024](https://inbetweendrafts.com/the-new-magic-the-gathering-standard-looks-like-a-live-service/)) 2025 then produced a **record 10 Standard bans** chasing Izzet dominance. ([Draftsim](https://draftsim.com/the-biggest-magic-the-gathering-news-stories-from-2025/))

### 2.2 Universes Beyond: backlash, then dominance

- **2020 Walking Dead Secret Lair** (4 Oct 2020): six *mechanically unique* cards sold only for a limited window. The objection was not the IP but the **scarcity of unique game pieces**. Wizards promised "Universes Within" in-universe versions; they arrived three years later in *Wilds of Eldraine* (Aug 2023). ([Hipsters](https://www.hipstersofthecoast.com/2023/08/the-walking-dead-universes-within-mtg/)) Lesson: a limited-time unique card is a debt you will eventually have to pay.
- **Oct 2024 announcement** that UB would be Standard-legal and 50% of releases: "There is not one announcement I've hated as much as this in the entire history of MTG" (Reddit); Rhystic Studies' "Your Foundation is Rotten." Rosewater: "Because the players are saying loudly that they want it... This cadence is not forever. It's for now." ([MTG Rocks, 29 Oct 2024](https://mtgrocks.com/universes-beyond-backlash-mtg/))
- **Then it won.** *Final Fantasy* (June 2025) did **~$200m in its first day** and is the best-selling Magic set ever; LOTR second; *Avatar* third. ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/magic-the-gatherings-final-fantasy-set-made-200-million-in-one-day); [Wargamer](https://www.wargamer.com/magic-the-gathering/2025-financial-results-best-year)) *Marvel Super Heroes* (June 2026) drove the first $500m quarter. ([Hasbro](https://newsroom.hasbro.com/node/36961))
- **Spider-Man (Sept 2025): the paradox.** Rosewater later said it was a **top-ten best-selling set**; the market saw a crash, with sealed product below MSRP, low prerelease turnout, abandoned FNMs. Reconciliation: Wizards sells to distributors; retailers over-ordered after Final Fantasy. The glut wiped out scalpers. ([Magic Untapped, Feb 2026](https://magicuntapped.com/index.php/articles/the-spider-man-valley-how-one-of-the-worst-selling-sets-in-mtg-history-still-did-some-good); [Draftsim, Mar 2026](https://draftsim.com/spider-man-becomes-bestseller)) Lesson: sell-in is not sell-through, and a publisher's "record" can coexist with a community's bad year.

### 2.3 Secret Lair: print-to-demand abandoned

Jan 2024: Secret Lair moved from print-to-demand to **limited print runs** to "expedite shipping." Commentators noted print-to-demand had kept secondary prices sane and that the 30th Anniversary Countdown Kit (2022) sold out in an hour with scalpers taking 30 each. ([Wargamer, 3 Jan 2024](https://www.wargamer.com/magic-the-gathering/mtg-secret-lair-change-scalpers)) 2025: queue failures on multiple drops and players asking for print-to-demand back, even as Cocks called it a record year. ([Draftsim](https://draftsim.com/the-biggest-magic-the-gathering-news-stories-from-2025/)) This is the cleanest natural experiment in the dataset: **same product, demand-printed = healthy; fixed-run = scalped.**

### 2.4 Alchemy and digital-only rebalancing (Dec 2021 - Feb 2027)

Launched Dec 2021 with digital-only cards and rebalanced versions of paper cards; disliked from day one ("never really captured the hearts of most MTG players"). On 11 Sept 2026 Wizards announced Alchemy ends **2 Feb 2027**; rebalanced cards revert to paper text on 22 Sept 2026 with five bans. ([Draftsim, 11 Sept 2026](https://draftsim.com/mtg-arena-removes-alchemy-format/); [Wargamer](https://www.wargamer.com/magic-the-gathering-arena/alchemy-replacements)) Lesson: players treat a card's text as part of what they bought; silently changing it reads as a breach, even when free.

### 2.5 The Pinkerton incident (April 2023)

Wizards sent Pinkerton agents to the home of YouTuber Dan Cannon (oldschoolmtg) to retrieve mis-shipped *March of the Machine: Aftermath* boxes he had opened on camera. ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/magic-march-of-the-machine-aftermath-leak-pinkertons-1850369015); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/magic-the-gathering-pinkerton-youtube-wotc-mtg-1850372856)) Relevant because it shows what *protecting a spoiler/scarcity schedule* looks like when it goes wrong: a company treating its own release calendar as more important than a customer.

### 2.6 The Commander bans crisis (Sept 2024)

23 Sept 2024: the volunteer Commander Rules Committee banned Jeweled Lotus, Dockside Extortionist, Mana Crypt (and Nadu). Owners saw hundreds of dollars evaporate; RC members received death threats. On 30 Sept the RC dissolved and Wizards took over the format, promising power "brackets" (launched 2025). ([Wargamer, 30 Sept 2024](https://www.wargamer.com/magic-the-gathering/mtg-wizards-of-the-coast-takes-over-commander-rules-committee)) Lesson: when cards are expensive assets, every balance decision is a financial event, and volunteers cannot carry that.

### 2.7 Layoffs and leadership

Dec 2023: **1,100** roles cut, on top of ~800 earlier in 2023 (roughly a third of Hasbro's workforce), with Wizards hit in design, art and IT; Cocks: "Cost-cutting is not a strategy." ([Pocket Gamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/another-1100-hasbro-layoffs-mean-a-third-of-workforce-slashed-in-2023)) 2025 feb: Cocks' public tone shifted to "more places to buy, more people playing." ([BoardGameWire](https://boardgamewire.com/index.php/2026/02/11/record-magic-the-gathering-success-powered-hasbro-to-4-7bn-revenue-for-2025-remains-its-primary-growth-engine/)) I could **not verify** any specific Cocks "whales" quote; treat that meme as unverified.

### 2.8 Price increases

April 2022: ~11% across boosters, bundles and Commander decks, effective Sept 2022, citing goods/transport/manufacturing costs. ([Wizards](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/announcements/magic-the-gathering-pricing-update)) 2025: Play Booster MSRP and tariff-related increases were widely reported around *Final Fantasy* and *Lorwyn Eclipsed* **[specific 2025 percentage unverified here]** ([Commander's Herald](https://commandersherald.com/trade-war-with-midgar-erupts-over-price-of-mtg-booster-packs/)).

### Pattern

Hasbro's error is not scarcity; it is **treating cardboard as a financial instrument and then being surprised when players behave like investors**: fatigue when supply rises, rage when bans cut value, scalping when supply is capped, lawsuits-in-waiting when reprints threaten. Every one of these is downstream of letting market price carry meaning the game should carry.

## 3. How other TCGs handle print runs and reprints

| Game | Model | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| **Flesh and Blood** (LSS) | 2019-22: *1st Edition* (cold-foil, fixed run) then *Unlimited*. **FAB 2.0 (Apr 2022)** retired the split; reprints go into white-bordered, non-foil *History Packs* so original printings keep collectability. ([SCG, Apr 2022](https://articles.starcitygames.com/flesh-and-blood/retiring-1st-edition-new-products-reprints-casual-play-rules-and-more-coming-to-flesh-and-blood/)) | *Crucible of War* "Unlimited" went out of print after three waves in 2021; Shiyana hit $399.99 and players felt misled because "Unlimited" implied demand-printing. ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/flesh-and-blood-has-stopped-printing-the-crucible-of-war-set-and-players-arent-happy/)) **Living Legend**: heroes accrue points from tournament wins (Worlds 300, Pro Tour 200, Calling 100...), ~1,000 points/season, and retire from Classic Constructed at a threshold; target lifespan 15-20 months. Rationale: "recognize sustained excellence... elegantly evolve the meta-game." ([LSS](https://legacy.fabtcg.com/en/articles/living-legend-update-sept-22/)) **This is scarcity by rotation, earned in play, not by printing.** |
| **Lorcana** (Ravensburger) | Reprint-friendly. | *First Chapter* (Aug 2023): boxes $144 MSRP peaked $403; rares to $1,000. Ravensburger reprinted; by Dec 2023 boxes were ~$159. ([WDWNT](https://wdwnt.com/2023/12/disney-lorcana-market-price-dropping-near-msrp-following-first-chapter-reprint)) Then normalisation: Ravensburger's 2025 revenue fell 5.9% to €744m, Lorcana "settled at a high level" as speculators "withdrew." ([BoardGameWire](https://boardgamewire.com/?p=5759)) Digital game announced at D23, 14 Aug 2026. ([Toys n Playthings](https://www.toysnplaythings.media/disney-lorcana-announces-digital-game-and-major-2027-plans-at-d23/)) Reprinting cost them the speculators and kept them the players. |
| **Pokémon** (TPCi) | Print to demand, explicitly. | 18 Dec 2024: "We are actively working to print more of the impacted Pokémon TCG products as quickly as possible and at maximum capacity." ([Wargamer](https://www.wargamer.com/pokemon-trading-card-game/reprint-promised-prismatic-evolutions)) Production (Mar-Mar): 2023 52.9bn, 2024 64.8bn, 2025 75bn, **2026 85bn+ cards**; still "demand outpacing supply"; new plant 2027 earliest. ([ComicBook, 28 May 2026](https://comicbook.com/gaming/news/new-report-confirms-pokemon-tcg-massively-increased-production-in-2026-but-its-still-not-enough/)) Scalping persisted anyway: scarcity is not needed to create it; brand heat alone will. |
| **Sorcery: Contested Realm** (Erik's Curiosa) | *Alpha* (Kickstarter, 2022-23) then *Beta* (same 403 cards, β symbol, 2023). Beta pre-orders were explicitly to "gauge interest... not to over- or under-print" and Beta "widely available through regular distribution." ([Sorcery](https://sorcerytcg.com/news/beta-preorders)) | Alpha keeps a collector premium through the **edition mark**, not a reprint ban. Clean and honest: the cards are the same, the stamp is different. |
| **MetaZoo** | Scarcity marketing, 1st-edition hype, celebrity equity (Steve Aoki), NFT tie-ins, PSA top-10 submissions in 2021. | Shut down 29 Jan 2024 via Discord, socials wiped, bankruptcy; IP bought for $2m; relaunched 2025 as a different game. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MetaZoo); [KYM](https://amp.knowyourmeme.com/news/contentious-card-game-metazoo-shuts-down-after-years-of-combatting-scam-allegations)) The cautionary tale: when the pitch is "these will be worth more," the game is a pump the moment it stops being one. |
| **KeyForge** (FFG → Ghost Galaxy) | **Literally print-once:** an algorithm generated unique, unmodifiable decks; no two the same. | Launched Nov 2018 to 4th-place TCG sales; collapsed by late 2019; FFG said in 2021 the algorithm was "broken and needs to be rebuilt." Ghost Galaxy revived via Gamefound ($1.1m, 2022), but the next set fell 54%/64%. ([Tabletop Gaming](https://www.tabletopgaming.co.uk/Features/what-happened-to-keyforge)) *KeyForge Online* announced 11 Sept 2026 (Steam 2028); physical decks will be playable digitally. ([God is a Geek](https://godisageek.com/2026/09/keyforge-online-steam-release/)) Uniqueness worked **because no card was a scarce commodity**: every deck was one-of-one, so none was. |
| **Cardfight!! Vanguard** (Bushiroad) | Routine reprint sets (*Festival Collection*, *History Collection*, all-holo reissues). ([ICv2](https://icv2.com/articles/news/view/45676/festival-collection-premium-collection-2020)) | Japanese TCGs generally treat reprints as a service; premium comes from alt-art/foil treatments, not supply caps. |
| **Netrunner** (FFG LCG → Null Signal Games) | LCG: fixed, non-random distribution; everyone can buy the same pool. | FFG ended it June 2018 ("Jacking Out"); Null Signal, a nonprofit, continues with slower releases, rotation, revised core set; cards free to print-and-play and available print-on-demand **[POD specifics unverified in source]**. ([Tabletop Gaming, Nov 2024](https://www.tabletopgaming.co.uk/features/what-happened-to-netrunner-with-null-signal-games/)) Proof that a game can live with **zero** scarcity. |

### Print-once done well vs. done as a pump

**Done well:** the scarcity is *structural to the design or the event*, is stated plainly up front, and the publisher never implies price. KeyForge (every deck unique), Living Legend (retirement earned in play), Sorcery's edition marks, Pokémon Worlds participation cards (below).

**Done as a pump:** the scarcity is *about the product line*, is used in marketing ("first edition", "limited"), and secondary prices are quoted approvingly. MetaZoo, the 30th Anniversary Edition, FAB's early 1st Edition/Unlimited (which LSS itself abandoned), Secret Lair's 2024 switch to fixed runs.

The tell is simple: **does the publisher ever cite what their cards resell for?** If yes, it is a pump.

## 4. Digital TCG economics with scarcity

- **Web3/NFT card games.** Naavik's Dec 2023 review: Splinterlands DAU fell from 300-400k (2021-22) to <40k; Skyweaver from ~192k to ~4.7k; Parallel "largely unplayable since 2021"; Gods Unchained survived on secondary-market royalties but Immutable cut ~20 GU staff in July 2022 as IMX fell from $13.57 to $1.26. Diagnosis: "Making cards tradeable isn't enough on its own; the game must work." ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/web3-tcg-games/); [SmartCompany](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/technology/immutable-gods-unchained-redundancy-layoffs-blockchain-game/)) Digital scarcity *as the pitch* attracts people who leave when the price stops rising.
- **Hearthstone.** Full-dust refunds on nerfed cards and a free, rotating Core set since 2021 (Year of the Gryphon) **[details from general knowledge, not re-verified here]**; forums show the Classic-set owners felt "uncompensated" when Core replaced it. ([Blizzard forums](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/hearthstone/t/introducing-the-core-set-and-classic-format/53997?page=17)) Players tolerate rotation when the replacement is free and the rule is known in advance.
- **MTG Arena.** Wildcards by rarity, no trading, no dusting; the Alchemy reversion (Sept 2026) shows even "free" digital edits are resented. ([Draftsim](https://draftsim.com/mtg-arena-removes-alchemy-format/))
- **Marvel Snap.** New cards enter at Series 5 (6,000 tokens / 0.25% cache rate), drop to Series 4 (3,000 / 2.5%), then Series 3 (1,000) via scheduled "series drops." ([Marvel Snap Zone](https://marvelsnapzone.com/series/)) **Scarcity that decays on a public schedule** is the most player-accepted digital model found: early access costs more, nobody is locked out forever.
- **Pokémon TCG Pocket.** $120m in two weeks (Oct 2024); trading (Jan 2025) gated by trade tokens and rarity caps drew backlash; Creatures Inc. relaxed it within days. ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/pokemon-tcg-pockets-trading-feature-is-changing-after-fan-backlash)) Lesson: friction on *exchange* reads as manipulation faster than friction on *acquisition*.
- **Riftbound / Lorcana digital.** Riot job listing (Aug 2026) hints at a digital Riftbound; Lorcana digital announced Aug 2026 with no monetisation details. ([Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/riot-games-job-listing-hints-at-digital-riftbound-tcg/); [Toys n Playthings](https://www.toysnplaythings.media/disney-lorcana-announces-digital-game-and-major-2027-plans-at-d23/))

### Is digital scarcity meaningful or manipulation?

Players accept it when it is (a) a *schedule* (Snap series drops, Hearthstone rotation), (b) *earned* (ranked rewards, event participation), or (c) *free and reversible*. They reject it when it is (a) *purchased randomness*, (b) *trading friction*, or (c) *silent text changes*. Nobody rejected the Worlds participation card.

### 2023-2026 consumer-protection moves

- **EU CPC Network, 21 Mar 2025:** seven "key principles on in-game virtual currencies" (real-money price display, no forced bundle sizes, 14-day withdrawal rights, plain terms, protection of minors) plus enforcement against Star Stable for practices including "time-limited offers" aimed at children. ([Sheppard Mullin](https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/eu-new-european-consumer-protection-guidelines-for-virtual-currencies-in-video-games); [GamesMarket](https://www.gamesmarket.global/business/case-of-star-stable-online-conflict-ahead-eu-commission-and-cpc-set-sights-on-games-industry-1870db4b2004d7d284e1c833a28c1f38))
- **EU Digital Fairness Act:** proposal slated for **Q4 2026**; options include outright loot-box bans or parental-consent gates, mandatory real-money equivalents next to virtual prices, and limits on dark patterns, urgency cues and engagement rewards. ([Freshfields, Nov 2025](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/the-eus-proposed-digital-fairness-act-a-game-developers-guide-to-potential-imp-102ltio))
- **US FTC v. HoYoverse (Jan 2025):** $20m; no loot boxes to under-16s without parental consent; odds and exchange-rate disclosure; **direct real-money purchase alternatives to randomised systems**; brought under Section 5 (deception), not gambling law. ([Esports Legal News](https://esportslegal.news/2025/11/25/ftc-vs-genshin-impact-20-million/))
- **UK:** Ukie self-regulatory loot-box principles (July 2023); a 2024 audit of the top-100 grossing iOS games found no effective age verification, 23.5% disclosure of loot-box presence, 8.6% odds disclosure; 5Rights calls self-regulation failed. ([5Rights](https://5rightsfoundation.com/research-reveals-non-existent-enforcement-of-industry-led-standards-on-loot-boxes)) **Belgium** has treated paid loot boxes as gambling since 2018 **[enforcement status 2025-26 not verified here]**.

Nothing above targets *limited availability* as such. What regulators target is **randomness for money, hidden prices, urgency cues aimed at minors, and virtual-currency obfuscation**. A print-once model that sells a known card at a known price with a known window is on the right side of all four; a "last chance, 3 hours left" banner is not.

## 5. Legal and ethical edges of artificial scarcity

Three things that get called "scarcity" are legally and ethically different:

| Claim | What it is | Liability profile |
|---|---|---|
| **"Limited print run"** | A statement of fact about *this* production. | Low, if true. Must not imply there will be no *future* runs. (FAB's "Unlimited" confusion is the failure mode.) |
| **"Numbered edition"** | A collectible convention: the print is 37/500. | Regulated in art law. California Civil Code §1742-1744 requires written disclosure of edition size, medium, whether it is a reproduction, and plate/matrix status before sale. ([Cal. Civ. Code §1742](https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_1742)) New York's Arts and Cultural Affairs Law Art. 15 similar. *Sobel v. Eggleston* (S.D.N.Y. 2012): numbering a "limited edition" does **not** by itself promise no later editions in other sizes/media; the court dismissed because "the photographer made no enforceable promise preventing future editions." Buyers need an *explicit written* non-reproduction promise to have a claim. ([Center for Art Law](https://itsartlaw.org/2019/12/05/in-sobel-v-eggleston-limited-edition-is-no-limit-to-subsequent-editions/)) |
| **"We promise never to reprint"** | A forward-looking commitment to a market. | High and open-ended. This is the Reserved List: reliance, detriment, 30 years. It also carries **game-health liability**: you lose the ability to fix, re-issue, or make the game accessible. |

**How printmakers handle it.** An edition states: image, size, medium, number, and (the honest ones) whether artist's proofs or other formats exist. The ethical consensus is that the *edition* is the promise, not the *image*: Eggleston could legally re-issue the same image at a different size because the edition was defined by its format. ([Wikipedia: Edition (printmaking)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edition_(printmaking))) This is precisely the distinction Manzil can use: **the edition (the night) is closed; the image (the card's rules text) is not.**

**What a print-once promise should actually say (principles):**
1. Define the *unit* that is printed once narrowly and factually (a dated edition), not the *card* broadly.
2. State what *may* be reissued (rules-identical cards with different marks) so no one relies on a broader promise.
3. Never state or imply resale value, never quote secondary prices, never use urgency language.
4. Put the policy in writing before the first sale and version it; promissory estoppel attaches to what people reasonably understood.
5. Keep a documented escape hatch for errata, damage replacement, accessibility reprints and game-balance.

## 6. The in-universe angle: scarcity as fiction

Precedents where the card is scarce because **the occasion was**:

- **Pokémon Worlds participation cards** (since 2004): every year's card carries the Worlds logo, year and city on the art; placement stamps ("Top 32" to "Champion"); STAFF versions; multilingual. Some remain tournament-legal (Tropical Beach 2011-12 still shows up in Expanded). 2023 *Paradise Resort* ~$528; the 2026 PokémonXP-stamped Rayquaza was immediately sought. Nobody calls these manipulative: **you had to be there.** ([Folio](https://folio.fyi/blog/pokemon-worlds-promo-cards))
- **Pokémon prerelease/stamped promos**: PRERELEASE or set-logo foil stamps; STAFF stamps since Diamond & Pearl; the error-stamped Base Set Raichu that Wizards denied for years. ([Bulbapedia](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Prerelease_cards_(TCG)))
- **Magic dated/stamped promos**: prerelease cards date-stamped with the event ([CardTrader: Magic Origins Prerelease](https://www.cardtrader.com/en/games/magic/expansions/magic-origins-promos/blueprints_search)); the *Weatherlight Saga* (1997-2001) was Magic's first continuous storyline where set-by-set story events were tied to specific cards **[details from general knowledge, not re-verified]**.
- **KeyForge**: each deck exists once because the algorithm generated it once; the card is unique by *provenance*, not by *policy*. ([Tabletop Gaming](https://www.tabletopgaming.co.uk/Features/what-happened-to-keyforge))
- **Living Legend**: a hero leaves the format because of what happened in tournaments, a *history* the card now carries. ([LSS](https://legacy.fabtcg.com/en/articles/living-legend-update-sept-22/))

**Could "the card for the night of 14 March 2027" be the honest scarcity model?** Yes, and it is the only one in this survey that is simultaneously (a) true, (b) non-financial in its framing, (c) regulator-safe, and (d) load-bearing for the fiction. The sky of 14 March 2027 (Moon in a given mansion, with given planets in given signs) will not recur; a card that *records* that sky is scarce for the same reason a Worlds 2026 card is: the event is over. The risk is purely in execution: if the dated card is also the *only* way to get a rules-relevant game piece, you have rebuilt the Reserved List with astrology stickers. Hence section 7.

## 7. A print policy for Manzil

### The principle

**Scarcity lives in the edition, never in the game piece.** Every rule-bearing card is permanently available (digital) and reprintable (physical). What is printed once is the *record of a night*: a dated, stamped, numbered edition of a card whose rules-identical twin anyone can always get.

### The structure

1. **Canon (always available).** The core 28 mansions and all rules-bearing cards exist as the *Canon printing*. In the app and web client they are earnable through play, purchasable at a fixed posted price, and never random. Physically, the Kickstarter deck is the Canon printing; it may be reprinted at any time, forever, and the policy says so in the first sentence.

2. **Night Editions (printed once).** For each mansion night in a given year (e.g., *Al-Sharatain, 14 March 2027*), a Night Edition of that mansion's card exists. It is rules-identical to Canon. It differs only in: the date; the actual ephemeris of that night (Moon's position, visible planets, phase) rendered on the card; a year/night stamp; a sequential number. Digital Night Editions are issued only to accounts that *played* on that night (or at that event); physical Night Editions are printed in exactly the quantity claimed during a stated window after the night, then the plate closes. **No Night Edition is ever issued after its window.** This is a fact about the calendar, not a market promise.

3. **How you get a Night Edition.** Play a match on the night (digital, free); attend a sanctioned table (physical); or, during the post-night window only, order the physical print at the posted fixed price. No lottery, no pack, no currency, no streak requirement; missing a night costs you nothing in game terms.

4. **Rotation by sky, not by ban.** If a future design needs retirement, use the FAB model honestly: a *year* of Night Editions can "set" (leave a competitive format) on a published astronomical schedule (e.g., when the lunar cycle for that year completes), while Canon remains legal everywhere. Nothing is ever banned for value reasons; balance changes are published, errata'd on Canon, and Night Editions carry the same errata.

5. **The 2010 lesson, inverted.** Reserve the *right*, in writing, to make Night Edition **art** available later in non-card formats (prints, digital wallpapers, a yearbook) and to issue damage-replacement copies marked "REPLACEMENT." This is exactly the Eggleston distinction: the edition is closed, the image is not.

6. **Secondary market stance.** Manzil never quotes resale prices, never runs "last chance" countdowns, never calls anything "rare" in marketing, and discloses the exact count of each Night Edition after the window closes (the California §1744 disclosure norm, applied voluntarily).

### What the promise to players should say

> *Every card you need to play Manzil will always be available at a fixed price, and may be reprinted at any time. Night Editions record a real sky on a real date. Each one is issued only to people who played on that night or ordered it in the posted window after; once the window closes the number is final and published. Night Editions are identical in play to the standard card. We will never issue a Night Edition after its window, and we will never sell cards by chance.*

### What it should NOT say

- "Never reprinted" (unqualified). You will need errata, replacements, accessibility runs. Say what *is* closed (the dated edition) and what is not (the game piece).
- "Limited" / "rare" / "collectible" / "investment" / any resale figure. These are the words that (a) invited the Reserved List's reliance problem, (b) are what MetaZoo sold, (c) are what the DFA and FTC are looking at when combined with urgency.
- "First Edition" vs "Unlimited." LSS abandoned it in 2022 for a reason; it makes the ordinary card feel like the lesser one.
- Anything about *future* Night Editions being fewer, scarcer, or more valuable.
- Any promise that binds Manzil's balance decisions to card value.

### The tension, named

Artificial scarcity is a monetisation pressure because *the thing you sell by scarcity is a price*. This model refuses to sell a price. It sells **attendance**: the Night Edition is proof you were under that sky. That is the only form of scarcity in this survey (Worlds cards, KeyForge, Living Legend) that players have consistently read as honest, and it is the one that happens to be literally true for a game about the moon.
