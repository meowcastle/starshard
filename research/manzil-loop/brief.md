# Manzil — what it is (brief for researchers)

Manzil is the mini-game inside Star Shard, a web (and later native) astrology game built on the 28 lunar mansions. The moon crosses one mansion per night (~27.3-day cycle). Audience skews young (~25% aged 13–17); the product has a strict ethics floor: no loot boxes, no virtual currency, no energy/stamina, no streak counters, no paid time-skips, no missable-forever content, no guilt/shame states, notifications opt-in. One-time purchase ($19–24) unlocks the game; cosmetics (artist card-face skins) are the only recurring revenue. "The sky is the drop table."

## The game (v3 ruleset, locked 18–19 Aug 2026) — Triple-Triad-principled
- A capture duel vs "the sky" (a deterministic AI), played on a 1×9 row ("the road": the nine mansions the moon will cross next, starting tonight).
- You and the sky alternate lodging cards on the nine slots. Each card has two numbers: left and right. Left fights the left neighbour's right number, right fights the right neighbour's left. Bigger number claims (flips) the smaller. No cascades (one comparison, once; Mars is the exception). Own most of the 9 when full to win the board. A match is best of five boards (first to 3). Lead alternates each round.
- Your hand: five cards ("the walking five") chosen from the cards you own. Cards are the 28 mansions (e.g. The Storm 8|5, The Throne 6|9, The Void 9|2). Numbers are derived from real star data (total = star prominence; side gap = how much the four astrological traditions agree).
- The sky's hand: tonight's five REAL planets (Saturn 9|5, Mars 8|6, Venus 4|7, Mercury 6|5, Jupiter 7|8), face down until played. Same night = same duel for everyone on Earth. Planet signatures: Saturn's ground is locked (never claimed); Mars's claim strikes onward (chain one step); Venus softens both neighbours' facing numbers by 1; Mercury picks its better face (may reverse); Jupiter counts as two at the count.
- AI: greedy arithmetic that reads your best single reply (2-ply) before committing. Deterministic.
- Dominion: a card lodged on its OWN mansion counts as two at the count, whoever holds it; sky planets get it where they truly stand tonight. Not hinted on deck screen (spotting it is skill).
- Levels 1–4 per card, earned only by playing (wins level faster than visits). L1 vanilla numbers, L2 wakes the card's signature move (e.g. Storm claims ties; Heart safe while touching your card; Blaze's claims are final; Throne two-faced — choose which number faces which way), L3–4 +1 to numbers. Card faces shed info as they level (L2 drops the name, L3 drops the printed move, L4 is nearly all art) — the "familiarity law". 28 signatures designed (Gate, Bearer, Gathered Stars, Follower, Blaze, Storm, Return, Ghost, Glance, Throne, Mane, Turning, Hand, Jewel, Veil, Claws, Crown, Heart, Root, Flock, Empty District, Listener, Drum, Void, Hideaway, Chamber, Guide, Thread); 4 live in prototype.
- Collection: all 28 playable from day one as grey "loaners" (always L1). Your birth-chart's cards start truly owned. Winning tonight's mansion's match CLAIMS it (name + date go on it; levels unlock). Unlimited matches per night. Win → a "game-shard" (a separate collection from the 28 sky-earned road shards, which are never skill-gated) + tonight's mansion joins the deck. Lose → nothing lost.
- Event nights (designed, not built): outer planets (Uranus swaps two lodged cards, Neptune mists her numbers, Pluto removes a card) arrive only on real astronomical event nights; an opt-in 14-slot "eclipse fortnight" board. Real celestial events mint dated event cards with computed rarity printed; the set never closes.
- Cards are "living": one slow ambient motion each. Palette: parchment (you) vs amber (sky). Tone: quiet, lowercase, no fourth-wall, no prophecy.
- Already-known research in the project (don't re-derive, build on it): Pokémon GO vs Genshin D365 (20% vs 1.8%) — real-world-sourced content beats authored content; Wordle's global synchrony; Neko Atsume "absence generates content"; Duolingo binge vs pace; "ship the archive day one"; Stardew 28-day calendar; refuse streaks / paid repair / seasonal expiry / stamina.
- Known open questions from the designer: how does it stay fresh for YEARS; is the AI opponent enough; is best-of-five the right match length; does the walking-five deck create enough expression; is the collection loop (claim a mansion per night) too slow or too fast; what does mastery look like at month 12.
