# Corpus batch 4 — stations 15–21

*Star Shard reading corpus · August 12, 2026 · The Veil through The Empty
District — the scorpion sequence, the ostriches, and the crossing into
the Black Tortoise. Same four slots, same laws.*

**Note on this stretch:** stations 16–19 walk the scorpion the way 9–12
walked the lion — claws, crown, heart, sting — and it is the most
unanimous run on the whole road: three of those four are `STRONG`
matches, and two of them were given the *same word* by sky-cultures that
never met. Station 21 is the strangest station in the system and gets the
longest note.

---

## STATION.15 — The Veil · 亢宿

*0°00′–12°51′ Libra · ι/κ/λ Virginis · Arabic al-Ghafr, "the cover" ·
Svāti, the wind's shoot · guardian: the dragon · kept by Venus · Azure
Dragon*

**STATION.15.strike**
your sun landed at **0° libra** — the autumn balance point, where day and
night come level — on three stars so faint the arabs named the station
for the fact that you can barely see it: *al-ghafr*, **the cover**. the
thing that veils. and the old election books give this sky **digging and
buried treasure**, which is the joke and the instruction at once: the
station about what's hidden is the station about what's worth finding.
india names this sky **svāti**, and gives it to vāyu, the wind — its
emblem a young shoot bending in open air. so your hunger is **to move
freely, and to bend without breaking**, and you have organized more of
your life around not being pinned down than you would probably admit.
the cost is that a cover works on everyone. the drafts folder, the
private practice, the version of the work nobody has seen — the habit of
keeping the good thing under wraps stops being protection at some point
and starts being the reason nobody has found it.

**STATION.15.root**
this sky's guardian is **the dragon** itself — the second station of the
azure dragon's country, and the one that carries the creature's own name
— and it is **venus-kept**. that is an inner life with real power in it
and a genuine preference for not using power: you'd rather be liked than
obeyed, and you are quietly aware you could have it the other way.
the room reads you as **easy**, and it is wrong in a specific way that
will follow you. people mistake your flexibility for having no position.
you have positions; you have simply decided most rooms haven't earned
them. a veil-rooted moon can go a very long time being underestimated,
enjoy it, and then discover that being underestimated has become the
whole arrangement rather than a tactic inside it.

**STATION.15.facing**
you enter without announcing anything, and people fill the space with
their own assumptions — which you allow. it takes a specific kind of
attention to notice how much you're not saying, and you tend to like
whoever notices.

**STATION.15.answer**
across the wheel, **the veil** answers you — the covered sky the old
books gave to buried treasure. once a year the full moon rises there and
asks what you've been keeping under wraps, and whether it was ever really
about protecting it.

## STATION.16 — The Claws · 氐宿

*12°51′–25°43′ Libra · Zubenelgenubi and Zubeneschamali · Arabic
al-Zubānā, "the claws" · Viśākhā, the forked sky · guardian: the raccoon
dog · kept by Saturn · Azure Dragon*

**STATION.16.strike**
your sun landed on two bright stars whose names still say what they used
to be: **zubenelgenubi and zubeneschamali** — *the southern claw* and
*the northern claw*. they belong to libra now, the scales, but they were
the **scorpion's grip** first. babylon saw both readings; greece kept the
claws; rome gave the balance back. the same two stars, held by every
tradition as a weapon and as a measuring instrument, at once.
india agrees in its own vocabulary: **viśākhā** means *forked* — two
branches — and its deity is indrāgnī, a fused pair, force and fire
together. its emblem is a triumphal arch, which has two legs and one
opening.
so the hunger of this sky is **to reach the thing, and to find out what
the reaching made of you.** you weigh and you seize, and the order in
which you do those two is the entire question of your life.
the cost: a person who can hold both readings can also stall between
them. weighing forever is a way of never gripping, and you will call it
fairness, and sometimes it will be.

**STATION.16.root**
this sky is guarded by **the raccoon dog** — the tanuki, the shapeshifter
of east asian folklore, the animal famous for changing what it looks like
— and it is **saturn-kept**, the planet of consequence and time. an inner
life that adapts its shape and then has to live with everything it agreed
to while shaped that way.
the room reads you as **fair**, and comes to you to settle things. what
that costs you is invisible from outside: to arbitrate well you have to
keep your own preference out of it, and after enough years of that,
people stop asking what you want — including, on bad days, you. a
claws-rooted moon should be asked, out loud, by someone who loves them:
*which one did you actually want?*

**STATION.16.facing**
you enter and start weighing — the room, the mood, who's not being
listened to. people find you even-handed on sight and bring you both
sides of things within a week of knowing you.

**STATION.16.answer**
across the wheel, **the claws** answer you — the grip that is also a set
of scales. once a year the full moon lands there and asks what you've
been weighing that you already know the answer to.

## STATION.17 — The Crown · 房宿

*25°43′ Libra–8°34′ Scorpio · β/δ/π Scorpii · Arabic al-Iklīl, "the
crown" · Anurādhā, Mitra's sky of alliance · guardian: the hare · kept by
the Sun · Azure Dragon*

**STATION.17.strike**
your sun landed on **three stars in a clean row across the scorpion's
brow** — *al-iklīl*, **the crown**, and one of the very few stations
every old sky-map agreed about completely. the election books give this
sky one signature above all others: **love of long duration.** not the
spark. the thing that is still there in year nine.
india calls it **anurādhā** and gives it to **mitra** — and that word is
worth stopping on. it began as *covenant*, the sworn contract, and only
in later sanskrit did it simply become the word for **friend**.
he is the god of the sworn alliance, the contract, the promise between
equals. so this is not a crown of rank. it is a crown you are given by
the people who chose to keep you.
your hunger is **to stay through the part that isn't fun** — and you are
unusual in that you are not actually bored by the maintenance. you like
the ninth year.
the cost is that loyalty this durable will occasionally be spent on
something that stopped deserving it, and you will be the last to notice,
because noticing feels like betrayal.

**STATION.17.root**
this sky is guarded by **the hare** and kept by **the sun**: the animal
that survives by being alert and quick rather than armed, under the
steadiest light in the sky. that pairing is your inner weather — warm,
constant, and quietly ready to bolt. you are more easily startled than
anyone who knows you would guess.
the room reads you as **dependable**, which is true and incomplete.
people treat your steadiness as a fact of nature rather than something
you are doing on purpose, daily, at some cost. a crown-rooted moon rarely
gets asked how they are, because the answer has been fine for so long
that the question stopped occurring to anyone.

**STATION.17.facing**
you enter and people relax. you are read as safe within minutes, and
told real things within days — which is a gift and a filter you didn't
ask for, since it also draws people who need somewhere soft to put
things down.

**STATION.17.answer**
across the wheel, **the crown** answers you — the three stars in a row,
and the god whose name is just *friend*. once a year the full moon rises
there and asks who has been keeping you, and whether you have thanked
them out loud.

## STATION.18 — The Heart · 心宿

*8°34′–21°26′ Scorpio · Antares · Arabic Qalb al-ʿAqrab, "the scorpion's
heart" · Jyeṣṭhā, the eldest · guardian: the fox · kept by the Moon ·
Azure Dragon*

**STATION.18.strike**
your sun landed on **antares** — the red star at the center of the
scorpion, and one of the few places where two sky-cultures that never met
reached for the **same word**. the arabs called it *qalb al-ʿaqrab*, the
scorpion's **heart**. china's name for this station is 心 — **heart**.
the greek name says something else worth knowing: *antares* means
**rival of ares** — the rival of mars. it is red enough, and bright
enough, that it competes with a planet.
india names this sky **jyeṣṭhā**, which means **the eldest**, and gives
it to indra, king of the gods. so the hunger here is layered and honest:
you want to be **the most important one to somebody** — the first called,
the one who gets told first — and you will take on any amount of
protecting to earn it.
the cost is the rivalry buried in the name. a heart that burns that hot
keeps score of its own significance, and the ugliest hours of this
station are the ones spent quietly measuring your place against someone
else's. it isn't jealousy exactly. it's the eldest's terror of being
replaceable.

**STATION.18.root**
this sky is guarded by **the fox** and kept by **the moon** — the
cleverest animal in the sky-list under the most changeable light. your
inner life is strategic *about feeling*: you know precisely how much to
show, to whom, and when, and you are rarely caught out. it is not
calculation. it's a survival habit that started early.
the room reads you as **intense and self-possessed** at the same time,
which is an unusual pair and makes people slightly wary in a way they
enjoy. a heart-rooted moon is loved at high temperature by a small number
of people and misread by everyone else, and mostly finds that trade
acceptable — but should notice how small the number has gotten.

**STATION.18.facing**
you enter at full color and people know how they feel about you almost
immediately. nobody meets you neutrally. that's the red star doing its
work — you can't arrive dimly.

**STATION.18.answer**
across the wheel, **the heart** answers you — the red star two skies both
called by that name. once a year the full moon lands there and asks who
you are still trying to matter most to, and whether they know.

## STATION.19 — The Root · 尾宿

*21°26′ Scorpio–4°17′ Sagittarius · Shaula and Lesath, the raised sting ·
Arabic al-Shawla, "the sting" · Mūla, the tied roots · guardian: the
tiger · kept by Mars · Azure Dragon*

**STATION.19.strike**
your sun landed at the **end of the scorpion's tail** — and the two
traditions named the same spot from opposite directions. the arabs saw
*al-shawla*, **the raised sting**: the business end, lifted. india saw
**mūla**, which means **the root** — its emblem a bundle of tied roots,
because the tail's end is also where the tail begins.
that is the reading, and it's unusually literal: **you go to the bottom
of things.** where other people take the account they were given, you dig
for the cause. the origin, the real reason, the first version of the
story before it got tidy.
the cost is what's at the bottom. digging to the root means handling the
sting, every time — the true reason is frequently the one nobody wanted
found, and you will be the person who found it. you have been thanked for
this far less often than you have been right.

**STATION.19.root**
this sky is guarded by **the tiger** and kept by **mars**. that is the
most straightforwardly fierce pairing on the road, and in an inner life
it doesn't read as aggression — it reads as **certainty**. when you know,
you know all the way down, and you do not experience your own conviction
as an opinion.
the room reads you as **formidable**, and adjusts before you've done
anything. the honest difficulty: certainty that deep is very hard to
update in public, so you will occasionally defend a position past the
moment you stopped believing it, purely because reversing in front of
people costs more than you can spend that day. a root-rooted moon does
its changing in private, and should tell at least one person when it's
happening.

**STATION.19.facing**
you enter and people sense the floor of you — that there is something
solid and slightly dangerous down there. it makes small talk difficult
and real conversation fast.

**STATION.19.answer**
across the wheel, **the root** answers you — the sting that is also the
beginning of the tail. once a year the full moon rises there and asks
what you've stopped digging into, and why you stopped.

## STATION.20 — The Flock · 箕宿

*4°17′–17°09′ Sagittarius · eight stars in two groups of four · Arabic
al-Naʿāim, "the ostriches" · Pūrva Aṣāḍhā, the waters · guardian: the
leopard · kept by Mercury · Azure Dragon*

**STATION.20.strike**
your sun landed on one of the loveliest pictures in the whole sky-map:
**eight stars at the bank of the milky way**, which the arabs read as
**ostriches** — four of them walking down to the river to drink, and four
walking back. the same birds, on both sides of the water, and **none of
them missing.**
china's name for this station is **the winnowing basket**, and india's
name for the sky, **pūrva aṣāḍhā**, belongs to the waters — with a
winnowing fan among its emblems. two traditions independently put a
basket for separating grain from chaff next to the river.
so the hunger is double and it fits together: **you keep the group
together, and you can tell what's worth keeping.** your instinct in any
gathering is a headcount, and your gift is that you can do it without
making it strange.
the cost is that the person counting heads never quite gets to be at the
river. you have been present at a great many good evenings and fully
inside surprisingly few of them.

**STATION.20.root**
this sky is guarded by **the leopard** — the one that hunts alone and
hauls what it catches up a tree to eat in peace — and it is
**mercury-kept**. an inner life that is far more solitary than your
social life suggests, and that needs its own branch to process anything
on.
the room reads you as **everyone's friend**, and that reading is not
wrong so much as it is missing a whole floor. people are startled to
learn how much of your life happens where they can't see it. a
flock-rooted moon holds the group together and goes home alone by
preference — and the ones who understand that about you without taking it
personally are the ones who last.

**STATION.20.facing**
you enter and immediately know who hasn't been introduced. people
experience you as the connective tissue of a room within one evening,
and start routing things through you shortly after.

**STATION.20.answer**
across the wheel, **the flock** answers you — the eight birds at the
river, none of them lost. once a year the full moon lands there and asks
who you've been counting, and who has been counting you.

## STATION.21 — The Empty District · 斗宿

*17°09′ Sagittarius–0°00′ Capricorn · **no stars** — π Sagittarii stands
at the edge as a marker · Arabic al-Balda, "the empty district" · Uttara
Aṣāḍhā, the all-gods · guardian: the xièzhì · kept by Jupiter · **first
station of the Black Tortoise***

**STATION.21.strike**
your sun landed in the one station on the road with **almost nothing in
it**. the old arab astronomers mapped twenty-eight stations across the
sky, and this one — *al-balda*, **the empty district** — is the bare
stretch between the archer's head and the goat's horns. al-bīrūnī
described it by comparing it to the blank space between two eyebrows that
don't meet. one modest star sits in the middle of all that room, and it
still carries the station's name: albaldah.
and here is the fact that makes this station the strangest and best on
the whole wheel: **the center of our galaxy lies in this direction.** the
hundreds of billions of stars we orbit are behind that patch — invisible,
because the dust and gas between here and there is too thick to see
through. the emptiest sky on the map is the way to the middle of
everything.
that is your hunger, and it is not what people assume when they hear
"empty": **you want the thing that stays won.** india's name here is
uttara aṣāḍhā, the sky of the all-gods, the undefeated victory. the pause
is not the absence of the work. it's where the work is.
the cost is that you will be read as unproductive during the exact
periods when you are doing the most, and you will not always have the
vocabulary to defend it — least of all to yourself, at 2am.

**STATION.21.root**
this sky opens the **black tortoise** — the north, winter, the guardian
whose name literally reads *dark warrior* and who is drawn as a tortoise
with a snake wound around it. and its own animal is the **xièzhì**, the
horned justice-beast of chinese legend that was said to gore the guilty
party in a dispute. it is **jupiter-kept**.
so: an inner life of enormous, slow, winter judgment. you know who was
wrong. you knew early, you didn't say, and the knowledge has weight and
patience behind it.
the room reads you as **calm**, and doesn't realize a verdict is being
formed. people are consistently surprised by how much you had already
decided about a situation, and how long ago. an empty-rooted moon should
watch the gap between the verdict and the saying — the horn is only
useful if anyone finds out it's there.

**STATION.21.facing**
you enter and the room gets slightly quieter, in a way nobody can
account for. you don't fill space; you make some. it is the rarest first
impression on the road and people remember it without being able to
describe it.

**STATION.21.answer**
across the wheel, **the empty district** answers you — the starless sky
that faces the center of the galaxy. once a year the full moon rises on
that blank stretch and asks what you'd do with a season nobody was
measuring.

## Production notes

**~3,400 words; stations 15–21 complete. Twenty-one of twenty-eight
done.** Batch 5 finishes the road at stations 22–28.

**The scorpion run (16–19) is the strongest sequence in the corpus.**
Three of the four are `STRONG` matches, and two of them are cases where
sky-cultures that never met chose the *same word*: Antares is *qalb* in
Arabic and 心 in Chinese — heart, twice — and the claws/scales pair is
attested as a genuine historical hand-off (Babylon read both, Greece kept
the claws, Rome restored the balance). Those aren't embellishments; they
are the reason this system is worth building.

**Station 21 is the crown jewel of the whole road.** The Arabs left a
station deliberately starless, and it happens to point at the galactic
center, invisible behind dust. That is `physics-paradox.md`'s "the dark
is not empty" landing on a real station without being forced — and it
makes the emptiest card the one people will want most. Design should know
this: **The Empty District should be the hardest card in the game to
stop looking at.** (Earlier note confirmed: this material belongs to
station 21, not 19, where it was first mis-filed.)

**Two costs deliberately kept distinct:** station 10 (The Throne) and
station 18 (The Heart) both risk the "nobody takes care of me" reading.
Throne's cost is *legitimacy* — the inherited chair, the assumption that
you're fine. Heart's cost is *rivalry* — the eldest's fear of being
replaceable, which is a different wound with a different shape. Later
batches should keep watching for this; the corpus is now large enough
that repetition is the main quality risk.
