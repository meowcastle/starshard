# NAMING.md — naming the 784

**August 14, 2026.** Justin: *"there are a total of 784 star shards /
archetypes? the naming of those is going to be very important. what are
some best approaches?"*

Researched against folk-taxonomy linguistics, the I Ching, the Chinese
sexagenary cycle, nakshatra padas, tarot, Pokémon, and the procedural-
generation literature. **The evidence converges hard, and it says
something more specific than "yes" or "no."**

---

## 1. The finding

> **Every durable compositional identity system uses two layers: a
> compositional address that is canonical and complete, plus a proper-name
> layer that is partial, secondary, and never load-bearing.**

Systems that tried to make the name layer complete and primary all lost to
the address:

- **Tarot's 56 minor arcana.** The Golden Dawn and Crowley assigned proper
  names to every one — Three of Cups = *Abundance*, Five of Swords =
  *Defeat*. **130 years later everyone still says "Three of Cups."** The
  titles survive as an interpretive aid, which is a good thing to be, and
  not the identifier.
- **The sexagenary cycle.** When a tradition with two millennia to work on
  it added evocative names to a 60-cell grid, the *nayin* system named
  **30 things, not 60** — one name per two cells — and it stayed a
  specialist layer.
- **MBTI.** "INTJ" is universal; "The Architect" is one vendor's
  marketing. **Enneagram wings are the diagnostic case: "4w5" is
  completely stable while the nickname varies by vendor** — Artistic
  Philosopher, Bohemian Individualist, The Bohemian. *An unstable nickname
  layer is the signature of naming that didn't take* — and that is
  happening at **N = 18**, not 784.

### And the number that settles it

Berlin, Breedlove & Raven (*American Anthropologist*, 1973) established
that folk taxonomies spend unanalyzable names at the **generic** rank and
switch to **modifier + head binomials** below it. Cross-cultural surveys
put the generic budget at **400–500 names per language, for all plants and
animals combined.** Tzeltal: 471.

> **784 arbitrary proper names would be about 1.6× the entire named
> biological vocabulary of an entire culture — for one product feature.**

**Our 28 mansions are the generics. The 784 are the specifics. Berlin says
specifics get binomials, and every system above agrees.**

### The astrology counter-evidence, which cuts in our favour

Western astrology has had **144 sun × moon combinations** for a century,
with overwhelming commercial incentive and a large identity-hungry
audience, and **has never named them.** Every book organised around all
144 titles its chapters compositionally.

**But the reason does not apply to us, and this matters.** Astrology's
system is **open** — sun and moon are two of ten-plus placements, so
naming 144 would privilege an arbitrary 2-slice of an unbounded space.
Also, "Sun in Gemini" is a labelled-slot predicate, a *description* that
extends by adding slots; compounds can lexicalise into names, slot
predicates structurally cannot.

**Our 784 is closed.** The shard is *defined* as sun mansion × moon
mansion. **Closure is what licenses naming** — it is why the I Ching's 64
could be named and astrology's 144 could not. That is a real structural
advantage and we should use it.

## 2. The architecture

### Layer 1 — the address. Canonical, complete, permanent.

```
6·10
```

Sun mansion · moon mansion. **Order fixed forever, everywhere.** Every
durable system enforces order invariance — stem-then-branch, nakshatra-
then-pada, number-then-suit, lower-then-upper trigram.

Ship it alongside the name always, the way Pokémon carries **#025
Pikachu**. The dual identity is why 1,025 names stay navigable.

### Layer 2 — the compositional name. Derivable, complete.

```
The Storm of the Throne          [6·10]
```

**Sun epithet as head; moon epithet in the of-phrase.**

Four evidence-backed reasons for this exact construction:

1. **Of-genitive, because our components are inanimate abstract nouns.**
   Rosenbach (*Language* 81:3, 2005): animate possessors take the
   *s*-genitive, inanimate strongly prefer *of*. *"The Throne's Storm"*
   personifies.
2. **Head-initial with a partitive of-phrase is tarot's construction** —
   *Three of Cups* — and the first position carries the identity. The sun
   is what you are; it goes first.
3. **Never blend.** Brand-name memory research finds blending actively
   *hurts* recall (β ≈ −.2). *Stormthrone*, *Throne-Storm* and
   *Stormthro* are all contraindicated. Keep both morphemes intact.
4. **The diagonal inherits.** The I Ching's eight doubled hexagrams take
   their trigram's own name — Heaven over Heaven is simply 乾. So **6·6 is
   "The Storm,"** never *the Storm of the Storm*. That is our 28 Seedborn
   cells, 3.5% of people, and they get the unmarked atomic name.

**Tested against the real 28 epithets:** median 23 characters, only 2 of
784 over 34. Samples — *The Claws of the Storm* · *The Root of the Drum* ·
*The Thread of the Heart* · *The Hideaway of the Throne* · *The Bearer of
the Root*.

**One fix required before generation.** Twenty-six of our epithets are
"The + single noun." Two are not — **The Gathered Stars** (3) and **The
Empty District** (21) — and they produce every overlong name in the set
(*The Gathered Stars of the Empty District*, 40 chars). Give those two a
**short form used only inside the of-phrase**: *…of the Stars*, *…of the
District*. Fifty-five names each are affected.

### Layer 3 — proper names. Partial, secondary, and that is correct.

Per nayin, the Golden Dawn titles, amsa names and MBTI nicknames: **this
layer is always secondary. Do not fight it.**

- **Start with the 28 diagonals**, which already have names by inheritance.
- **Grow only where real written content exists.** The I Ching's names are
  *excerpts from its own text*, not functions of its trigrams — the name
  came out of the writing. Write about a cell, then let a phrase from that
  writing become its title.
- **Never let this layer become the identifier.** If it ever appears
  without the address next to it, it will start competing with it, and the
  address wins anyway — we would just have spent the goodwill.

## 2b. Whose name is the epithet? (Justin, Aug 14 — a real problem)

> *"Two civilizations put the lion's brow and the king's seat. How do we
> render it as the Storm of the Throne? That is what is the Star Shard?"*

**Yes — that is the name. But he has caught a genuine incoherence, and it
sits at the exact point we claim to be strongest.**

Mansion 10 is flagged **DIVERGENT** in our own data. Arabia says *al-Jabha*,
the Lion's forehead. India says *Maghā*, and its symbol is **the royal
throne**. China says *星 Xīng*, determinant **Alphard — the Serpent's heart,
in Hydra**.

**Our epithet for it is "The Throne."** That is India's symbol, in India's
word. So at the naming layer we quietly picked a side — while at the
content layer we claim to render the disagreement rather than resolve it.
**Both halves of Justin's own archetype, The Storm (6) and The Throne (10),
come from the Indian reading.**

### The audit

Testing whether each epithet's own word appears in a tradition's entry:

| tradition | epithets echoing its wording |
|---|---|
| **Arabia** | **11** |
| India | 8 |
| Japan | 4 |
| **China** | **2** |
| ours alone | 7 — Gate · Bearer · Flock · Listener · Hideaway · Chamber · Thread |

**The skew is not toward India. It is away from China — and China is the
tradition that dissents most often.** Our naming layer is systematically
quietest about the voice that most often disagrees, which means **the
epithets are suppressing exactly the divergence the product exists to
show.**

The counter-example proves the point: **mansion 18, "The Heart," echoes
Arabia, China *and* Japan at once.** That is what an epithet looks like
when the traditions actually agree — and its match flag is STRONG.

### The resolution — three layers, never confused

1. **The place.** 12°51′25″ of ecliptic. A fact. No opinion, no culture.
2. **The testimony.** What four civilisations said about that place. They
   disagree, and **the disagreement is content, not noise.**
3. **The epithet.** Star Shard's English handle for the place — **a fifth
   voice, ours, and it must be labelled as ours.** Not a translation.

**Right now the epithet looks like a translation and is actually an
editorial choice.** That is the whole defect. Naming the layer honestly
costs one sentence and fixes it.

> **The Star Shard is not what the traditions call you. It is the place
> you landed — and the traditions are the argument about what that place
> is.**

### Two rules that follow

**a. Name from the function, not the image, where the traditions diverge.**
Look at what mansion 10's three readings actually share. Arabia: the lion's
**forehead**. India: the **throne**. China: the serpent's **heart**. Three
different animals — and all three name **a seat of authority in a body.**
*They disagree about the creature and agree about the function.* An epithet
built on the shared function takes no side. One built on a single
tradition's noun does.

**b. A divergent mansion's name must point at the argument, not settle it.**
When the traditions fight over the degrees a traveler was born on, that is
one of the best things we will ever be able to tell them:

> *you were born on a stretch of sky two civilisations could not agree
> about. Arabia saw a lion's brow. India saw a throne. China looked at the
> same degrees and saw a serpent's heart.*

**That is not a flaw in the name. It is the content the name points at.**

### The decision for Justin

**Keep "The Throne."** It carries the 2-of-3 majority *and* the shared
function, which is the defensible basis. But two things have to happen:

1. **Say once, in the arrival, that the epithet is ours** — and that the
   four traditions underneath it are testimony, not translation.
2. **Re-audit the 28 against rule (a)**, prioritising the mansions where
   China dissents. Two of twenty-eight is not a naming system that has
   listened to four traditions.

**This is also the strongest argument yet for closing the 16 missing match
flags.** We cannot apply rule (b) — point at the argument — on mansions
where we have not yet recorded whether there *is* one. **Mansion 10 is
visibly divergent in its own text and carries no flag.**

## 3. What kills generated names, and what to do

**The oatmeal problem** (Kate Compton, the most-cited practitioner text on
generators): *"I can easily generate 10,000 bowls of plain oatmeal… and
mathematically they will all be completely unique. But the user will
likely just see a lot of oatmeal."* Perceptual *differentiation* is easy;
perceptual *uniqueness* is the real metric.

**Mitigations, all documented:**

- **Chains of meaning.** Nothing should be related only to itself. Our
  name is already entangled with the mansions, the epithets, the four
  traditions and the rarity number — the name is the *last* thing
  generated, not the first. That is what makes it read as earned.
- **Let salience be unequal by design.** Compton: let most artifacts be
  "drab background noise, highlighting the few characterful artifacts."
  You cannot manufacture uniform salience across 784 cells and should not
  try. The 1966 *hinoeuma* year moved Japanese births by ~414,000 — **one
  cell in sixty carried all the weight.** Cells earn salience; it cannot
  be allocated in advance.
- **Curate by hand. We can afford it.** 784 items at a minute each is
  about **thirteen hours.** *Elite* (1984) had to discard an entire galaxy
  of 256 planets over a single obscene name because per-item review was
  impossible on 8-bit hardware. That constraint is not ours.
- **The failure clusters are enumerable in advance** — inspect the 28×28
  grid before generating: (a) the diagonal and near-diagonal band, (b)
  cells where one epithet is already an ordinary modifier of the other,
  (c) accidental idioms and unfortunate collocations.
- **Build an override table and tolerate exceptions.** The I Ching's names
  come from its line statements *except* in hexagrams 2, 9, 26, 61 and 63,
  where "an unrelated character of unclear purpose appears." **A
  state-canonised text runs ~8% unexplained exceptions and is fine.**
- **Do not build a substring blocklist.** The Scunthorpe problem —
  Penistone, Clitheroe, Cockburn, a paleontology conference banning
  "bone." At 784 with hand review, a blocklist adds only false positives.

**Honest gap: there is no published rate for what fraction of N×M
combinations come out badly.** I looked. Any number quoted on this is folk
wisdom. Ours will be measured by reading the list.

---

## 4. What to do

1. **Ship the address today.** `6·10` costs nothing and is permanent.
2. **Give epithets 3 and 21 short forms**, then generate all 784.
3. **Read all 784 once** and build the override table. Thirteen hours.
4. **Name the 28 diagonals** by inheritance — done automatically.
5. **Leave layer 3 empty for now.** Add a proper name only when a cell has
   real written content to take an excerpt from, and never present it
   without the address.

**The thing to resist:** commissioning 784 proper names up front. It
exceeds the naming budget of a language, every comparable system that
tried it lost to the address, and the names would be oatmeal because they
would be generated from a formula rather than drawn from writing that
already exists.
