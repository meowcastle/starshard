## 2b the razor: the best seat row on any sheet, and it is not close

**Fresh seat −3.1, deep seat −4.5, and everything else quiet.** That is the first law measured to move
seat by a single digit in either direction, and the reason is exactly the one Design gave: it makes
the middle *harder to profit from* without handing anyone a station.

The seven-law table I sent this morning had a hole between +5 and +11 and no entries inside it. **The
razor lands in the hole from the other side** — −3.1 and −4.5 — which means the finding needs one
amendment, and Design found it by writing a sentence rather than by arguing with the table.

**The amendment: it is not that seat has no gentle instrument. It is that seat has no gentle
*ownership* instrument.** Every law in that table moved who ends up holding the middle. The razor
does not touch ownership at all — it changes what a lodge there is *worth as a stroke*, and a
subtractive change to a stroke is a fraction of a station rather than a whole one. So the corrected
rule is:

> **Reassign the middle and the seat moves by a quarter-board. Price what the middle can do and the
> seat moves by points.**

Two of the three passing rows today are subtractive strike laws — the razor and the crowd — and both
are single-digit on seat. The price of the mark was the third. That is now a class with three
members and a shape.

**One flag, and it is Design's own.** Suzaku −6.7. The bird holds five of nine stations on this
night, and the razor makes a two-sided lodge conditional, which costs most where the road is most
contested. It is inside the bound and the spread still narrows, but it is the same lean the crowd
shows harder, and it is worth watching if the razor and a Suzaku house ever share a road.

**The boards-per-match and lodge-order rows Design asked for: no bare-station shape appears.** The
spread narrows rather than blowing out, and seat falls rather than inverting, which is the opposite
signature to the m21 bare station in every respect. The middle is still lodged; it is just lodged
later and more deliberately, which is what the sentence promises.

## 2a the crowd: passes at both dials, and the honest answer is that −1 does nothing

**Both dials pass every bound. Neither moves the tiger.** Byakko −0.2 at −2 and −0.1 at −1, on a
board where byakko is top.

**At −1 it is flat by the sheet's own standard** — seat, spread and skill all inside 1.2 points, every
quadrant inside 4.7. Design said in advance that a flat result is a pass by the bounds and a law that
does nothing, and would say so rather than ship a sentence for its own sake. **That is what happened,
and I am saying it back: −1 is a law that does nothing.**

**At −2 it does one thing and the thing is not what the sheet wanted: suzaku −12.7, spread +4.0.**
Company at the middle is a consequence of holding the neighbours, and the bird holds five of nine
stations here, so the bird builds company at the crossing far more than anyone else. **The law does
not charge the winning quarter; it charges the quarter with the most road.** That is the crow's
mechanism inverted — the crow found the winner by looking at worth, the crowd finds the crowded by
looking at position, and on this night those are different quarters.

So the fiction is right and the physics is aimed at the wrong thing on *this* road. Company is a
good condition; it is just a condition that reads geography rather than strength.

## 2c the cock's hour: the boundary, drawn

**Design was right that it would overshoot, and right about which shape it would take.** At `hourN` 5
the fresh seat inverts to −20.2 and the deep seat collapses 21.7. At `hourN` 4 it still fails fresh
seat at −11.2.

But the interesting part is the dial's behaviour. **Tempo is not a class with no small settings; it is
a class whose settings are not smooth.** Going from five cards to four halves the seat effect
(−24.0 → −11.2) — which is a real dial with a real gradient, unlike ownership, where the tie clause
alone was worth 14 points. **A third setting at `hourN` 3 would very likely land inside the bound.**

I am not running it, and I want to be explicit about why rather than just declining. A law that opens
the middle on the fourth card is a law nobody notices — the middle rarely gets lodged in the first
three placements anyway, so the sentence would be true, safe, and invisible. It would pass the bounds
by becoming the thing 2a-at-−1 already is.

**So the boundary is: tempo has a gradient, and the part of the gradient that passes is the part
where nothing happens.** That is a more useful finding than "tempo overshoots", and it is only
visible because the sheet asked for two dials. The spread rows are worth keeping too — the hour is
the only candidate today that narrowed the board meaningfully (−8.0 at `hourN` 5), which says the
closed middle *is* doing something structural, just at a price nobody would pay.

## Recommendation

**Ship the razor.** `lawAt {3:{station:5, kind:"razor"}}`, index 4 on m3's road, which is **m7, the
return, suzaku ground.** Best seat behaviour measured, the spread narrows, the class is subtractive
and now three-for-three, and it is the sisters' own gesture: one stroke, the whole width of the road.

**Do not ship the crowd at either dial.** At −1 it is inert; at −2 it charges the bird for holding the
most road. Design named both outcomes in advance and both happened. The sentence is good and should
be kept for a house whose night is not five-ninths one quarter — it is the price's shape with a
condition that will bite properly on a board where company and strength coincide.

**2c is closed.** The boundary is drawn, the dial has a gradient, and the passing end of it is empty.

**And the amendment stands as the day's finding:** seat has no gentle *ownership* instrument, but
subtractive strike laws move it by points. That is three passes now — the price, the crowd, the razor
— and it is the first thing we have that reaches seat without a match-structure change.
