## The measurement Design asked for, and it settles the 1b argument

**Who lodges the middle first, plain boards, no law, real placements only:**

| night | fresh | deep |
|---|---|---|
| m3 the gathered stars | leader **56.6%** | leader **66.0%** |
| m5 the blaze | leader 59.4% | leader 62.4% |
| m4 the follower | leader 60.5% | leader 65.2% |

**It is never 50/50 and it gets worse as the collection deepens.** The soft ground's premise —
*"whoever is standing at the middle is the leader exactly as often as it is not"* — is wrong by six to
sixteen points, and the deep column is where the seat problem lives anyway.

That is the whole mechanism. A law that makes the middle worse to hold is a tax the leader pays two
times in three. Design's arithmetic on 1b was right in structure and wrong in scale: it did not land
at +16, it landed at **−20.8 fresh**. The estimate assumed the m5 delta would transfer; the delta is
not a constant, it is a fraction of how often the leader is standing there, and m3's deep board has
the leader on the middle two-thirds of the time.

**So 1b confirms the shell's lesson twice over.** A number from m5 is not a number from m3 — and the
sheet said exactly that, and ran it anyway, which was the right call. It is now measured rather than
feared.

## 1a — the smaller party: the strongest seat instrument we have, and far too strong

The defender row moves fresh seat **32.6 points**. Nothing else measured comes close in either
direction; the soft ground's −14.6 on m5 was the record this morning.

**It works exactly as designed and that is the problem.** The law hands a station to whoever is
behind. Whoever is behind is usually the follower. So it is not a correction applied to a seat
advantage; it is a rebate paid every board to whoever is losing, and on a board with 4.2 stations a
side, one guaranteed station is a quarter of the board.

Three things confirm it is over-correction rather than a broken sentence:

- **Both tie rules fail, and `nobody` fails less.** Defender −28.8, nobody −14.5. The gap between
  them is the tie clause alone, worth 14 points of seat — a tiebreak should not be a fifth of a law.
- **Every quadrant falls together** (defender: −21.4, −18.4, −17.9, −4.2) and **the spread does not
  move at all** — +0.3 and 0.0. It is quadrant-blind exactly as promised, and being quadrant-blind
  means it does nothing for the thing the byakko brief is for.
- **The skill gap falls on both rows** (−8.2 and −8.8 deep), which is the risk Design named. A rule
  that helps whoever is behind helps the careless player, because the careless player is behind more
  often. **Design ranked skill as the thing to watch hardest, and it was right.**

Genbu barely moves (−4.2, −2.1) while everyone else drops fifteen to twenty-two, which is worth one
line: the tortoise's grant is about the count's ownership, so a law that reassigns a station's owner
at the count is the one quarter already fluent in it.

## What this says about the seat brief

I recommended m3 two days ago because it isolates seat. **The isolation worked and the answer is not
the one I expected: seat is not a station-scale quantity.**

Every law that has moved seat more than four points has moved it more than eleven, in one step, with
no dial in between:

| | seat move |
|---|---|
| the planted station, as written | +20.2 |
| the gate | +21.0 |
| the mark, keep = lost | +23.5 |
| the soft ground, m5 | −14.6 |
| **the soft ground, m3** | **−20.8** |
| **the smaller party, nobody** | **−14.5** |
| **the smaller party, defender** | **−32.6** |

**Seven laws, four classes, both signs, and not one of them landed between +5 and +11.** The middle
station is roughly a quarter of a nine-station board's decided value; any rule that changes who gets
it changes the seat by a quarter-board-sized amount. There is no gentle version, because the
instrument has no small setting.

**The instruments that do move seat gently are structural, and we already shipped both of them.**
Loser-leads took the match seat from 29 to 13. The level board going to the non-leader took it from
21.6 to 5.4. Both work by acting *across* boards, where a fractional correction is possible, rather
than by handing someone a station.

**So my recommendation for the brief changes.** m3's seat is a match-structure problem, not a law
problem, and the three sentences on this sheet are the evidence. The two questions worth asking next
are structural:

1. **Is a best-of-five worth it for the mansion fight specifically?** Measured at 9.3 against
   best-of-three's 12.5, and it costs 4.2 boards against 2.6. That is a real trade and it is the only
   dial we have that moves seat by single digits.
2. **How does the server assign board one's leader in PvP?** Still unanswered, still the largest
   unmeasured lever in the game, and on a +27.5 night it is worth more than any law on any sheet.

**And m3 should get a law about the gathered stars rather than a law about the seat.** Its spread is
19.0, one of the calmest boards in the game — there is nothing to narrow and no reason to spend the
house's identity fixing a number that laws cannot reach. The sisters' verb is shelter; the price of
the mark just showed that shelter conditioned on the strike is a working shape. That is a sentence
about this house.

## 1c

Design's gate was *"run only if 1a and 1b both miss the bound."* They did, so by the sheet's own rule
1c is now live as a diagnostic. **I would not run it.** It is the deny class in its purest form —
one side cannot be taken at one station — and the three failures above already answer the question it
was built to ask. The diagnostic value was *how much seat is shelter*; the answer is that shelter at
the middle is worth twenty to thirty points of seat whichever way it points, and we have that number
three times over now without spending a fourth run on it.
